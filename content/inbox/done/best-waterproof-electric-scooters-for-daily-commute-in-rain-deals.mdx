## What waterproof really means: IP ratings explained

"Waterproof" on a scooter page is a marketing word. What counts is the IP code, which has two digits: the first for dust and solids, the second for water. An "X" means that part was not rated. For scooters, the second digit is the one that matters, and Wikipedia's [IP Code article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) summarizes the tests:

| Water rating | Test |
|---|---|
| IPX4 | Splashing from any direction, for about 10 minutes |
| IPX5 | Water jets from a 6.3 mm nozzle at 12.5 L/min, at least 3 minutes |
| IPX6 | Powerful jets from a 12.5 mm nozzle at 100 L/min, at least 3 minutes |
| IPX7 | Immersion, lowest point 1 m below the surface |

Two points trip people up. First, the ratings do not stack above IPX6: IPX7 (immersion) does not imply IPX5 or IPX6 (jets), and a device that passes both is listed as IPX5/IPX7. So an IP67 scooter is not automatically "better in the rain" than an IPX6 one in every test, though it does mean the first digit (dust) is also rated. Second, a rating describes a lab test on the sealed device, not a commute through puddles with a worn deck seal. Electric Scooter Guide says the same in its rain roundup: no scooter is fully waterproof, and it advises against riding through puddles even on lightly rated models.

Electric Scooter Guide's IP rating guide (published March 15, 2024, modified June 11, 2026) suggests IPX4 or higher for light rain and says to assume your warranty won't cover water damage. It doesn't describe any water testing of its own. That matches my reading: the rating is a starting point, and wet-weather testing by reviewers is thin. For a deeper explanation, see my guide on [riding an electric scooter in the rain](/can-you-ride-use-or-drive-an-electric-scooter-in-the-rain/).

## How I chose these waterproof scooters and what I trust

1. **A stated IP rating of IPX5 or better.** Below that, I'd call it rain-tolerant, not rain-ready.
2. **A reviewer's measured performance.** ERideHero tests range, speed and braking with a 175 lb rider. It doesn't run water tests, so its IP rating is the maker's.
3. **Any rain use by a tester.** EScooterNerds reports riding several of these in light, moderate or heavy rain with no issues. That is anecdotal, not a lab test, and it has no method, so I label it as such.
4. **Tires and brakes.** Tubeless, self-healing tires reduce flats on wet roads, and braking distances tell you how much margin you have.
5. **Weight and price.** A scooter you can't carry or afford isn't a commuter.

What I could not find is an independent immersion or hose test of these scooters. So the IP ratings here are claims, backed in a few cases by tester anecdotes. Where sources conflict on a rating, I show both. The Navee ST3 Pro is rated IPX5 by ERideHero and IP66 by Electric Scooter Guide, and the EMOVE Cruiser is IPX6 by ERideHero and IP67 by ESG. Because the second digits differ (and per the non-stacking rule above, IPX6 and IPX7 are separate tests), check the maker's manual for the model you're buying.

## After a wet ride: drying and checking

A wet commute doesn't end when you park. Electric Scooter Guide's advice, which I agree with, is to towel-dry the scooter and let it dry in a warm, dry place overnight. Before the next wet ride, it suggests inspecting cable routing, deck openings and how components fit, and notes that silicone caulking can help on some models. I wouldn't open the deck to do that unless your manual allows it, because opening it can break the seal the rating depends on.

A short routine I'd follow, based on that advice:

1. Wipe the deck, stem and tires, and dry the charge port area.
2. Keep the scooter indoors or somewhere dry and warm overnight if you can. My guide on [leaving a scooter outside in the rain](/can-you-leave-an-electric-scooter-outside-in-the-rain/) covers what to do if you can't.
3. Charge it only when it is dry.
4. Look at the tires and brakes, because grit from wet roads wears both.
5. Watch for signs of water damage: odd noises, flickering display, error codes, or a battery that drains fast.

If the scooter is ever dunked or soaked, stop riding it and read the manual. That is the point where a rating stops protecting you.

## Electric scooter that can be used in rain: which rating for which weather

If you want an electric scooter that can be used in rain, this is how I'd match the rating to the weather. It is my own reading of the IP tests and the advice in Electric Scooter Guide's IP rating guide, not a standard.

| Rating | What I'd do |
|---|---|
| IPX4 to IP54 | Light rain and damp roads only. Avoid puddles. |
| IPX5 to IP55 | Steady rain on the road is reasonable. Avoid standing water. |
| IPX6 to IP66 | Heavy rain and spray from other vehicles are covered by the test. Still avoid puddles and floods. |
| IP67 | Brief immersion is covered by the test. Don't test it. |

ESG's advice is to avoid riding through puddles even on a lightly rated scooter, and I'd extend that to every scooter. The rating covers a sealed, undamaged unit, so check for gaps at the deck, cables and charge port before wet rides. Opening the deck or modifying the scooter can void the rating. I haven't verified the rating of every model here, so use your manual.

## Electric scooter you can ride in the rain: wet-road tips

An electric scooter you can ride in the rain is only half the answer, because the road is the other half. Electric Scooter Guide's rain roundup (updated August 13, 2026) says riders usually have about 90 percent as much traction on wet pavement as on dry, a figure it says it measured but without a method. It advises riding about 10 percent slower, avoiding manhole covers and wet leaves, and practicing emergency stops in a parking lot before a wet commute. It also lists a 10.8 ft wet stopping distance for the Segway Max G2, though it doesn't describe how that was measured.

For the scooters here, the dry braking distances at ERideHero run from 10.2 ft (Cruiser S) to 12.3 ft (Apollo Go). Wet distances will be longer, but I have no tested wet figure for these, so give yourself extra room. A full-face helmet and gloves matter in cold rain, and my [guide to riding in the rain](/can-you-ride-use-or-drive-an-electric-scooter-in-the-rain/) covers gear and technique in more detail.

## Range, charging and weight for wet-weather commuting

Rain and cold take range, and charging in a hurry is a bad idea after a wet ride. Using ERideHero's regular-riding range, here is the watt-hours per mile (my own math, battery divided by range) for the scooters with data: Max G3 about 24.0 (597 Wh over 24.9 mi), Cruiser S about 33.4 (1,560 Wh over 46.7 mi), Apollo Go about 27.3 (540 Wh over 19.8 mi) and ST3 Pro about 24.0. The Cruiser S has the most range because its battery is far larger, not because it is more efficient.

Charging is slow on several: the Cruiser S and ST3 Pro take 10 hours, the Apollo Go 7.5, and the Max G3 3.5. For wet weather, let the scooter dry before you charge it. Electric Scooter Guide says to towel-dry it and let it dry in a warm, dry place overnight. ## Safety and certification

Water and large batteries are a combination to respect. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death), and asked manufacturers to certify to UL standards, including UL 2272. I couldn't verify certification for these models, so check the box or the maker's page. If a scooter has been submerged or heavily soaked, don't charge it until it is dry, and stop using it if the battery swells or smells.

Wear a helmet, use lights, and slow down on wet roads. I haven't summarized state or city rules on where scooters may ride, because they vary and I couldn't find reliable official summaries, so check your local rules.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best waterproof electric scooters for daily commute 2027 deals?
The Segway Max G3 for most people, the EMOVE Cruiser S for range, the Apollo Go under $1,000, and the Navee ST3 Pro for value. Prices in my sources conflict, so check the current price and the exact model.

### Is there an electric scooter that can be used in rain?
Yes, if it has an IP rating of IPX5 or higher. The Max G3 (IPX6), Cruiser S (IPX6 or IP67), Apollo Go (IP66) and Burn-E 2 Max (IP67) are the ones here.

### Is there an electric scooter you can ride in the rain every day?
Some can handle regular wet commutes, but no scooter is fully waterproof, and the warranty may not cover water damage. Dry it after each ride.

### Is IP67 better than IPX6?
They test different things. IPX6 is powerful water jets, IPX7 is immersion, and the ratings don't stack. IP67 also rates dust protection.

### Can I ride through puddles?
Electric Scooter Guide advises against it, even on lightly rated scooters, and I'd do the same.

### Which scooter has the highest IP rating here?
The Nami Burn-E 2 Max at IP67 per EScooterNerds, and the EMOVE Cruiser at IP67 per ESG, though ERideHero lists the Cruiser S at IPX6.

### Does rain damage the battery?
Water getting into the battery or electronics can. A rated scooter resists splashes and jets, but a damaged seal or standing water can still get in.

### How much slower should I ride in the rain?
Electric Scooter Guide suggests about 10 percent slower and says traction on wet pavement is about 90 percent of dry.

### Do waterproof scooters cost more?
Not always. The Apollo Go is IP66 from $799 per ERideHero, while the Burn-E 2 Max is listed at $3,899.

### Does the warranty cover water damage?
Often not. Electric Scooter Guide says to assume it doesn't, so read your warranty.

### Can I wash my scooter with a hose?
I wouldn't. IPX5 and IPX6 tests use jets on a sealed unit, but wear and openings change that. Wipe it down instead.

### What is the lightest waterproof option here?
The GoTrax G3 at 36 lb per EScooterNerds, then the Apollo Go at 46 lb.
