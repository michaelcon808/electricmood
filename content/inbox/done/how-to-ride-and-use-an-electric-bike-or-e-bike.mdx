# How to Ride an E-Bike: A Beginner's Guide

How to ride an e-bike comes down to this: check the bike, turn it on, start on the lowest assist level in a quiet place, push off and pedal, and brake gently with both levers. Levy, EVELO and Urtopia all say the same thing in different words. An e-bike rides like a regular bike with a motor that helps while you pedal, and the surprise for new riders is the push at the start and the extra weight when stopping.

I haven't ridden or tested the bikes in these guides. The steps come from Levy (a scooter and e-bike company), EVELO and Urtopia (e-bike sellers), eurorad (a bike-leasing company), an undated-author article on the Global Cycling Network site (GCN) and the Naval Postgraduate School safety office. The makers' advice applies to their own bikes, so I say when something is brand-specific. Your own manual wins.

## Before you ride

Checks from the guides, in rough order:

- **Charge and battery.** Levy says to make sure the bike is fully charged and properly adjusted to your size. Urtopia says to confirm the battery is locked in and charged.
- **Tires.** Check pressure with a gauge. EVELO says the sidewall gives the maximum pressure and you don't have to inflate to it.
- **Brakes.** Test that the brakes engage before the lever reaches the handlebar (Naval Postgraduate School, NPS). If they feel soft or uneven, see my guide to [adjusting e-bike brakes](/how-to-tighten-and-adjust-electric-bike-and-ebike-brakes/) or visit a shop.
- **Wheels and bars.** NPS says to check the wheels and axles are secure, the handlebar doesn't move independently of the front tire, and to listen for rattles after lifting and dropping the bike. Don't ride until an unresolved issue is fixed.
- **Saddle height.** EVELO gives a common guideline of just the ball of your foot touching the ground when stopped, and says lower is fine if you want flat-footed contact. Its handlebar tip is that your wrists stay straight and you can reach the brake levers.
- **Helmet.** EVELO says it should be snug with two fingers fitting under the chin strap. NPS calls a helmet "the best safety step you can take," and says many states require them depending on age and bike type.
- **Lights and visibility.** Wear bright or reflective clothing in low light (Levy, Urtopia).

The Naval Postgraduate School's [e-bike safety page](https://nps.edu/web/safety/ebike) has the full list and is undated.

## How to use an electric bike: the controls

How to use an electric bike starts with learning four controls, and EVELO's guide is the most detailed (its button layout is for EVELO bikes, so yours may differ):

- **Power.** Press the handlebar button. On EVELO bikes there are no keys. My [how to turn on an e-bike guide](/how-to-turn-on-and-start-an-electric-bike-or-ebike/) covers starting.
- **Pedal assist (PAS) buttons.** On EVELO bikes, plus and minus on the left side. Level 0 is no motor, levels 1 to 3 light to moderate help, and levels 4 to 5 maximum help, best saved for steep hills. Raising the level makes the bike faster; it is not the same as shifting gears.
- **Throttle, if you have one.** EVELO says it is on the left, powers the bike without pedaling, and the bike "will take off on its own, so make sure not to press this until you are ready." Most EVELO bikes need PAS level 1 or higher for the throttle to work.
- **Gears.** Learn your system. EVELO notes its Enviolo automatic shifting is set through an app.

Levy adds that you should learn the display, which shows your speed and battery as you ride.

## How to ride an electric bike, step by step

How to ride an electric bike on your first outing, combining Levy, Urtopia, eurorad and EVELO:

1. **Pick a quiet place.** Flat, low traffic, no obstacles (Levy, Urtopia, EVELO). Urtopia suggests keeping first rides to about 15 to 20 minutes.
2. **Mount.** Swing your leg over the saddle, or step through the frame if it has one, then sit (EVELO).
3. **Power on and set the assist to the lowest level.** Urtopia says this is usually called Eco. EVELO says to start at level 0 if you are new and that levels 1 to 3 are a good starting point once comfortable. Those differ, so go with whichever your bike calls lowest.
4. **Push off.** Levy says to put one foot on the lowest pedal, push off, and put the other foot on once moving. Urtopia says to pedal normally and not fight the gentle boost. Eurorad warns that the initial thrust when starting off can be quite strong, which is why you start low.
5. **Pedal steadily.** Eurorad says a steady 60 to 80 RPM cadence is more efficient than irregular pedaling. That is one company's figure, not a test.
6. **Raise assist one level at a time** once comfortable (Urtopia, Levy). Higher modes use the battery faster (Urtopia).
7. **Shift gears.** Eurorad says to start in a low gear and shift up as you go, shift derailleurs with light pedal pressure and not when stopped or under full load, and shift hub gears while coasting. Shift down before a climb gets hard.
8. **Turn wide and gently at first** (Urtopia). Levy says to use hand signals for turns. None of the guides gives a detailed turning technique, so I won't invent one.
9. **Stop.** Urtopia says to stop pedaling, since the motor cuts out right away, then brake gently, put a foot down and turn the power off.

How to ride an e-bike up hills: EVELO says to pedal up with assist rather than relying only on the throttle, because throttle-only climbing strains the motor and cuts range, and to use the throttle at the top for a break.

## Braking on an e-bike

Every source says brake earlier and more gently than on a regular bike:
- Levy says e-bikes can be heavier and may take longer to stop, so practice smooth braking before riding busier routes.
- Eurorad says to use both hydraulic disc brakes together, modulate pressure, avoid sudden braking especially when turning or on wet surfaces, and practice at different speeds in a quiet area.
- EVELO says to squeeze both levers together and not squeeze the front lever too hard, and to keep your feet ready to reach the ground.
- NPS recommends motor-interrupt brake levers, which cut motor power when pulled, and disc brakes, saying rim brakes don't offer adequate stopping power for e-bikes.

**Which lever is which?** EVELO says in the U.S. the left lever usually controls the front brake and the right the rear. GCN says each lever controls either the front or the rear and to test both. They partly conflict, so test yours at walking speed, in a safe place, before you rely on it. Levy also says to test the front and rear brakes to learn how each responds. For maintenance and adjustment, see [how to adjust e-bike brakes](/how-to-tighten-and-adjust-electric-bike-and-ebike-brakes/). If your bike has hydraulic brakes and they feel wrong, a shop is the safer choice.

## Assist levels at a glance

| Source | Lowest | Middle | Highest |
|---|---|---|---|
| EVELO | Level 0 no help; 1 to 3 light to moderate | Levels 1 to 3 as a starting point | Levels 4 to 5 for steep hills |
| Eurorad | Eco or low for learning, flat terrain and saving battery | Medium for everyday riding, gentle inclines, headwinds | Turbo or high for steep climbs or quick acceleration |
| Urtopia | Lowest, usually Eco, before you move | Raise one level at a time | Uses battery faster |

Eurorad notes the motor assists only while you pedal and shuts off when you stop. GCN says assistance can be strong enough to surprise new riders, and gives limits of about 25 km/h in the UK, EU and Australia and about 20 mph in most of the U.S. Neither says how much of a boost each level gives, because it varies by bike.

## Throttle, classes and where you can ride

Urtopia says a bike with a throttle can move without pedaling, which makes it a Class 2 e-bike in the U.S., while Class 1 and 3 bikes need pedaling for motor help. NPS says many states follow Class I, II and III rules covering rider minimum ages, helmets and trail access, and that you should check the class sticker on the frame. NPS says Class III models can reach 28 mph. See my [e-bike classes guide](/what-are-the-different-e-bike-classes-class-1-2-3-and-4/).

Rules on where to ride vary. Levy says to review local traffic and e-bike laws, ride with the flow of traffic and be courteous to pedestrians. Urtopia says sidewalks are generally not allowed or advised and that local rules vary. My guides to [riding an e-bike on the road](/can-you-ride-or-drive-an-electric-bike-on-the-road-street-legal/) and [on the sidewalk](/can-you-ride-an-electric-bike-or-ebike-on-the-sidewalk/) go further. GCN says to avoid illegal, uncapped e-bikes, which can act like mopeds.

## A simple first-week plan

My own plan, built from the sources and labelled as opinion:
1. Day 1: 15 to 20 minutes on a flat, empty path on the lowest assist. Practice starting, stopping and braking until it is boring.
2. Days 2 to 3: raise the assist one level and practice gentle turns, signaling and shifting.
3. Later: try a quiet street with the flow of traffic, then a gentle hill using assist and not the throttle.
4. Throughout: helmet on, lights on at dusk, and charge the battery per the maker's instructions after rides.

## Frequently asked questions

### How to ride an e-bike for the first time?
Check the bike, start on the lowest assist level in a quiet flat place, push off, pedal steadily and brake gently with both levers.

### How to ride an e-bike without pedaling?
Only if it has a throttle. Urtopia says a throttle bike can move without pedaling and counts as Class 2 in the U.S.

### How to use an electric bike's assist levels?
Start at the lowest and raise one level at a time. EVELO says to save its top levels for steep hills.

### How to use an electric bike's throttle?
EVELO says it powers the bike without pedaling, can take off on its own, and on its bikes needs assist level 1 or higher.

### How to ride an electric bike uphill?
EVELO says pedal up with assist rather than throttle only, because throttle-only climbing strains the motor and cuts range.

### Which brake lever is front?
EVELO says left is usually front in the U.S.; GCN says it varies and to test both. Test yours.

### Do I need to pedal when starting?
Yes on pedal-assist bikes. Eurorad says the motor assists only while you pedal and cuts out when you stop.

### Do I need a helmet?
NPS calls a helmet the best safety step you can take, and many states require one depending on age and bike type.

### How long should my first ride be?
Urtopia suggests about 15 to 20 minutes on a flat, quiet route.

### What cadence should I use?
Eurorad says a steady 60 to 80 RPM is more efficient than irregular pedaling.

### Can I ride an e-bike on the sidewalk?
Urtopia says it is generally not allowed or advised and rules vary locally.

### How to use an electric bike in the rain?
No guide I read covered it. Eurorad says to avoid sudden braking on wet surfaces.

## The bottom line

How to ride an e-bike: check it, start on the lowest assist level in a quiet place, pedal steadily, raise assist one level at a time and brake early with both levers. How to use an electric bike's controls is mostly learning the assist buttons, the throttle if it has one, and the gears. Wear a helmet and keep first rides short.
