# Electric Bike Laws by State

Electric bike laws are set state by state, and the four states people ask about most differ a lot. In Pennsylvania, PennDOT says an e-bike is limited to 20 mph and 750 watts, the minimum age is 16, and helmets are required under 12. California uses a three-class system, requires a helmet for Class 3 riders and for anyone under 18, and sets 16 as the minimum age for Class 3. Florida treats an e-bike like a bicycle, with no license, registration or insurance. New Jersey changed its law in 2026: registration and a license or e-bike permit are now required, with insurance for the faster bikes, and advocates call it the most restrictive e-bike law in the country.

This page covers PA electric bike laws 2027, California electric bike laws 2027, Florida electric bike laws 2027 and New Jersey electric bike laws 2027, then where the sources disagree. I haven't tested any e-bike and I am not a lawyer. The sources are state agencies and statute text, [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/transportation/state-electric-bicycle-laws-a-legislative-primer), and news and advocacy coverage, and some are out of date, which I flag. Laws change, so check your state's official page before you buy or ride.

## PA electric bike laws 2027

PA electric bike laws 2027, per [PennDOT's micromobility information sheet](https://eastpetersburgborough.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/PennDOT-Micromobility-Info-Sheet-6-26.pdf) (PUB 944, marked 6-26):

- **Definition:** a "pedalcycle with electric assist," capable of not more than 20 mph on a level surface on motor power only, with a motor rated at not more than 750 watts, weighing no more than 100 lb with two or three wheels over 11 inches.
- **Minimum age:** 16.
- **Helmet:** required under 12. All pedalcycle laws apply to e-bikes.
- **License, registration, insurance:** the sheet lists none.
- **Where:** sidewalks outside a business district (yield to pedestrians); in a business district only where traffic-control devices permit; bike lanes allowed; freeways prohibited except for approved events; shoulders allowed.
- **Classes and trails:** the sheet does not address classes or trails.

NCSL's older primer (updated February 2021) listed Pennsylvania as defining e-bikes but outside the two classification systems, with a helmet requirement under 12. The same PennDOT sheet says e-scooters are "not specifically classified or defined in the PA Vehicle Code," which is a separate rule; see my [electric scooter laws guide](/how-old-do-you-have-to-be-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-age-limit/).

## California electric bike laws 2027

California electric bike laws 2027, from the Vehicle Code text on California Public Law ([section 312.5](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_312.5) updated January 1, 2025; [section 21213](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_21213) updated January 1, 2016). I did not open the official legislature site, so check it for newer changes.

- **Definition:** a bicycle with fully operable pedals and an electric motor of no more than 750 watts.
- **Class 1:** pedal assist only, stops assisting at 20 mph.
- **Class 2:** throttle-assisted, not capable of assisting at 20 mph and above.
- **Class 3:** pedal assist only, stops assisting at 28 mph, and must have a speedometer.
- **Label:** manufacturers and distributors must permanently affix a label showing class, top assisted speed and motor wattage.
- **Class 3 age:** "A person under 16 years of age shall not operate a class 3 electric bicycle."
- **Class 3 helmet:** riders and passengers must wear a properly fitted helmet meeting ASTM or CPSC standards.

NCSL's 2021 primer adds that under the general bicycle law, California riders under 18 must wear helmets, and that three-class states typically exempt e-bikes from registration, license and insurance. That primer is old, so confirm on the DMV or legislature site.

If you searched electric bike laws california 2027, these class rules are the answer; for electric bike laws california 2027 on where you can ride, see my guides to [riding an e-bike on the road](/can-you-ride-or-drive-an-electric-bike-on-the-road-street-legal/) and [on the sidewalk](/can-you-ride-an-electric-bike-or-ebike-on-the-sidewalk/).

## Florida electric bike laws 2027

Florida electric bike laws 2027, from [Florida Statutes section 316.20655](http://www.leg.state.fl.us/statutes/index.cfm?App_mode=Display_Statute&URL=0300-0399/0316/Sections/0316.20655.html) (history note ends with ch. 2025-149):

- **Status:** "An electric bicycle is a vehicle to the same extent as a bicycle," with the rights and duties of bicycle operators.
- **License, registration, insurance:** e-bikes and their operators are exempt from financial responsibility law and from "driver or motor vehicle licenses, vehicle registration, title certificates."
- **Age:** a local government may adopt an ordinance setting one or more minimum ages. No specific age is stated in the excerpt I read.
- **Where:** operators may ride "where bicycles are allowed," including streets, shoulders, bicycle lanes and multiuse paths. Local governments may regulate e-bikes on streets, sidewalks and sidewalk areas, and a path or trail manager may restrict or prohibit them.
- **Label and tampering:** manufacturers must label the classification number, top assisted speed and motor wattage; a person may not modify an e-bike to change its motor-powered speed unless the label is replaced.

The excerpt I read does not list classes or helmet rules. NCSL's 2021 primer says Florida riders under 16 must wear helmets, matching the general bicycle rule. For a better picture of Florida, check your city or county rules, which can differ.

## New Jersey electric bike laws 2027

New Jersey electric bike laws 2027 changed in 2026, and the sources do not fully agree on the details. The official page is the New Jersey Motor Vehicle Commission's "New E-bike Requirements Effective July 2026." Here is what [it says](https://www.nj.gov/mvc/vehicletopics/ebike.htm), with what the other sources add:

**From the MVC page**
- **Types:** low-speed electric bicycles, pedal assist only, up to 20 mph; and motorized bicycles or mopeds, defined by motor size, pedal assistance and throttle speed limits up to 28 mph. The page does not use "Class 1/2/3."
- **Age and license:** riders must be at least 15 and hold an e-bike license or a valid driver's license. Commercial rentals of low-speed e-bikes require the operator to be at least 16 and no license.
- **Registration and insurance:** all e-bikes must be registered with the MVC; only motorized bicycles require insurance. Registration involves an appointment, form BA-49EB, ID and proof of ownership, and plate stickers on both sides of the front fork.
- **License process:** a permit test, practice, then a road test 20 to 45 days later, with a helmet required for the road test. The permit exam fee is $5, waived until January 2027.
- **Out-of-state riders:** must be at least 15 and hold a valid license, and bikes must be registered.

**From other sources**
- [PeopleForBikes](https://www.peopleforbikes.org/news/new-jersey-most-restrictive-ebike-law) (January 21, 2026) says the law limits "low-speed electric bicycle" to Class 1 and treats Class 2 and Class 3 as motorized bicycles, and that all e-bikes must be registered and ridden by a license holder, with insurance for Class 2 and 3. It also says the law bans online advertising and sale of "electric motorized bicycles" over 750 watts that can exceed 28 mph. PeopleForBikes opposes the law.
- The [Bicycle Coalition of Greater Philadelphia](https://bicyclecoalition.org/governor-murphy-signs-e-bike-bill-and-some-questions-answered/) (January 22, updated January 27) says the law took effect immediately with a six-month grace period to July 20, 2026, that first-year registration is free, and that it found no age requirement. It warns the law is confusing and that police agencies shared incorrect information.
- The [New Jersey Monitor](https://newjerseymonitor.com/briefs/nj-ebike-law-registration-insurance/) (July 20, 2026) says the rules took effect that weekend, that riders need a license or special MVC e-bike license, that helmets are required for all riders, that fines are up to $50 per violation, and that as of July 15 the MVC had issued more than 4,100 registrations and 250 permits.

Where they differ I have not resolved it: the Monitor says helmets for all riders but the MVC page states a helmet only for the road test; the Coalition found no age requirement but the MVC page and the Monitor say 15. The official bill is S4834/A6235, and the MVC page is the authoritative summary.

## Where the sources disagree

- **New Jersey helmet:** Monitor says all riders; MVC page states no general rule.
- **New Jersey age:** MVC and Monitor say 15; the Bicycle Coalition found none.
- **New Jersey insurance:** PeopleForBikes says Class 2 and 3 need it; the MVC page says motorized bicycles; the Monitor article is unclear on low-speed bikes.
- **New Jersey timing:** immediate with a grace period to July 20 (Coalition) versus effective July 2026 (MVC).
- **NCSL's 2021 primer:** says New Jersey used a two-tier system and that 26 states use three classes; PeopleForBikes says 46 states use a three-class system. The first is stale and the second is an advocacy count, so I do not give a current number.
- **NCSL's helmet count:** its own page says both 25 states have no helmet requirement and at least 25 states and D.C. have one.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the PA electric bike laws 2027?
PennDOT says 20 mph and 750 W maximum, minimum age 16, helmets required under 12, and no license, registration or insurance listed.

### Can you ride an e-bike on the sidewalk in Pennsylvania?
PennDOT says sidewalks outside a business district are allowed with yielding to pedestrians, and in business districts only where traffic-control devices permit.

### What are the California electric bike laws 2027 classes?
Class 1 pedal assist to 20 mph, Class 2 throttle assist to 20 mph, Class 3 pedal assist to 28 mph with a speedometer.

### How old do you need to be to ride a Class 3 e-bike in California?
16, per Vehicle Code 21213, with a helmet required for rider and passengers.

### Do you need a license for an e-bike in California?
NCSL's older primer says three-class states typically exempt e-bikes. Confirm on the DMV site.

### Do you need a license for an e-bike in Florida?
No. The statute exempts e-bikes from driver licenses, vehicle registration, title and financial responsibility.

### What are Florida electric bike laws 2027 for age?
The statute lets local governments set a minimum age. NCSL's older primer says under 16 need helmets.

### Do you need to register an e-bike in New Jersey?
Yes. The MVC page says all e-bikes must be registered.

### Do you need a license for an e-bike in New Jersey?
Yes, a valid driver's license or an MVC e-bike license, with a minimum age of 15 per the MVC.

### Do you need insurance for an e-bike in New Jersey?
The MVC page says only motorized bicycles do. Low-speed e-bikes need registration, not insurance.

### What are the New Jersey electric bike laws 2027 penalties?
The Monitor reports fines of up to $50 per violation. The MVC page lists no penalties.

### Do I need to register an out-of-state e-bike in New Jersey?
The MVC page says out-of-state bikes must be registered and riders must be 15 with a valid license, though other sources call it ambiguous.

### Do all states use the three-class system?
NCSL's 2021 primer lists 26 states; PeopleForBikes says 46. I do not give a current number.

### Where can I find my e-bike's class?
California and Florida require a label with the class, top assisted speed and motor wattage.

## The bottom line

Electric bike laws by state come down to four things: class, age, helmet and license or registration. Pennsylvania is 20 mph and 750 W with a minimum age of 16; California uses three classes with Class 3 at 16 and helmets; Florida treats e-bikes like bicycles; and New Jersey now requires registration and a license or permit.
