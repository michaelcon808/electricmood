# Can You Ride an Electric Scooter in the Rain? 2027 Guide

Can you ride an electric scooter in the rain? Yes, in light rain, if your scooter has a genuine water-resistance rating of at least IPX4 and you ride gently. If it lists no rating, or the rain is heavy or the road is flooded, leave it at home. Every maker I read says some version of the same thing, and Apollo puts it this way: avoid heavy downpours and deep puddles, even on an IP54 scooter.

Can you leave an electric scooter outside or in the rain when you are not riding? Mostly no: makers say to keep it dry and indoors where you can, and a cover is the minimum if it has to stay out. The rest of this guide covers what decides which side of that line you are on: what the rating really promises, what rain does to a scooter and to you, and what to do before and after the ride so a drizzle does not become a repair bill. ## Can electric scooters be used in the rain? It depends on three things

Whether electric scooters can be used in the rain depends on your scooter's rating, how hard it is raining, and how much standing water is on the road. Here is how I would read it for each rating level:

| Your scooter | Drizzle or light rain | Steady rain | Heavy rain, storms, deep puddles |
|---|---|---|---|
| No IP rating listed | Don't ride | Don't ride | Don't ride |
| IPX4 or IP54 | Usually fine, ride gently | Avoid if you can | No |
| IPX5 or IP55 | Fine | Short rides only | No |
| IPX6 or IP66 | Fine | Fine | Avoid when you can |
| IPX7 | Fine | Fine | Avoid. Surviving a dunk is not an invitation to ride through a flood |

So, can you ride an electric scooter in light rain? On a rated scooter, yes. That table is my reading of what manufacturers say, not a standard, and there is no official rain standard for scooters. Gotrax, for example, says its scooters handle light to mild rain and tells riders to avoid deep puddles and heavy rainstorms, even though its G3 carries an IP54 rating. Your manual always outranks any table, mine included.

Before you decide for your own scooter, ask three things:

1. **Does my scooter have an IP rating, and what is it?** No rating means no rain riding.
2. **How much water is falling, and what is on the road?** Drizzle is not a thunderstorm, and a wet road is not a flooded one.
3. **Am I prepared?** A quick pre-ride check and a proper dry-down afterwards do more for a scooter's life than a higher IP number.

### Ride, drive or use: does the wording matter?

No. Can you ride and use electric scooters in the rain? The rated ones can, in light rain. Can you drive an electric scooter in the rain? Same answer, because "drive" just means ride in everyday speech. Can you use an electric scooter in the rain if you only have a short trip? The rating and the weather decide it, not the verb or the distance.

Can electric scooters be used in the rain when you commute on them every day? Yes, if they are rated and you dry them afterwards. Leaving one parked in the rain is a separate question, answered in its own section below.

## What an IP rating actually tells you

IP stands for ingress protection. The code comes from the international standard IEC 60529 and has two digits. The first (0 to 6) covers solid objects such as dust. The second (0 to 9) covers water. A scooter marked IP54 is protected against dust and splashing water. When a spec sheet says IPX4, the "X" means no data was gathered for the dust digit, according to the [Wikipedia article on the IP Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code).

Here is what the water digit means in lab terms:

| Second digit | Name | The test, in plain English |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Splashing water | Water splashed at the enclosure from any direction, 10 minutes on an oscillating fixture or at least 5 minutes with a spray nozzle (Wikipedia) |
| 5 | Water jets | A 6.3 mm nozzle at 12.5 litres a minute, at least 3 minutes, from any direction |
| 6 | Powerful water jets | A 12.5 mm nozzle at 100 litres a minute, at least 3 minutes |
| 7 | Temporary immersion | Submerged, with the lowest point 1 metre down. Testing-lab summaries give 30 minutes |

That is useful, but four things the rating does not tell you matter just as much:

- **It is a lab result on a new, sealed product.** A scooter doing 15 mph through road spray for 40 minutes is a different test from a nozzle held still for 3 minutes.
- **Seals do not last forever, and opening the scooter breaks them.** Apollo says breaking the seal, for example by opening the deck, can affect the water-resistance rating. That includes opening the deck to swap a battery or fix a wire.
- **The numbers do not stack above IPX6.** Wikipedia notes that water ratings are not cumulative beyond IPX6: a device that passes both a jet test and an immersion test is listed with both, such as IPX5/IPX7. So treat IPX7 as the immersion test only.
- **Many scooters have no rating at all.** Levy and Emoko both put the share without an IP rating at roughly two thirds (67 and 65 percent). Treat those as approximate. If you cannot find a rating, treat the scooter as unrated.

### How to find your scooter's IP rating

Check the spec page in the manual, the maker's product page, any label on the scooter, and the support site. If none gives a rating, email the maker and keep the reply. A brand that will not state a rating in writing is telling you something.

## Can electric scooters get wet in the rain? What water does to one

Can electric scooters get wet in the rain? Yes, and a rated scooter is built for exactly that. The problem is not one dramatic failure. It is slow damage: moisture creeping past a worn seal, corrosion on connectors, and a battery that does not love it.

**Where water gets in.** The weak points are the charging port, the seams around the deck and battery compartment, the point where cables enter the frame, and the controller. Electric Scooter Guide suggests inspecting the battery compartment and cable entry points if you ride in the wet a lot. One caution of mine: don't open the deck to reseal things yourself unless you know what you are doing, because that can void the rating and the warranty.

**The shock myth.** Electric Scooter Guide says you will not be electrocuted by riding in rain. The real risks are traction, visibility and damage to the scooter.

**The battery.** This is the one I would take seriously. Water in a lithium-ion pack can short cells, and fire services warn about these fires. eMobility Australia's advice for batteries exposed to floodwater is blunt: don't charge them, don't use them, and watch for swelling, corrosion or leaks, because internal damage can show up later. For background on the chemistry, the [Wikipedia article on lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) is a neutral place to start.

**Cold and freezing rain.** Unagi advises never riding in freezing rain, which can hurt battery and motor life and cause sudden shutdowns. Some Segway-Ninebot warranty documents also list freezing among the excluded causes of damage.

## Can you ride an electric scooter on wet roads?

Can you ride an electric scooter on wet roads once the rain stops? Yes, with a rated scooter, but the surface can stay slippery for hours, and what the rain does to you matters as much as what it does to the scooter.

- **Traction drops, but not as much as you would think.** Electric Scooter Guide reports measuring about 90 percent of dry-pavement traction on wet pavement, which suggests a small speed reduction goes a long way. Its testers also found manhole covers and wet leaves seriously slippery.
- **Puddles hide things.** Can you use an electric scooter in the rain when the road is flooded? No. Puddles hide potholes, drain grates and debris, and they throw water at the parts of your scooter that are hardest to seal.
- **Cold hands lose feel.** Ridester notes that cold rain numbs your hands, which makes smooth braking harder.
- **Other people see you less.** Rain, spray and fogged glass make you easier to miss. Emoko points out that a scooter's small wheels cope with road hazards less well than a bike's.

## Can I ride or use my electric scooter in the rain? A two-minute pre-ride check

If it is already drizzling, or you are checking before a commute, do this first:

- **Confirm the rating.** Know it before you leave, not at the first puddle.
- **Check the forecast.** A passing shower is one thing. A thunderstorm or flood warning means you wait.
- **Close the charging port cover.** Make sure the rubber plug is fully seated.
- **Test both brakes and the lights.** Rain reduces visibility fast.
- **Look at your tyres.** Check tread and pressure against the manual. Some brands suggest slightly lower pressure for grip, but I would stay inside the range in your manual.
- **Soften the settings.** If your scooter has an app, Electric Scooter Guide recommends a gentler launch and softer regenerative braking on wet roads.
- **Plan the route.** Skip rail tracks, painted crossings and metal plates if you can. Where you are allowed, pick quieter streets, because spray and traffic speed both get worse in the wet.

## How to ride a scooter in the rain

How to ride a scooter in the rain without drama? Mostly it comes down to being smoother than usual.

1. **Slow down.** Electric Scooter Guide suggests about 10 percent slower than normal, with gentler inputs on throttle, brake and handlebars. That is a sensible starting point, not a rule.
2. **Brake early and gradually.** Stopping distances get longer. Squeeze, don't grab.
3. **Avoid the slippery stuff.** Manhole covers, painted lines, metal plates, rail tracks and wet leaves are all worse than bare asphalt.
4. **Go around puddles.** Never ride into standing water you cannot see the bottom of.
5. **Take corners upright.** Slow down before the turn, not in it.
6. **Leave more room.** Give cars and cyclists extra following distance and look further ahead.
7. **Be seen.** Run your lights and add reflective gear.

Practise an emergency stop in an empty car park on a wet day before your first real rainy commute, so you learn your scooter's limits when you are not late for work. If you are new to riding altogether, start with my guide on [how to ride an electric scooter](/how-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-for-beginners-first-time/).

### Gear for riding in the rain

- **Helmet.** Always wear one, rain or not. Electric Scooter Guide points to a full-face helmet for keeping head and neck dry on a long wet ride.
- **Shoes with grip.** A wet deck is slippery.
- **Waterproof jacket and gloves.** Dry hands brake better.
- **Eye protection.** Rain in your eyes ruins your reaction time.
- **Reflective clothing.** Wear it even in the daytime when it is grey.

## After the ride: dry it out properly

Can you ride an electric scooter in the rain and get away with skipping the dry-down? Not for long. What you do afterwards decides whether rain is a harmless inconvenience or a slow killer.

1. **Power it off.**
2. **Towel it down.** Dry the deck, the base of the stem, the folding joint, the brakes and around the charging port.
3. **Park it indoors somewhere warm and ventilated.** Electric Scooter Guide recommends a warm indoor spot so it dries completely before the next ride.
4. **Don't plug it in right away.** Wait until the scooter is fully dry. The New York fire department also advises against charging directly after riding and says to charge in an open, dry area.
5. **Hold off on the next ride** until it is fully dry.
6. **Look it over.** Check for warning lights, a flickering display, odd noises, or a battery that feels hot or smells off.

## If your scooter gets soaked or dunked

If your scooter is caught in a downpour or dropped in a deep puddle:

1. **Stop riding and turn it off.**
2. **Don't charge it.**
3. **Dry it.** Apollo suggests removing the battery if the design allows, drying the scooter with a clean cloth and leaving it somewhere warm and dry for 24 to 48 hours. Apollo also mentions blowing compressed air carefully into crevices. My view: I would skip that unless your manual says it is fine, because it can push water further in.
4. **Check before powering on.** If anything seems wrong, call the maker.
5. **Watch the battery.** If it swells, leaks, smells or gets hot, stop using it, keep it away from anything flammable and get professional help.

Never use a pressure washer. High-pressure water spray is a common warranty exclusion.

## Can electric scooters be left in the rain? Parking outside

Can electric scooters be left in the rain, parked and unattended? A scooter that is rated for splashes is designed for the rain it meets while you ride, not for sitting in it for hours. Gyroor's guide says repeated exposure to rain can shorten a scooter's lifespan and lists battery short circuits or corrosion, motor faults, corroded wiring and rust among the risks. Gotrax's winter guide advises against leaving a scooter outdoors in heavy snow or rain, or exposed to standing water for long periods, and says mild rain and splashes are generally fine. Apollo's advice is to store the scooter indoors in a dry place, including overnight.

So can I leave my electric scooter in the rain? For a short wet spell, a rated scooter will probably cope, but I would not make it a habit. Can you leave an electric scooter in the rain overnight? Every maker I read says to keep it dry, and I would too. If you have no choice, here is what the sources suggest:

- **Use a snug waterproof cover.** Gyroor says it should fit snugly so wind does not blow it off, and that it guards against rain, snow and UV.
- **Keep it off wet ground in heavy rain** (Gyroor).
- **Bring a removable battery indoors** (Gyroor, Gotrax).
- **Lock it to a fixed object** with a heavy-duty U-lock or chain in a well-lit, busy place, and check local rules on where scooters may be parked (Gyroor).
- **Inspect it often** for rust, loose parts and electrical problems if it lives outside long term (Gyroor).

Can you leave a scooter out in the rain if it has no IP rating? I would not. An unrated scooter has no maker statement that it handles water at all, and water damage is typically excluded from warranty, as covered below. If it has already soaked, follow the soaked-scooter steps above before you touch the charger.

## Can I leave my electric scooter outside in winter?

Can I leave my electric scooter outside in winter? Cold is a bigger problem for the battery than for the frame. Apollo says that near or below freezing (0°C or 32°F), lithium-ion batteries can degrade over the long term, that cold also drains charge faster and shortens range, and that leaving a scooter outside in freezing conditions risks battery damage, rust and moisture problems. It recommends storing it indoors at room temperature, including overnight. Gotrax says long exposure to freezing temperatures can reduce range and may damage the battery, controller or other electronics.

**Charging after a cold night.** Both say not to charge a cold battery. Apollo says to let it reach room temperature first, and Gotrax says never to charge below freezing and to warm the battery indoors, avoiding overheating. The [Wikipedia article on lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) gives the chemistry: charging is recommended within 5 to 45°C, between 0 and 5°C the charge current should be reduced, and many cell types cannot be charged safely below 0°C, because metallic lithium can plate on the anode.

**Heat is a problem too.** Wikipedia says degradation increases for batteries stored or used at high temperature, usually above 35°C, and that charging above 45°C degrades performance. A scooter left in direct sun for hours is not helping its battery. Gyroor also lists long sun exposure as a wear factor, though it gives no threshold.

**Salt and slush.** Apollo says salt, slush and grime can corrode electrical components, so wipe the scooter down after every ride, paying attention to joints and ports. Gotrax says road salt can corrode metal parts and should be cleaned off regularly.

My own rule, not a maker's: if it is below freezing, the scooter lives indoors, and you let it warm up before you plug it in. For the right charge level when you store a scooter for the winter, see [how long an electric scooter battery lasts](/how-long-does-an-electric-scooter-battery-last-on-one-charge-and-how-to-make-it-last-longer/).

## Can I wash my electric scooter with water?

Can I wash my electric scooter with water? The two makers I read disagree on how much. Gyroor says you can clean it but only by wiping, never by spraying or soaking, and warns: "Never use a pressure washer or hose." It suggests a wrung-out damp microfibre cloth for the frame, handlebars and deck, mild soap and a soft brush only for stubborn dirt (then wipe dry), no submerging even on water-resistant models, no harsh chemicals and no automatic car washes, and drying with a towel before air-drying in a ventilated area. It also suggests turning the scooter off and removing the battery if possible first. Gotrax's winter guide, by contrast, says to wash the scooter with water and an occasional small amount of soap to remove grime and salt, and to dry it thoroughly afterwards.

I would follow the stricter advice: a damp cloth, no hose, no pressure washer, and extra care around the charging port, deck seams and stem base. That is my opinion, and if your manual says more or less than this, follow the manual.

## Does riding in the rain void the warranty?

An IP rating is not warranty coverage. Segway-Ninebot's warranty documents for several lines exclude damage from water submersion and high-pressure water spray, and some also list freezing. Its D series lists "water damage" itself. Apollo states that any damage caused by water exposure is not covered, including from riding in wet conditions. Electric Scooter Guide notes the same pattern across brands.

So can you ride an electric scooter in the rain and still keep your warranty? Not always, which is why this matters. Before you commit to rain riding, open your warranty PDF and search for "water", "moisture" and "submersion". Ask support in writing what is covered if the scooter is rated IP54 and gets rained on.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you ride an electric scooter in the rain?
Yes, in light rain, if your scooter has an IP rating of at least IPX4 and you ride carefully. Unrated scooters, storms and flooded roads are a no.

### Can electric scooters get wet in the rain?
Yes, that is what the IP rating is for. They can get wet, but they should not be submerged, sprayed with a pressure washer or left soaked.

### Can electric scooters be used in the rain if they are only IPX4?
Light rain, yes. IPX4 is a splash rating, so avoid steady downpours and standing water.

### Can you ride an electric scooter in light rain without damaging it?
Usually, if it is rated and you dry it afterwards. Heavy rain, puddles and pressure washing are where damage happens.

### Can you ride an electric scooter on wet roads after the rain stops?
Yes, with a rated scooter, but expect slippery manhole covers and leaves, and watch for standing water.

### Can you use an electric scooter in the rain when the road is flooded?
Never. Deep water can reach the battery, motor and electronics, and it hides hazards.

### Can I ride or use my electric scooter in the rain if it has no IP rating?
I wouldn't. Without a rating you have no evidence it can handle water, and the warranty probably won't cover damage.

### Can you ride and use electric scooters in the rain every day?
Rated models can handle regular wet commutes if you dry them properly. Daily exposure ages seals, so inspect them regularly.

### Can I ride and use my e scooter in the rain at night?
It is the hardest combination. Visibility drops sharply, so you need strong lights, reflective gear and a slower pace.

### Can you ride or drive a scooter in the rain if it isn't electric?
A kick scooter has no electronics to worry about, so grip and brakes matter most. A gas scooter is a different machine with its own manual. Everything else in this guide is about electric scooters.

### Can you drive an electric scooter in the rain on a main road?
Check local rules for where scooters are allowed first. In rain, main roads add faster traffic and more spray, so I would choose quieter streets.

### Can I ride in the rain on solid tyres?
You can, but solid tyres grip poorly on wet pavement, so I would avoid it. Air-filled tyres are the safer choice.

### Can I charge my scooter after riding in the rain?
Wait until it is completely dry first, and don't charge it right after a ride.

### Does riding in the rain void the warranty?
It can. Several makers exclude water damage, so read your warranty before relying on it.

### Can I wash my scooter with water?
A damp cloth, yes. Avoid hose or pressure-washer spray at the charging port, deck seams and stem base.

### How to ride a scooter in the rain safely?
Slow down, brake early, avoid slippery surfaces, skip puddles, stay visible and dry the scooter afterwards.

### Can electric scooters be left in the rain for a few hours?
A rated scooter can usually cope with a short wet spell, but repeated exposure shortens lifespan, per Gyroor. Cover it and dry it afterwards.

### Can I leave my electric scooter in the rain overnight?
I wouldn't. Makers advise indoor storage, and Apollo says to store it indoors even overnight.

### Can I leave my electric scooter outside in winter if it is covered?
A cover helps with rain and snow, but freezing temperatures can still hurt the battery, per Apollo and Gotrax. Keep it indoors below freezing.

### Can you leave a scooter out in the rain if the battery is removable?
Bring the battery indoors, as Gyroor and Gotrax advise, and cover the rest.

### Can you leave an electric scooter in the rain if it is IP54 or higher?
A rating covers splashes and light rain, not long exposure. Cover it and dry it afterwards.

### Can I wash my electric scooter with water if it has a high IP rating?
I would still use a damp cloth. Gyroor says never to use a hose or pressure washer, even on water-resistant models.

### Can I wash my electric scooter with water to remove road salt?
Apollo and Gotrax both say to clean salt off. Wipe with a damp cloth and dry it thoroughly.

### Can I charge my electric scooter after leaving it outside in the cold?
Not until it reaches room temperature, per Apollo and Gotrax.

## The bottom line

So, can you ride an electric scooter in the rain? Yes, if your scooter has a real rating and you treat light rain as the limit. Check the IP number in your manual, ride slower and smoother, stay out of puddles, dry the scooter properly and wait before charging. If your scooter has no rating, or it is pouring, the safest ride is the one you skip, and the safest parking spot is indoors.
