# Can You Ride an Electric Scooter in the Rain? The Real Answer

Short answer: yes, you can ride an electric scooter in the rain, but only some scooters, in some kinds of rain, and with a few habits most riders skip. If your scooter has a genuine water-resistance rating, light rain is fine. If it doesn't list one, keep it home when the sky turns gray.

That's the answer you came for. The rest of this guide covers the part that matters more: working out which side of that line your scooter sits on, what the rating really promises, what rain does to a scooter and to you, and what to do before and after the ride so a drizzle doesn't become a repair bill.

People type this question many ways. Whether you ask can I ride or use my electric scooter in the rain or can you drive an electric scooter in the rain, it's the same question underneath, and this guide answers all of it.

A note on how I put this together. I can't test every scooter on the market and I won't pretend otherwise. What I did was read the manufacturers' own rain guidance and warranty documents, the test definitions behind IP ratings, and fire-safety advice on lithium batteries. Every source is listed at the end, and where something is my opinion I say so. Last checked: October 4, 2026.

## The short answer: can electric scooters be used in the rain?

Whether electric scooters can be used in the rain depends on three things: your scooter's rating, how hard it's raining, and how much standing water is on the road. Here's how I'd read it for each rating level:

| Your scooter | Drizzle or light rain | Steady rain | Heavy rain, storms, deep puddles |
|---|---|---|---|
| No IP rating listed | Don't ride | Don't ride | Don't ride |
| IPX4 or IP54 | Usually fine, ride gently | Avoid if you can | No |
| IPX5 or IP55 | Fine | Short rides only | No |
| IPX6 or IP66 | Fine | Fine | Avoid when you can |
| IPX7 | Fine | Fine | Avoid. Surviving a dunk is not an invitation to ride through a flood |

So, can you ride an electric scooter in light rain? On a rated scooter, yes. That table is my reading of what manufacturers say, not a guarantee. There is no official "rain standard" for scooters. Gotrax, for example, says its scooters handle light to mild rain and tells riders to avoid deep puddles and heavy rainstorms, even though its G3 carries an IP54 rating. Your manual always outranks any table, mine included.

### Ride, drive or use: does the wording matter?

No. Can you ride and use electric scooters in the rain? The rated ones can, in light rain. Can you drive an electric scooter in the rain? Same answer, because "drive" just means ride in everyday speech. The rating and the weather decide it, not the verb.

### Riding in the rain versus parking in it

Can electric scooters be used in the rain when you commute on them every day? Yes, if they're rated and you dry them afterwards. Parking one outside is a separate question, and I cover it in [can you leave an electric scooter outside](/can-you-leave-an-electric-scooter-outside-in-the-rain/).

## Can you ride an electric scooter in the rain? Three questions to ask first

Before you decide for your own scooter, ask yourself these three things:

1. **Does my scooter have an IP rating, and what is it?** No rating means no rain riding.
2. **How much water is actually falling, and what's on the road?** Drizzle is not a thunderstorm, and a wet road is not a flooded one.
3. **Am I prepared?** A quick pre-ride check and a proper dry-down afterward do more for a scooter's life than a higher IP number.

The next sections take them in order.

## What an IP rating actually tells you

IP stands for ingress protection. The code comes from the international standard IEC 60529, and it has two digits. The first digit (0 to 6) covers solid objects like dust. The second digit (0 to 9) covers water. A scooter marked IP54 is dust-protected and handles splashing water. When a spec sheet says IPX4, the "X" simply means the dust digit wasn't tested.

Here's what the water digit means in lab terms:

| Second digit | Name | The test, in plain English |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Splashing water | Water splashed at the enclosure from any direction, about 10 minutes |
| 5 | Water jets | A 6.3 mm nozzle at about 12.5 litres a minute, at least 3 minutes, from any direction |
| 6 | Powerful water jets | A 12.5 mm nozzle at about 100 litres a minute, at least 3 minutes |
| 7 | Temporary immersion | Submerged up to 1 metre for 30 minutes |

That's useful, but four things the rating doesn't tell you matter just as much:

- **It's a lab result on a new, sealed product.** A scooter doing 15 mph through road spray for 40 minutes is a different test than a nozzle held still for 3 minutes.
- **Seals don't last forever, and opening the scooter breaks them.** Apollo, one of the brands that publishes a lot on this, says that once the factory seals are broken, the rating no longer applies. That includes opening the deck to swap a battery or fix a wire.
- **The numbers don't stack above IPX6.** IPX7 means the scooter passed the immersion test. It doesn't automatically mean it passed the jet tests. Treat each number as its own test.
- **Many scooters have no rating at all.** Retailers who've catalogued the market put the share without any IP rating at roughly two-thirds. If you can't find a rating, treat the scooter as unrated.

### How to find your scooter's IP rating

Check the spec page in the manual, the manufacturer's product page, any label on the scooter itself, and the support site. If none of those give a rating, email the manufacturer and keep the reply. A brand that won't state a rating in writing is telling you something.

## What rain does to a scooter

Can electric scooters get wet in the rain? Yes, they can get wet, and a rated scooter is built for exactly that. The problem isn't one dramatic failure. It's slow damage: moisture creeping past a worn seal, corrosion on connectors, and a battery that doesn't love it.

**Where water gets in.** The weak points are the charging port, the seams around the deck and battery compartment, the point where cables enter the frame, and the controller. Electric Scooter Guide suggests inspecting the battery compartment and cable entry points if you ride in the wet a lot. One caution of mine: don't open the deck to reseal things yourself unless you know what you're doing, because that can void the rating and the warranty.

**The shock myth.** You're not going to be electrocuted by riding through rain. The scooters are insulated, and the real risks are traction, visibility, and damage to the scooter.

**The battery.** This is the one I'd take seriously. Water in a lithium-ion pack can short cells, and fire services warn about these fires. eMobility Australia's advice for batteries exposed to floodwater is blunt: don't charge them, don't use them, and watch for swelling, corrosion, or leaks, because internal damage can show up later.

**Cold and freezing rain.** Unagi advises never riding in freezing rain, which can hurt battery and motor life and cause sudden shutdowns. Some Segway warranty documents also list freezing among the excluded causes of damage.

## What rain does to you

The scooter isn't the only thing at risk. A few facts change how I'd ride:

- **Wet roads are not the same as falling rain.** Can you ride an electric scooter on wet roads once the rain stops? Yes, with a rated scooter, but the surface can be slippery for hours.
- **Traction drops, but not as much as you'd think.** Electric Scooter Guide reports measuring about 90 percent of dry-pavement traction on wet pavement, which suggests a small speed reduction goes a long way. The same testers found manhole covers and wet leaves were seriously slippery.
- **Puddles hide things.** Can you use an electric scooter in the rain when the road is flooded? No. Puddles hide potholes, drain grates, and debris. They also throw water at the parts of your scooter that are hardest to seal.
- **Cold hands lose feel.** Cold rain numbs your hands, which makes smooth braking harder.
- **Other people see you less.** Rain, spray, and fogged glass make you easier to miss. A scooter's small wheels also cope with road hazards less well than a bike's.

## Can I ride or use my electric scooter in the rain? A 2-minute pre-ride check

If it's already drizzling, or you're checking before a commute, do this first:

- **Confirm the rating.** Know it before you leave, not at the first puddle.
- **Check the forecast.** A passing shower is one thing. A thunderstorm or flood warning means you wait.
- **Close the charging port cover.** Make sure the rubber plug is fully seated.
- **Test both brakes and the lights.** Rain reduces visibility fast.
- **Look at your tires.** Check tread and pressure against the manual. Some brands suggest slightly lower pressure for grip, but I'd stay inside the range in your manual.
- **Soften the settings.** If your scooter has an app, Electric Scooter Guide recommends a gentler launch and softer regenerative braking on wet roads.
- **Plan the route.** Skip rail tracks, painted crossings, and metal plates if you can. Where you're allowed, pick quieter streets, because spray and traffic speed both get worse in the wet.

## How to ride a scooter in the rain

Can you ride or drive a scooter in the rain without drama? Mostly it comes down to being smoother than usual.

1. **Slow down.** Electric Scooter Guide suggests about 10 percent slower than normal, and gentler inputs on the throttle, brake, and handlebars. That's a sensible starting point, not a rule.
2. **Brake early and gradually.** Stopping distances get longer. Squeeze, don't grab.
3. **Avoid the slippery stuff.** Manhole covers, painted lines, metal plates, rail tracks, and wet leaves are all worse than bare asphalt.
4. **Go around puddles.** Never ride into standing water you can't see the bottom of.
5. **Take corners upright.** Slow down before the turn, not in it.
6. **Leave more room.** Give cars and cyclists extra following distance and look further ahead.
7. **Be seen.** Run your lights and add reflective gear.

Practice an emergency stop in an empty car park on a wet day before your first real rainy commute. It's better to learn your scooter's limits when you're not late for work. ## Gear for riding in the rain

- **Helmet.** Always wear one, rain or not. A full-face helmet keeps your head and neck dry, which is a real comfort gain on a long wet ride.
- **Shoes with grip.** A wet deck is slippery.
- **Waterproof jacket and gloves.** Dry hands brake better.
- **Eye protection.** Rain in your eyes ruins your reaction time.
- **Reflective clothing.** Wear it even in the daytime when it's gray.

## After the ride: dry it out properly

What you do afterwards decides whether rain is a harmless inconvenience or a slow killer.

1. **Power it off.**
2. **Towel it down.** Dry the deck, the base of the stem, the folding joint, the brakes, and around the charging port.
3. **Park it indoors somewhere warm and ventilated.** Electric Scooter Guide recommends a warm indoor spot so it can dry completely before the next ride.
4. **Don't plug it in right away.** Wait until the scooter is fully dry. New York's fire department also advises against charging directly after riding and says to charge in an open, dry area.
5. **Hold off on the next ride.** Only head out again once it's fully dry.
6. **Look it over.** Check for warning lights, flickering displays, odd noises, or a battery that feels hot or smells off.

## If your scooter gets soaked or dunked

If your scooter is caught in a downpour or dropped in a deep puddle:

1. **Stop riding and turn it off.**
2. **Don't charge it.**
3. **Dry it.** Apollo suggests removing the battery if the design allows, drying the scooter with a clean cloth, and leaving it somewhere warm and dry for 24 to 48 hours. Apollo also mentions blowing compressed air into crevices. My view: I'd skip that unless your manual says it's fine, because it can push water further in.
4. **Check before powering on.** If anything seems wrong, call the manufacturer.
5. **Watch the battery.** If it swells, leaks, smells, or gets hot, stop using it, keep it away from anything flammable, and get professional help.

Never use a pressure washer. High-pressure water spray is a common warranty exclusion.

## Warranty: the part nobody reads

Here's a fact that surprises people. An IP rating is not warranty coverage. Segway-Ninebot's warranty documents for several lines exclude damage from water submersion and high-pressure water spray, and some also list freezing. Its D series lists "water damage" itself. Apollo's page carries a disclaimer that water damage isn't covered, including from riding in wet conditions. Electric Scooter Guide notes the same pattern across brands.

Can you ride in the rain and still keep your warranty? Not always, which is why this matters. Before you commit to rain riding, open your warranty PDF and search for "water", "moisture", and "submersion". Ask support in writing what's covered if the scooter is rated IP54 and gets rained on.

## What to look for if you ride in the rain often

If rain is part of your regular commute, buy for it. Based on what testers and manufacturers say, these are the features I'd weigh:

- **A high IP rating.** IPX5 or better if you can get it.
- **Air-filled tires.** Electric Scooter Guide avoids solid tires in its wet-weather list because they grip poorly on wet surfaces.
- **Rear-wheel drive, or the option to switch off the front motor.** Electric Scooter Guide explains that front-wheel acceleration on a slippery road can spin the front tire in a corner.
- **Brakes that don't lock easily.** Drum brakes combined with regenerative braking are less prone to skidding.
- **Full fenders.** They keep the spray off you and off the electronics.
- **Bright lights and a brake light.**
- **Good deck grip.**

## Frequently asked questions

### Can you ride an electric scooter in the rain?
Yes, in light rain, if your scooter has an IP rating of at least IPX4 and you ride carefully. Unrated scooters, storms, and flooded roads are a no.

### Can electric scooters get wet in the rain?
Yes, that's what the IP rating is for. They can get wet, but they shouldn't be submerged, sprayed with a pressure washer, or left soaked.

### Can electric scooters be used in the rain if they are only IPX4?
Light rain, yes. IPX4 is a splash rating, so avoid steady downpours and standing water.

### Can you ride an electric scooter in light rain without damaging it?
Usually, if it's rated and you dry it afterwards. Heavy rain, puddles, and pressure washing are where damage happens.

### Can you ride an electric scooter on wet roads after the rain stops?
Yes, with a rated scooter, but expect slippery manhole covers and leaves, and watch for standing water.

### Can you use an electric scooter in the rain when the road is flooded?
Never. Deep water can reach the battery, motor, and electronics, and it hides hazards.

### Can I ride or use my electric scooter in the rain if it has no IP rating?
I wouldn't. Without a rating you have no evidence it can handle water, and the warranty probably won't cover damage.

### Can you ride and use electric scooters in the rain every day?
Rated models can handle regular wet commutes if you dry them properly. Daily exposure ages seals, so inspect them regularly.

### Can I ride and use my e scooter in the rain at night?
It's the hardest combination. Visibility drops sharply, so you need strong lights, reflective gear, and a slower pace.

### Can you ride or drive a scooter in the rain if it isn't electric?
A kick scooter has no electronics to worry about, so grip and brakes matter most. A gas scooter is a different machine with its own manual. Everything else in this guide is about electric scooters.

### Can you drive an electric scooter in the rain on a main road?
Check local rules for where scooters are allowed first. In rain, main roads add faster traffic and more spray, so I'd choose quieter streets.

### Can I ride in the rain on solid tires?
You can, but solid tires grip poorly on wet pavement, so I'd avoid it. Air-filled tires are the safer choice.

### Can I charge my scooter after riding in the rain?
Wait until it's completely dry first, and don't charge it right after a ride.

### Does riding in the rain void the warranty?
It can. Several manufacturers exclude water damage, so read your warranty before relying on it.

### Can I wash my scooter with water?
A damp cloth, yes. Avoid hose or pressure-washer spray at the charging port, deck seams, and stem base.

### How to ride a scooter in the rain safely?
Slow down, brake early, avoid slippery surfaces, skip puddles, stay visible, and dry the scooter afterwards.

## The bottom line

So, can you ride an electric scooter in the rain? Yes, if your scooter has a real rating and you treat light rain as the limit. Check the IP number in your manual, ride slower and smoother, stay out of puddles, dry the scooter properly, and wait before charging. If your scooter has no rating, or it's pouring, the safest ride is the one you skip. Whether you call it an electric scooter or an e scooter, the question "can I ride and use my e scooter in the rain" comes down to the same thing: the rating, the weather, and how you care for it afterwards.
