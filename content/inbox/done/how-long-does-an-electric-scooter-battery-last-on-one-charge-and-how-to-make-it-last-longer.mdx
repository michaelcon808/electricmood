# How Long Does an Electric Scooter Battery Last? 2027

How long does an electric scooter last on one charge? Usually about 1 to 2 hours of riding. Levy puts it at "1-2 hours of continuous use", and tested scooters agree: by my arithmetic from ERideHero's range tests, a Segway Ninebot Max G2 ran about 2 hours at a 15 mph average, and an Apollo Explore ran anywhere from 51 minutes to 2.5 hours depending on speed. The battery itself, meaning how many years it lasts before it is worn out, is a different question. Sources put it at roughly 2 to 5 years, depending on how often you charge, and the habits that stretch it are covered below, including how often to charge and how to store it.

Both answers depend on a few numbers you can find yourself, and this guide gives the sums. I focus on ride time here, not distance, and on how to make your electric scooter battery last longer. For miles per charge, see my guide to [how far an electric scooter can go on one charge](/how-far-and-how-many-miles-can-an-electric-scooter-go/).

I haven't ridden or run-tested any of these scooters. The ride times come from ERideHero's and Tom's Guide's published tests, battery science from Wikipedia, and lifespan estimates from EScooterNerds, iScooter and Levy, which I label as estimates. Checked October 10, 2026.

## How long a scooter lasts on one charge: tested ride times

Distance is what most reviews report, so I converted tested ranges into time. The formula is simple: **ride time = tested range ÷ average speed** in the test. The ride times below are my arithmetic, not stopwatch readings, and ERideHero ran its tests with a 175 lb rider on controlled routes.

| Scooter and test | Battery | Tested range | Average speed | Ride time (my sum) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Apollo Explore, speed priority | 946.4 Wh | 21.2 mi | 25 mph | about 51 minutes |
| Apollo Explore, regular | 946.4 Wh | 29.3 mi | 17.5 mph | about 1 h 40 min |
| Apollo Explore, range priority | 946.4 Wh | 31.4 mi | 12.5 mph | about 2 h 31 min |
| Segway Ninebot Max G2, regular | 551 Wh | 29.8 mi | 15.2 mph | about 1 h 58 min |

Tom's Guide gives a rougher case: it rode an Apollo City in Sport mode for 9 miles at an average of 19 mph and used 43 percent of the battery. By my proportion that is about 28 minutes for 43 percent, or roughly 66 minutes for a full charge. That is an extrapolation from one ride, not a tested result, and

If you are shopping for run time, my [long-range scooter guide](/best-affordable-top-long-range-electric-scooters-for-adults-longest-range-reviews/) and [commuter scooter guide](/best-commuter-fast-electric-scooters-comparison-for-daily-city-commuting-reviews/) list models by range.

Notice the spread. The same Apollo Explore ranged from under an hour to over two and a half hours, only because of the speed. So the honest answer to how long does a scooter last on full charge, or how long does an electric scooter battery last per charge, is "about 1 to 2 hours at normal commuting speed", which matches Levy's "1-2 hours of continuous use".

Claimed times are less reliable: iScooter's page says "about one hour" in one place and 4 to 4.5 hours at 10 km/h for its own model in another, without explaining how either was measured.

## How many hours a scooter battery lasts: the formula

You can estimate how many hours does an electric scooter battery last, and how many hours does a scooter battery last on a bigger or smaller pack, with two numbers. The same sum answers how many hours does an electric scooter battery last for your pack, how long do electric scooters last on full battery and how long do electric scooters last on one charge for your own model.

1. **Battery size in watt-hours (Wh).** From the spec sheet, or volts times amp-hours.
2. **Energy use in Wh per mile.** ERideHero tested 78 scooters with a 175 lb rider at a moderate speed on mixed terrain until the battery was empty and averaged 26.3 Wh per mile.

**Ride time ≈ battery Wh ÷ (Wh per mile × average mph).**

Whether you ask how many hours does an electric scooter last or how long does an e scooter battery last for a bigger rider, the sum is the same. Take the 551 Wh Max G2 at the 78-scooter average and a 15 mph pace. The power draw is about 26.3 × 15 = 395 W, so the battery lasts about 551 ÷ 395 = 1.4 hours, or about 84 minutes. The Max G2's own tested figure was almost 2 hours, which means it used less energy than the average. So the formula gives a ballpark, and a model's own test beats it.

The same sum explains why how many hours does a scooter battery last changes with every ride. Faster means more watts, and the trip is shorter. Hills, rider weight, cold and hard acceleration all raise Wh per mile, as Levy and the Gyroor and ERideHero pages note.

## How long does 3 bars last on an electric scooter?

Battery bars are a rough readout, and sources disagree on what they mean. Gyroor's page says three bars typically represent about 50 to 75 percent of total capacity and gives 5 to 15 miles, which it says is "not an exact science". Levy's page says many scooters give "15-25% of total range per bar", so three bars would be about 3 to 5 miles on a 20-mile scooter. That second figure doesn't add up (three bars at 15 to 25 percent is 45 to 75 percent), so I wouldn't rely on it.

To answer how long does 3 bars last on an electric scooter for your model, do the sum yourself, which is also how long does a fully charged electric scooter last scaled down, and how long does a scooter battery charge last in bars:

1. Count the bars on a full battery (say 5).
2. Divide: 3 of 5 is about 60 percent.
3. Multiply by your full-charge ride time, for example 60 percent of 1 h 58 min is about 70 minutes at the same pace.

Bars aren't guaranteed to be evenly spaced, so treat the result as a guide, and recharge before you reach the last bar, as Gyroor suggests. Check your manual for what the display means.

## How long does electric scooter battery last in a day?

A scooter doesn't have to last a whole day on one charge; it has to cover the day's trips, so how long does an electric scooter charge last is really about your trips. That is the practical answer to how long does a battery last on an electric scooter in a normal week, and how long does an electric scooter battery charge last across several short rides. If your ride time is about 2 hours and your commute is 30 minutes each way, you use roughly half the battery per day (1 hour of 1.96 hours is about 51 percent), my arithmetic. That suggests charging every second day at most, and charging when you get home is simpler.

The honest limit is the pack, and how long does the battery last on an electric scooter in a day of trips is just the pack divided by your daily use. Sources describe 1 to 2 hours of continuous riding at normal speeds, so how long can you ride an electric scooter without stopping is mostly set by the battery, not the motor. Any longer day needs a second pack or a charge in the middle. Levy suggests a removable battery so you can swap in a charged one, and I'd treat that as a claim to check for your model.

## How long the battery itself lasts: years and charge cycles

Now the other meaning of how long does an electric scooter battery last: how many years until it is worn out. In other words, how long do batteries last on electric scooters, and how long does the electric scooter battery last before it needs replacing.

**What the science says.** On how long can an electric scooter battery last with careful use, the best-supported answer is a few years. Wikipedia says manufacturers usually quote cycle life as the number of cycles until capacity falls to 80 percent of its rated value, lists 400 to 1,200 cycles for lithium-ion batteries in general, and says that at 25 °C they are expected to lose around 20 percent of their cyclable charge in 3 to 5 years. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) adds that high charge levels, heat (especially above 35 °C) and fast charging all hasten capacity loss, and that keeping a battery at about 60 to 80 percent can reduce loss.

**What scooter sources say.** They are estimates, not tests:

- EScooterNerds says 2 to 3 years or 300 to 500 charge cycles, "whichever comes first", with some batteries reaching 5 years and poor ones under 1. It says daily charging means 1 to 2 years. The page credits an editor but doesn't describe a test.
- iScooter says 3 to 5 years, after which endurance drops noticeably.
- Levy's page doesn't give a lifespan.

**My arithmetic.** If each charge is one full cycle, then 400 to 1,200 cycles lasts 2.2 to 6.6 years charging every other day and 1.1 to 3.3 years charging daily. That fits the 1 to 2 and 2 to 5 year estimates. It's a rough conversion, since partial charges count as fractions of a cycle.

**What the maker promises.** Warranty terms show how makers see it. Segway's warranty document for the Ninebot Max G2 (last updated December 2022) covers the battery assembly for 6 months, against 2 years for the frame, and excludes improper charging and storage. Terms vary by maker and market, so check your own.

The sections below cover what you can do about wear. For the distance view, see [how many miles an electric scooter lasts](/how-many-miles-does-an-electric-scooter-last/).

## How often should you charge an electric scooter?

People ask how often should I charge my electric scooter, and how often should I charge my scooter battery, because the advice conflicts. Here is what each source says. None of it is tested, and I haven't found a maker's test either.

| Source | Advice | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Levy | Charge after every ride or when below 20 percent; don't wait until fully drained | Retailer |
| scooter.guide | Charge after every ride, even short ones; don't run flat; unplug when full; let it cool first | Blog, nothing tested |
| Gyroor | Charge when it reaches 20 to 30 percent | Retailer |
| Aventura-X | Avoid frequent top-offs; let it fall to roughly 20 to 30 percent first | Retailer |
| Unagi | Charge after each ride, avoid dropping below 20 to 30 percent, and charging to 80 to 90 percent saves time and helps battery life | Brand |
| Wikipedia | Charging beyond 80 percent can accelerate degradation; keeping a battery at about 60 to 80 percent can reduce loss | Encyclopedia |

The sources agree on two things: don't run it flat, and don't leave it sitting full. They split on top-ups: Levy and scooter.guide say charge after each ride, Aventura-X says avoid frequent top-offs. My reading, which is judgment and not a test, is to plug in when you have used about half or when it nears 20 to 30 percent, stop at 80 to 90 percent if your scooter lets you, and follow your manual if it says otherwise.

Whether you can stop at 80 percent depends on the scooter. ERideHero notes the Segway Ninebot Max G2 app has a charging-limit option and that limiting to 80 percent suits storing it for several weeks, while Apollo's support site says its scooters don't currently support charge limit settings. If yours has no limit, unplugging by hand is the only option. For the charging steps themselves, see my [charging guide](/how-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery-at-home-and-know-if-it-is-charging/).

## How to make your electric scooter battery last longer

If you want to know how to make your electric scooter battery last longer, or how to make electric scooter battery last longer in general, and how to make your e scooter battery last longer too, these habits come from the sources above, with the science-backed ones first. The science part is in [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery); the rest are retailer and blog advice, so I mark them as such.

1. **Keep it away from heat.** Wikipedia says degradation is strongly temperature-dependent, minimal at room temperature and faster at high temperatures, usually above 35 °C. scooter.guide gives about 20 °C as ideal, says performance drops below 0 °C or above 40 °C and says not to charge in direct sunlight.
2. **Don't leave it full or flat.** Wikipedia says storing a lithium-ion battery in a charged state reduces its capacity and that high charge levels hasten loss; it also warns that discharging far too low can damage the cell.
3. **Let it cool before you plug in.** Levy suggests waiting 30 to 60 minutes after a ride, and the London Fire Brigade gives the same advice for safety.
4. **Use the original charger.** scooter.guide says the wrong voltage or capacity can damage the battery. See [charging without the original charger](/how-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery-without-a-charger/) if yours is missing.
5. **Ride smoothly.** Gyroor and scooter.guide both say to accelerate gradually, keep a steady speed and use eco mode where there is one. My ride-time table above shows how big the speed effect is.
6. **Keep tyres inflated and the load light.** Both pages list tyre pressure and extra weight as things that raise energy use.
7. **Use regenerative braking if your scooter has it.** scooter.guide recommends it, and Gyroor says it can extend range a little but won't fully recharge the pack.
8. **Keep the connections clean.** scooter.guide says to keep them free of corrosion and secure.

EScooterNerds claims tips like these extend battery life by 20 to 35 percent, but shows no test, so I wouldn't quote that as a result.

## How to store an electric scooter battery

Storage matters because a scooter that sits for weeks ages whether you ride it or not, so how long does a battery last on a scooter that is stored depends on charge level and temperature. Wikipedia describes calendar life as degradation during storage as well as cycling. Maker and retailer advice is close, with one conflict:

- **Charge level.** Levy says to store at about 50 to 70 percent, and says storing at 100 percent can slowly degrade capacity. INMOTION's blog says 50 to 70 percent, around 50 percent for long-term storage. Gyroor and Aventura-X say 50 to 60 percent. ERideHero's G2 review says an 80 percent limit suits storing it for several weeks. So the sources range from 50 to 80 percent, and 50 to 70 percent is the common middle.
- **Temperature.** INMOTION gives 0 °C to 25 °C (32 to 77 °F), out of direct sunlight and never where it can freeze. scooter.guide gives about 20 °C.
- **Check on it.** INMOTION says to check the battery every month or two and top it up if needed.
- **Where.** Indoors, such as a garage, basement or closet, is preferred. INMOTION recommends a waterproof cover if it must stay outside.

## Why does my electric scooter die so fast?

If you are asking why does my electric scooter die so fast, start with the cheap causes before blaming the battery. Gyroor lists five. I add what to check:

| Cause (Gyroor) | What to check |
|---|---|
| Battery age and degradation | Compare your ride time with the tested figures above; scooter.guide lists reduced range and more frequent charging as signs of a failing battery |
| Riding habits | Hard acceleration, frequent braking and top speed use more energy; try eco mode and a steadier pace |
| Terrain and weight | Hills, rough ground and heavy loads raise power demand |
| Temperature | Gyroor says cold weather reduces battery efficiency; heat speeds up wear |
| Poor maintenance | Low tyre pressure raises energy use |

If none of those explain it, the battery may be worn. scooter.guide lists the signs: range much shorter than before, charging more often, slower top speed, difficulty holding charge, overheating, and swelling or leakage. scooter.guide says to replace a swollen or leaking battery immediately, and EScooterNerds says that consistently getting half or less of your usual range probably means it's time for a new battery.

## Can you charge an electric scooter while riding?

Not in any practical way. Gyroor's page on the question says regenerative braking can recover some energy that would otherwise be lost as heat and so extend range, but doesn't fully recharge the scooter. It says solar panels aren't enough and wireless charging is experimental. Hiboy's warnings add "don't charge while riding".

Meanwhile, how long can you ride an electric scooter on a long trip comes down to the pack. So can you charge an electric scooter while riding? Only a little, through regenerative braking on models that have it. For a longer day, plan a charge stop or a spare battery if your model has a removable one, as Levy suggests, and use the ride-time formula above to know when you will need it.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long does an electric scooter last on one charge?
Usually about 1 to 2 hours of riding at normal speed. Levy says 1 to 2 hours of continuous use, and ERideHero's tests convert to about 2 hours for the Max G2.

### How long do electric scooters last on one charge in miles?
That is a distance question. Tested scooters in the sources above go roughly 20 to 30 miles; see my range guide for details.

### How long does an electric scooter battery last per charge?
About 1 to 2 hours of riding, or roughly 15 to 25 miles per Levy. Faster riding shortens it a lot, as the Apollo Explore tests show.

### How long does the battery last on an electric scooter before it needs replacing?
Estimates run from about 2 to 5 years, depending on how often you charge. EScooterNerds says daily charging means 1 to 2 years.

### How long does a fully charged electric scooter last?
About 1 to 2 hours in motion. If you only use it for short trips, a full charge can last a few days.

### How long does a scooter last on full charge when it is cold?
Less. Gyroor says cold weather reduces battery efficiency, but I haven't found a tested figure.

### How long do electric scooters last on full battery at top speed?
Much less. The Apollo Explore speed-priority test (25 mph average) works out to about 51 minutes.

### How long does a scooter battery charge last when the display shows 3 bars?
It depends on how many bars the display has. Sources give conflicting figures, so work it out as a share of your full-charge ride time.

### How long does an electric scooter charge last on a short commute?
Several days for a 20 to 30 minute trip each way, since a full charge gives about 1 to 2 hours of riding. That is my arithmetic, not a test.

### How long does electric scooter battery last in a day of delivery work?
A long shift can need more than one charge or a spare pack. At 1 to 2 hours of riding per charge, a full day of constant riding will not fit on one.

### How long can an electric scooter battery last if I look after it?
Longer. Avoiding heat, storing at about 50 to 70 percent and not draining it flat are what the sources list for reducing loss.

### How long do batteries last on electric scooters in general?
Estimates are 2 to 5 years. Wikipedia says lithium-ion batteries lose around 20 percent of their cyclable charge in 3 to 5 years at 25 °C.

### How long does a battery last on an electric scooter that is used daily?
About 1 to 2 years, per EScooterNerds, or 1.1 to 3.3 years by my cycle arithmetic.

### How long does a battery last on a scooter that sits unused?
Calendar aging still happens. Wikipedia says storing a lithium-ion battery in a charged state reduces its capacity over time.

### How long does an e scooter battery last for a heavy rider?
Less per charge. Levy and Gyroor both list rider weight as a factor, but I haven't found a tested figure.

### How long does an electric scooter battery charge last in standby?
I haven't found a tested figure. Leaving a lithium pack fully charged for long periods reduces capacity, according to Wikipedia.

### How many hours does an electric scooter last for a delivery rider?
About 1 to 2 hours of riding per charge, so a long shift needs a charge stop or a spare pack.

### How long does the electric scooter battery last in years if I only ride at weekends?
Likely more calendar years but fewer cycles. Age and storage then matter more than cycles.

### How often should I charge my electric scooter?
Sources conflict. Levy says after each ride or below 20 percent; Aventura-X says avoid frequent top-offs. A middle path is to charge around 20 to 30 percent and stop at 80 to 90 percent if you can.

### How often should I charge my scooter battery if I ride rarely?
Check it every month or two while it is stored, per INMOTION, and keep it at about 50 to 70 percent.

### How to make electric scooter battery last longer in hot weather?
Keep it out of direct sun and away from heat; Wikipedia says degradation rises at high temperatures, usually above 35 °C, and scooter.guide says not to charge in direct sunlight.

### How to make your e scooter battery last longer in winter?
Store it indoors, never where it can freeze, and warm it before charging. Wikipedia says many lithium-ion cells can't be charged safely below 0 °C.

### Why does my electric scooter die so fast?
Check cold weather, speed, load, hills and tyre pressure first. If range is half what it was, the battery is probably worn.

### Can you charge an electric scooter while riding?
Only a little, through regenerative braking on models that have it. There is no practical way to plug in and ride.

## The bottom line

So, how long does an electric scooter last on one charge? About 1 to 2 hours of riding at normal speeds, less at top speed and more at an easy pace, as the Apollo Explore tests show. The pack itself usually lasts 2 to 5 years, closer to 1 to 2 if you charge it fully every day. Use ride time = range ÷ average speed to check your own model, and compare it with the tested figures above.

The best thing you can do for both numbers is look after the battery: avoid heat, avoid leaving it fully charged for long periods and don't drain it flat. I haven't run any of these scooters myself, so treat the lifespan figures as estimates and the ride times as my arithmetic from published tests.
