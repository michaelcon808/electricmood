# How to Charge an E-Scooter and Know It's Charging 2027

How do you charge an electric scooter battery? Use the charger that came with it, connect it to the scooter's charge port, plug it into a wall outlet, and unplug it when the charger light and the scooter's display say it is full. On most chargers, a red light means charging and green means full, though it varies by model, so the manual is the last word. That also answers how to know if your electric scooter is charging: watch the charger light, the scooter's display or app, and a normal charge time.

Both questions have catches, so this guide covers how to charge a scooter battery at home and how to know if your scooter is charging together. Charger lights are not a universal code: green can mean full, or it can mean nothing is flowing. Makers even disagree on whether to plug in the scooter or the wall first. Below is the step-by-step process, what the lights mean according to named sources, a safe checklist if nothing happens, and the fire-safety rules from the CPSC and the London Fire Brigade.

I haven't charged or tested any of these scooters myself. The guidance comes from Xiaomi's support page, Apollo's support site, Levy, Gyroor, GreenMoov, Ride Review, Wikipedia, the US Consumer Product Safety Commission and the London Fire Brigade. Maker pages are labelled as such, and I note where an answer is unattributed. Checked October 10, 2026.

## How to charge an electric scooter battery at home, step by step

This is how to charge a scooter battery at home in the order most guides give, and how to charge electric scooter battery packs safely without guessing. Steps 1 and 2 come up in every source I read; the plug-in order in step 4 is where they differ.

1. **Read the manual.** Levy's guide puts this first, because charge ports, light codes and charge times differ by model.
2. **Use the charger in the box.** Levy says to use only the charger the manufacturer supplies, and the CPSC's charging advice says the same: "only use the supplied charger." A non-maker charger can also void the warranty, per Levy.
3. **Pick a spot.** Dry, cool and ventilated, away from heat and wet. Gyroor says to charge in a well-ventilated area; the London Fire Brigade says not to charge on an escape route such as a hallway.
4. **Connect it.** Open the port cover, plug the charger lead into the scooter, then plug the charger into the wall. That is the order in Xiaomi's support page for its Electric Scooter 4 Pro, and in Levy's guide. Apollo's support article for its own scooters says the opposite for its checks: "plug the charger into the wall first, then the scooter." I can't say which is better, so follow your own manual.
5. **Check that it started.** The charger light changes or the scooter's display shows charging. The next section covers what to look for.
6. **Stay nearby.** The CPSC says "always be present when charging micromobility products, never while sleeping." Levy says to check in periodically.
7. **Unplug when it is full.** Levy's order is outlet first, then the scooter. Xiaomi says to unplug the cable when charged and close the rubber plug over the port.
8. **Store the charger loosely coiled.** Levy warns that wrapping the cord tightly can damage it over time.

If you want to know how long step 5 to step 7 will take, see my guide to [how long an electric scooter takes to charge](/how-long-does-it-take-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery/).

## Can I charge my electric scooter at home?

Yes. Can I charge my electric scooter at home is one of the most common questions, along with how to charge electric scooter battery packs at home without a risk, and every source I read says a normal household outlet is fine. Gyroor says a standard 110 V or 220 V outlet is generally enough and that most scooters draw about 100 to 300 W while charging; that is a retailer's general figure, not a measured one. It suggests an electrician check homes with older wiring first.

Where and how matters more than whether:

- **Skip extension cords and power strips.** Gyroor warns they can overheat. If you want protection from surges, Gyroor suggests a surge protector.
- **Charge somewhere with a smoke alarm.** The London Fire Brigade says to fit smoke alarms where scooters are charged and to test them regularly.
- **Don't block the exit.** Its advice is never to store or charge on an escape route such as a hallway.
- **Let a hot battery cool.** The London Fire Brigade says to let the battery cool before charging, and Levy suggests waiting 30 to 60 minutes after a ride. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) says lithium-ion charging works best from about 5 to 45 °C.
- **Don't charge a freezing scooter.** Wikipedia says many lithium-ion cells cannot be charged safely below 0 °C, so bring it indoors to warm up first.

## How to know if your electric scooter is charging

There is no single signal, so check two or three of these. People ask how do i know if my e scooter is charging, how do i know if my electric scooter is charging, and how do i know if my scooter is charging, and the answer is the same for all three. This is how do you know if your electric scooter is charging in practice, in the order I would check.

1. **The charger light.** Plug in and watch it change. Xiaomi, Levy and Ride Review all describe red while charging and green when finished, with the caveats below.
2. **The scooter's display or battery meter.** Levy says a charging symbol or a rising percentage may appear. Ride Review's unattributed FAQ answer on GoTrax says the scooter's battery meter flashes while charging and stays solid when full. It doesn't cite GoTrax documentation, so I treat it as a hint only.
3. **The app.** Apollo's support article tells owners to check the battery level in its app's Battery tab. If your scooter has an app, a percentage that climbs between two checks is the clearest sign.
4. **A faint hum and mild warmth.** Levy lists a faint hum or buzz and slight warmth near the battery as signs. Excess heat is a warning, not a sign of good charging; stop and unplug.
5. **Time.** If the battery percentage hasn't moved after a few hours, it isn't charging. Typical full charge times are in my [charge time guide](/how-long-does-it-take-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery/).

So how to know if my electric scooter is charging when I can't see a light, or how to know if your scooter is charging when the lead is hidden under a cover? Use the display or app. And how to know if electric scooter is charging when the scooter has no display at all? The charger light is the main signal, plus a before-and-after ride check: if the scooter's range is back, it charged.

## What the charger lights mean

Here is what each named source says about how do i know if my scooter is charging from the lights, side by side, and how to know if your scooter is charging from them alone. These are not a standard, and none of them cover every model. Where a source is general, I say so.

| Source | Light | Stated meaning | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| Xiaomi support (Electric Scooter 4 Pro) | Red to green | "When the scooter is fully charged, the LED indicator on the charger will change from red to green." | Maker, one model |
| Levy | Solid red or orange | Charger connected and battery accepting charge | Retailer, generic |
| Levy | Slow or regular blinking red | Charging at a lower rate | Retailer, generic |
| Levy | Fast blinking red | Possible problem: loose connection, damaged charger, overheating or battery fault | Retailer, generic |
| Levy | Solid green | Battery full, safe to unplug; sometimes charging is held by a fault | Retailer, generic |
| Levy | Alternating red and green | Electrical fault in the pack; do not charge, seek service | Retailer, generic |
| Levy | No light | Charger not powered, faulty, or battery voltage too low | Retailer, generic |
| Ride Review (GoTrax) | Charger red / green | Charging / fully charged | Unattributed FAQ |
| GreenMoov | Green | Many chargers go green at 90 to 100 percent; solid green may be standby on one model and a fault on another | Guide, generic |
| Apollo support | Green | "Fully charged or not charging"; may stay green above 90 percent | Maker, Apollo scooters |

Notice that green has two meanings in two of these sources. Levy says a solid green light can also mean charging is held because of a battery, charger or connection issue. That is why a green light alone doesn't prove the battery is full. I haven't verified any of these light codes on a real scooter, and Levy's table is a general description, not any specific model's code.

## How to know when your electric scooter is charged

Knowing when to unplug is the same question from the other end. If you are asking how do i know if my electric scooter is charging properly before you walk away, check the display or app, not only the light. How do I know when my electric scooter is charged? Use these in order:

- **Display or app shows 100 percent.** This is the most reliable, if your scooter has one.
- **The charger light changes.** Red to green on Xiaomi's charger, per its support page. Apollo says its charger can stay green above 90 percent, so a green light there doesn't mean 100.
- **The usual time has passed.** If your model's full charge is about six hours and it has been plugged in overnight, it is done. Many chargers stop on their own, and Levy notes modern scooters have overcharge protection, but it still advises unplugging once it is full. Wikipedia says many packs have protective circuitry that cuts off outside the safe voltage range.

Don't leave it connected for convenience. The London Fire Brigade says to unplug the charger once it has finished. If your scooter has a charge-limit setting, it can stop at 80 percent; Apollo's support site says its scooters currently don't offer one, so check yours. ## If your scooter isn't charging: a safe checklist

If you can't tell whether it is charging, or the light isn't doing what the table says, work through this order. It combines the Apollo and GreenMoov troubleshooting guides and Levy's light guide. None of it needs tools until the last step.

1. **Check the display.** If the scooter says it is full, a green light is expected. GreenMoov suggests riding briefly and trying again.
2. **Check the outlet.** Try another socket, or test it with a lamp. Confirm the plug is fully seated at the wall and the scooter.
3. **Wiggle the plug gently.** Apollo says if the red light flickers when you move the plug, the port may be loose or worn.
4. **Look at the port.** Use a flashlight for dust, corrosion, bent pins or melted plastic. Apollo says not to charge if you see damage. Never put metal tools in it.
5. **Let a hot or cold battery settle.** Levy says to wait at least 30 to 60 minutes after a ride before charging.
6. **Note the light pattern.** Fast blinking red or alternating red and green, in Levy's table, means stop charging and ask the maker.
7. **For skilled owners, measure the charger.** With a multimeter, compare the charger's open-circuit output to its label. GreenMoov says about 42 V is typical for a 36 V system and 54.6 V for 48 V. Apollo says an output of 0 V means the charger needs replacing.

Stop and contact the maker if the battery case is swollen, dented or hot, if you smell burning or see melted plastic, or if you hear sparks or popping, as GreenMoov lists. Don't jump-start a lithium pack or bypass its battery management system. Contact Apollo's or your maker's support with the model, charger rating and photos of the port, as Apollo advises.

## Charging safety: what the fire services and CPSC say

Safety deserves its own section because this is the part with real consequences. The CPSC's "Micromobility Battery Charging Safety" page (September 4, 2025) covers e-scooters, e-bikes and hoverboards. It says to always be present when charging, never charge while sleeping, use only the supplied charger, use only an approved replacement battery pack, follow the maker's charging instructions and unplug when done. It also warns against batteries altered by unqualified people or built from reused cells, and says never to throw lithium batteries in the trash. Read it on the [CPSC page](https://www.cpsc.gov/node/65775).

The London Fire Brigade gives similar advice in an [incident report from July 2025](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/incidents/2025/july/e-scooter-fire-acton/): cool the battery first, use the correct charger bought from a reputable seller, check that the battery and charger meet UK safety standards, unplug when finished and, in its words, "we would advise not to leave it unattended or while people are asleep." It also says to watch for a battery that is hot or has changed shape.

Those pages are not about any one brand, and neither says charging is unsafe. They say charging is where problems show up, so charge where you can watch and hear, with the right charger. ## Frequently asked questions

### How do you charge an electric scooter battery?
Connect the maker's charger to the scooter's charge port, plug it into a wall outlet, and unplug it when the charger light or display shows it is full. Follow your manual for the plug-in order.

### How to charge a scooter battery at home?
For how to charge electric scooter battery at home, use the supplied charger on a normal outlet, in a dry, ventilated spot with a smoke alarm, away from exits, and stay nearby. Avoid extension cords.

### How to charge electric scooter battery at home safely?
Charge only with the supplied charger, let a hot battery cool first, don't charge while asleep, and unplug when done, as the CPSC and London Fire Brigade advise.

### Can I charge my electric scooter at home?
Yes. A standard household outlet is generally enough, according to Gyroor. Avoid extension cords and power strips, and don't charge on an escape route.

### Can I charge my electric scooter at home overnight?
Fire services advise against leaving it charging while people sleep. The CPSC says never to charge while sleeping.

### How do I know if my e scooter is charging?
Check the charger light, the scooter's display or app, and whether the percentage rises. A red light usually means charging, but check your manual.

### How do I know if my electric scooter is charging?
Look for a charging symbol or rising percentage on the display or app, a changed light on the charger, and a faint hum or mild warmth.

### How do I know if my scooter is charging when there is no light?
Levy lists no light as a charger, outlet or battery problem. Test another outlet, check the plug and port, and if it still shows nothing, contact the maker.

### How do I know when my electric scooter is charged?
The display or app shows 100 percent, or the charger light changes, such as red to green on Xiaomi's charger. Apollo's charger can stay green above 90 percent.

### How do you know if your electric scooter is charging when the charger is green?
A green light can mean full, or not charging. Check the display or app. Apollo says green can mean fully charged or not charging.

### How to know if my electric scooter is charging when it won't turn on?
Look at the charger light and whether the scooter or charger warms slightly. Apollo has a support article on scooters that won't turn on while charging; check your maker's.

### How to know if your electric scooter is charging properly?
A steady rise in battery percentage and a charger light matching your manual's description are the best signs. Fast blinking red or alternating colours point to a fault, per Levy.

### How to know if your scooter is charging in cold weather?
Be careful. Wikipedia says many lithium-ion cells cannot be charged safely below 0 °C. Warm the scooter indoors first.

### How to know if electric scooter is charging when the display is off?
Rely on the charger light and, if you have one, the app. If neither shows anything, try another outlet and inspect the port.

## The bottom line

To sum up how to charge an electric scooter battery at home: use the maker's charger, charge somewhere dry with a smoke alarm, stay nearby, and unplug when full. To tell it is charging, and to answer how do i know if my e scooter is charging at all, use two signals, the charger light plus the display or app, and don't trust a green light alone, because green can mean full or not charging. When the lights don't match your manual, stop and contact the maker before opening anything.
