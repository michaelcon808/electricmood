# Best Lightweight and Foldable Electric Scooters 2027

The best lightweight foldable electric scooters 2027 comes down to one trade: every pound you cut costs you battery, power or hill-climbing. The NIU KQi Air is the best balance I found. At 26.4 lb, ERideHero tested it at 20.1 mph and 24.2 miles of range, which is the best range ERideHero has measured on a scooter under 40 lb. If you want a lightweight scooter that still climbs and brakes, the Apollo Go is the one, at 46 lb with 26.7 mph and 19.8 miles. If you want the lightest electric scooters 2027 on a budget, the GoTrax XR Ultra weighs 26.4 lb but only managed about 14 to 16 miles in the tests I found.

Those are my picks for the best lightweight electric scooters 2027, and I chose them for tested range, braking and folded size, not for weight alone. Weight is easy to put on a spec sheet, and it is also the number least often checked: EScooterNerds' "lightest electric scooters" list says "One-Hand Carrying" in its title but describes no carry test, and it lists maker weights with no weighing. I haven't ridden or carried any of these. My picks come from ERideHero's tested numbers, EScooterNerds' range tests, and maker pages, and I say which is which.

## How light is lightweight? Weight classes that matter

"Lightweight" has no standard. If you want the best lightweight portable electric scooters 2027, these are the bands I use, and they're my judgment, not a rule.

- **Under 30 lb:** you can lift it with one hand for a short carry and take it on stairs. The KQi Air, XR Ultra, E-TWOW GT, GoTrax GXL V2 and Levy scooter sit here, at about 26 to 26.5 lb each.
- **30 to 41 lb:** carryable for a flight of stairs, but you'll feel it. The KQi 100F (38.8 lb) and Segway E3 Pro (40.1 lb) are here.
- **40 to 50 lb:** a short carry only. The Apollo Go (46 lb) is here, and it is the heaviest scooter in this guide that I'd still call portable.
- **Over 50 lb:** not lightweight, whatever the marketing says.

Weight does more than limit stairs. It sets the battery size, and the battery sets the range. I show that trade below with a number of my own, watt-hours per pound.

## How I chose these lightweight scooters and what I trust

1. **Tested range and speed first.** ERideHero tests range in regular, fast and eco riding with a 175 lb rider and a GPS logger. EScooterNerds also tests range for several light scooters, and I use those as separate figures.
2. **Weight and folded size.** These are mostly maker specs. ERideHero's list doesn't label weight as tested or claimed, and EScooterNerds says it didn't run weight tests on the scooters in its lightest-scooter list.
3. **Braking and hills.** Light scooters often brake and climb worse, so I give those numbers where they exist.
4. **Tires and build.** Tubeless tires and a decent IP rating are the nearest thing to a durability signal I could find, and I say below how thin that evidence is.

I rely on ERideHero and EScooterNerds because they publish numbers. A third lightest-scooters article, on Ride Review (August 31, 2026), runs about 750 words, cites no tests and puts the 54 lb Segway Ninebot Max G3 and a 77 lb EMOVE in a "lightweight" list, so I did not use it for picks. Levy's lightweight page lists its own scooters with maker figures, and I treat its numbers as claims.

## Lightest electric scooters 2027: the weight-versus-range trade

Here are the numbers that show what weight buys you. Watt-hours per pound is battery capacity divided by scooter weight, and watt-hours per mile is battery divided by tested range. Both are my own math from the figures above.

| Scooter | Weight | Battery | Wh per lb | Tested range | Wh per mile |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E-TWOW GT | 26.5 lb | 504 Wh | 19.0 | About 30 mi | 16.8 |
| NIU KQi Air | 26.4 lb | 451.2 Wh | 17.1 | 24.2 mi | 18.6 |
| Apollo Go | 46 lb | 540 Wh | 11.7 | 19.8 mi | 27.3 |
| Segway E3 Pro | 40.1 lb | 368 Wh | 9.2 | 17.2 mi | 21.4 |
| GoTrax XR Ultra | 26.4 lb | 187 Wh | 7.1 | About 14 to 16 mi | About 12 to 13 |
| NIU KQi 100F | 38.8 lb | 243 Wh | 6.3 | 11.4 mi | 21.3 |

The pattern is clear. The KQi Air and E-TWOW GT put 17 to 19 Wh into every pound, so they go far for their weight. The 100F and XR Ultra carry about a third of that, so they feel light but run out quickly. The low Wh per mile on the XR Ultra comes from its low speed and short test, not from efficiency, so don't read it as a good sign.

Also notice that the Apollo Go uses far more energy per mile than the ultralights. That is the price of 26.7 mph and hill-climbing, and it is why it weighs 46 lb.

## Best foldable electric scooters for travel 2027: cars, trains and planes

Folding makes a scooter easier to put in a car boot or carry onto a train. Flying is harder, and I'd not assume you can.

The U.S. FAA's [lithium battery page](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/lithium-batteries) lists limits for spare batteries: up to 100 Wh without special approval, and 101 to 160 Wh with airline approval, with spare batteries in carry-on only. It says nothing about e-scooters, hoverboards or batteries installed in devices, so it gives no answer on whether you can fly with a scooter. What I can say is that the batteries in my sources are 187 Wh (XR Ultra), 243 Wh (100F), 368 Wh (E3 Pro), 451 Wh (KQi Air), 504 Wh (E-TWOW GT) and 540 Wh (Apollo Go). All are above 160 Wh, so none of them fits inside the numbers that page gives. Check the airline's own rules before you plan to fly with one.

For trains, buses and trams, the rules are set by each operator, and I haven't summarized them. Ask the transit agency whether folded scooters are allowed and at what times. For the car, weight and folded length are the numbers that count: the E-TWOW GT at about 42 inches and the KQi Air at about 45 inches are the shortest folds here.

## Best lightweight durable electric scooters 2027: what the evidence says

I'd like to tell you which lightweight scooter is the most durable, but I can't. I found no long-term durability test of any scooter here. What exists are proxies:

- **Tires.** Tubeless tires cut flats. The KQi Air and Apollo Go have self-healing tubeless tires, the E3 Pro has tubeless, and the KQi 100F and XR Ultra have tubed tires.
- **Water resistance.** IP55 (KQi Air), IP66 (Apollo Go), IPX5 (E3 Pro and 100F) and IP54 (XR Ultra). See my [waterproof guide](/best-waterproof-electric-scooters-for-daily-commute-in-rain-deals/) for what the codes mean.
- **Rider limit.** 265 lb on the E3 Pro, 100F and Apollo Go, 220 lb on the XR Ultra. See the [weight limit guide](/how-much-weight-can-an-electric-scooter-hold/).
- **Brakes.** The XR Ultra has a rear disc only, which may wear faster. My [brake guide](/how-to-fix-adjust-and-change-brake-pads-on-an-electric-scooter/) covers adjustment and pad changes.

EScooterNerds noted a third-party report of a battery malfunction in rain on the GoTrax GXL V2, against no problem in its own light rain test. That is one anecdote, but it shows why I'd read owner reports and the warranty terms before buying. The most durable-looking option on paper is the Apollo Go, because of its IP66 rating, self-healing tires and 265 lb limit, but that is my reading and not a test.

## Safety and rules

A light scooter still has a large battery. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death). It asked manufacturers to certify to UL standards, including UL 2272. I couldn't verify certification for these models, so check the box or the maker's page.

Light scooters have small wheels and shorter wheelbases, which makes them more sensitive to potholes and cracks. Wear a helmet, and keep your speed down on rough pavement. I haven't summarized state or city rules on where scooters may ride, because they vary and I couldn't find reliable official summaries.
