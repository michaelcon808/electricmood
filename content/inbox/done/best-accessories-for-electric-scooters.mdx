# Best Accessories for Electric Scooters 2027

The best accessories for electric scooters 2027 starts with a helmet, then a lock if you park outside, then lights, then a phone mount and a flat-tire kit. For the lock, OutdoorGearLab's best overall was the Litelok X1 ($259) and its best value the Kryptonite KryptoLok ($80). For lights, BikeRadar's best overall front light was the Knog Blinder 1300 and its best rear the Lezyne Strip Drive Pro 400+. For a phone mount, OutdoorGearLab's top pick was the Peak Design Universal Bar Mount ($50), with the $15 Lisen as its best buy. For the helmet, I name no model, and I explain why below.

Here is the catch with every accessory guide, including this one. The testers I found tested locks, lights and mounts on bikes, not scooters, and I found no independent test of scooter-specific accessories. So treat these as well-sourced shortlists for scooter riders, check fit on your own scooter, and I name no model for helmets, pumps, chargers, brake pads or tubes because I found no test.

I haven't used any of these products. Everything comes from OutdoorGearLab, Tom's Guide, Consumer Reports, BikeRadar, ERideHero and Rider Guide, plus the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, the Bicycle Helmet Safety Institute and a Rutgers study. I label each number as tested or listed. Prices are what those sources showed, in dollars or pounds, not live prices. Checked October 10, 2026.

## How I chose these accessories and what the tests cover

The sources measure different things, so here is what each covers:

- **OutdoorGearLab (locks):** testers attack locks with angle grinders, bolt cutters, cable cutters, hacksaws and wire snips and record how long each takes to defeat. Overall scores weight security 40%, ease of transport 25%, ease of use 20% and ease of parking 15%.
- **Tom's Guide (locks):** updated February 12, 2025. It lists Sold Secure and ART star ratings for some locks only, and notes that any bike lock can be cut with the right tools and enough time.
- **Consumer Reports (locks):** tested 24 locks against brute force, hacksaws, reciprocating saws, grinders and bolt cutters, and rated them only on resisting being broken open. I could not read which locks it rated best.
- **BikeRadar (lights):** published September 28, 2026. It says it has tested more than 500 lights over 17 years in real dark-riding conditions. Many runtimes are manufacturer claims, and some lights fell short.
- **OutdoorGearLab (phone mounts):** updated November 17, 2025, scored on ease of reaching the phone (30%), security on bumpy terrain (30%), setup (25%) and phone protection (15%).
- **ERideHero and Rider Guide (scooter accessories):** scooter-specific lists with named picks, but neither documents any testing. I use them for what scooter riders buy and why, and label their picks as recommendations.

None of the lock, light or mount tests used a scooter. To be on my list, an accessory needed a named hands-on test and a price I could source, with one exception: the sealant, which has only a recommendation, and I say so.

## Helmet and protective gear

**Why a helmet comes first.** A Rutgers study of U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission data on [motorized scooter head injuries](https://www.rutgers.edu/news/motorized-scooter-head-injuries-rise-rutgers-study-finds), published June 11, 2019, estimated annual head and facial injuries rose from about 2,325 in 2008 to about 6,947 in 2017. Of injured riders with a recorded helmet status, 66% were not wearing one. The article says many state helmet laws do not address adults, and my [scooter laws guide](/how-old-do-you-have-to-be-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-age-limit/) covers your state's rules. The study covers motorized scooters in general, not only electric ones.

**What kind.** The [Bicycle Helmet Safety Institute](https://helmets.org/electric-mobility-helmets/) (page last modified August 15, 2026) says there is no U.S. government or other standard specifically for electric scooters, and quotes the CPSC: "CPSC believes that a bike helmet provides sufficient protection for electric scooter riding." It adds that scooters are less stable than bicycles and that most injuries come from falls, commonly from catching the small front wheel in a pavement irregularity and going over the handlebars. In one Southern California study it cites, only 4.4% of injured riders wore a helmet.

**How to pick one.** The [Insurance Institute for Highway Safety](https://www.iihs.org/news/detail/for-5-star-head-protection-cyclists-have-dozens-of-options) reported in November 2021 on Virginia Tech's ratings of 139 bike helmets: 73 earned 5 stars, 37 earned 4, 24 earned 3 and 5 earned 2. It explains that CPSC tests only require impact accelerations below 300 g, a level linked to skull fracture or severe brain injury, and don't address concussion-level forces, and advises choosing a 4 or 5 star helmet while weighing cost, fit and comfort: "any helmet is better than none." The [Virginia Tech ratings list](https://helmet.beam.vt.edu/bicycle-helmet-ratings.html) has an Urban filter, though I could not read the current list myself, so I name no model. Those ratings cover bicycle helmets, not scooters.

**Faster scooters.** ERideHero's author advises a standard bicycle helmet for scooters under 20 mph on smooth pavement, a downhill helmet for more protection, and a full-face e-bike or motorcycle helmet for faster riding or risky areas. That is one reviewer's advice, and BHSI doesn't address full-face helmets. If your scooter is fast, see my [fast and high-performance scooter guide](/fastest-high-performance-premium-dual-motor-full-suspension-electric-scooters-top-speed/) and think about more coverage.

**Gloves, eyewear and a vest.** ERideHero's author recommends flexible, lightweight gloves (thicker ones for faster scooters) and eye protection with yellow lenses for cloudy days and darker tints for sun. Rider Guide's author lists a safety vest, gloves and eye protection among the ten accessories, with typical prices of $15, $25 and $15 or more. Those are asserted prices and views, not tests.

## Best scooter locks

The locks trade security against weight, and Consumer Reports found U-locks gave the best security of the types it tested, while lightweight cables and chains are easier to defeat. Check the lock's opening against your scooter's stem and a rack before you buy: Tom's Guide says the Ottolock Sidekick Compact is too short for thick frames, which is the same problem a scooter stem can cause. For where and how to lock, see my [how to lock up an electric scooter](/how-to-lock-up-an-electric-scooter-to-a-bike-rack/) guide.

### Litelok X1: best overall

OutdoorGearLab's best overall and Editors' Choice, $259, overall score 70, security 9.5. It resisted an angle grinder for 395 seconds, weighs 3.7 lb, and comes with no frame mount, so you carry it or buy a mount. For a scooter that is easy to carry, I'd put this at the top if the price fits.

Testers found the keyway can get sticky and the frame mount clunky. A bonus cable is useful for a front wheel, but a cable is the weaker part.

## Best lights for electric scooters

Most scooters have a built-in headlight and a small rear light, but a brighter add-on helps on unlit paths and makes you easier to see. My [scooter settings guide](/how-to-turn-on-and-connect-bluetooth-on-an-electric-scooter/) covers turning your built-in lights on and off.

**How bright?** BikeRadar's lumen advice for commuting: about 200 lumens for a front light is enough to be seen on lit roads, about 300 lights some of the road ahead, at least 600 is needed on unlit roads, and over 1,000 is recommended for rough terrain or high speeds. For rear lights it suggests 20 to 100 lumens, with brighter ones for daytime. It says beam pattern matters as much as raw brightness. ERideHero's author suggests 300 to 600 lumens for most scooter riders and 800 for unlit paths. Those match well, and I'd lean higher if your scooter goes over 15 mph. That last part is my opinion.

**Rules.** BikeRadar's summary for the U.S. is that a white front light should be visible from at least 500 feet and a red rear reflector from at least 600 feet, with side reflectors in some states, and that rules vary by state. Scooter rules can differ from bicycle rules, so check your state.

## Flat-tire kit: sealant, valve extender and pump

Flats are the usual roadside problem. My [fix a flat tire on an electric scooter](/how-to-fix-a-flat-tire-on-an-electric-scooter/) guide covers the repair, and [removing and replacing a scooter wheel](/how-to-take-off-and-put-on-an-electric-scooter-back-wheel/) covers the harder cases. For a kit, three things come up in the scooter lists.

**Sealant.** ERideHero's author recommends Slime 10004 Tube Repair Sealant as inexpensive, effective at sealing flats, lasting two years, non-toxic and easy to carry. That is a recommendation, not a test, and no price was listed. Rider Guide's author calls an unnamed sealant "our favorite" and gives a typical price of $10. Limits: Gotrax says sealant works best on small punctures, a quarter inch or smaller, and calls it a temporary fix, and Varla says it won't help with a blowout. I found no source on aerosol flat fixers for scooters, so I don't recommend them.

[Check the current price of Slime 10004](AFFILIATE_LINK_SLIME_10004_SEALANT)

**Valve extender.** Rider Guide's author lists a tire valve extender at a typical $5 as an accessory, with no named product, and Apollo says a bent valve stem may need an angled adapter on a bike pump hose. Hard-to-reach valves are common on small scooter wheels, but I found no test of either product.

**Pump.** I found no tested scooter pump. My [best electric bike pumps](/best-electric-bike-pumps/) guide covers tested floor, mini and electric pumps, but the testers used bike tires, so check your scooter's valve and pressure before you rely on any of them.

**Patch kit and tube.** Levy and Apollo list a replacement inner tube or patch kit, tire levers and the hex keys your manual calls for. I name no product because I found no test.

## Brakes, parts and tools

Scooter-specific brake pads, tires and chargers are made for particular models, so I name no brand for these and the sources I read tested none. Buy replacement pads and tires matched to your scooter's model from the maker or a seller who lists your model. My [electric scooter brakes guide](/how-to-fix-adjust-and-change-brake-pads-on-an-electric-scooter/) says what tools you need: hex keys, a Phillips or flathead screwdriver and sometimes a wrench set, plus isopropyl alcohol (70% or higher) or brake cleaner for dirty pads and rotors, never oil-based products.

**Chargers.** Use the charger that came with your scooter. I found no independent test of replacement scooter chargers, so I list none. See [how long a scooter takes to charge](/how-long-does-it-take-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery/) and [how to charge it and tell if it's charging](/how-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery-at-home-and-know-if-it-is-charging/).

**Seat.** If you want to sit, see my [scooter seat guide](/how-to-put-a-seat-on-and-ride-an-electric-scooter/) before buying anything, because it depends on your model.

## Smaller add-ons and what I would skip

ERideHero's author also recommends a bell with a clear, moderately loud tone, checking the handlebar diameter first, and Rider Guide lists a safety vest. They are cheap, and neither source tested one. I'd add a bell if you share paths with walkers.

What I would skip: very cheap no-name lights or locks with no test, because I found no data on them; and a cable lock as your only lock, since Consumer Reports found cables and chains easier to defeat. Those are my own judgments.

## Frequently asked questions

### What are the best accessories for electric scooters 2027?
A 4 or 5 star helmet first, then a lock, front and rear lights, a phone mount and a flat-tire kit. See the table above for the tested picks.

### What is the best lock for an electric scooter?
OutdoorGearLab's best overall U-lock was the Litelok X1 ($259) and its best value the Kryptonite KryptoLok ($80). None were tested on a scooter.

### Do I need a helmet for an electric scooter?
The CPSC, quoted by the Bicycle Helmet Safety Institute, believes a bike helmet provides sufficient protection for scooter riding. Check your state's law too.

### How many lumens do I need for a scooter light?
BikeRadar suggests about 200 lumens to be seen on lit roads, about 300 to light the way, and 600 or more on unlit roads.

### Can I use a bike phone mount on a scooter?
Probably, if the bars have room. The mounts were tested on bikes. Measure your handlebar and display clearance first.

### Does tire sealant work on a scooter?
Gotrax says it works on small punctures of a quarter inch or less and is a temporary fix. Varla says it won't help with a blowout.

### What charger should I buy?
The one that came with your scooter. I found no independent test of replacement chargers.

### Are these products tested on scooters?
No. The lock, light and mount tests used bikes, and I found no independent scooter accessory test.
