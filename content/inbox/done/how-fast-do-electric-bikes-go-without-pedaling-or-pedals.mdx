# Do E-Bikes Need Pedaling?

Do e-bikes need pedaling? It depends on the bike. A pedal-assist e-bike only helps while you turn the cranks, and a throttle e-bike (Class 2 in the U.S.) can move without pedaling. How fast do electric bikes go without pedaling? Under U.S. rules the motor-only speed is capped below 20 mph, and the faster 28 mph class is pedal assist only, so you can't ride a legal e bike 28 mph without pedals. And why do electric bikes have pedals at all? In the U.S. and U.K. definitions, an e-bike has to have working pedals, and a bike without them is treated as a moped or motorcycle.

I haven't ridden or tested any bike in these sources. The explanations come from a U.S. federal statute, NCSL's e-bike primer, the U.K. government's e-bike rules page, Wikipedia, and four sellers (Ride1Up, Leoguar, Heybike and Levy). Seller figures on range and speed conflict, and I show where. Laws change by state and country, so check yours.

## Pedal assist vs throttle: do e-bikes need pedaling?

**Pedal assist (PAS).** Levy says the motor "doesn't activate unless you're turning the cranks," with typically 3 to 5 assist levels (often 0 to 5, or Eco, Tour, Sport, Turbo). A cadence sensor detects whether you're pedaling, using a magnet on the crank; a torque sensor measures how hard you pedal and scales the help to match. Levy says torque sensors feel more like regular cycling, typically offer 20 to 40% more range than cadence systems and cost $200 to $500 more; Heybike (a seller) says torque sensors give smoother power and better range, and cadence sensors are cheaper. Those are sellers' figures, not tests.

**Throttle.** The bike runs without pedaling, controlled by a twist or thumb throttle. Ride1Up compares it to a scooter and says you "don't necessarily have to" pedal on a bike with one; Levy compares it to a motorcycle. Heybike says it is easier on the legs and helps you pull away from stops.

Do e-bikes need pedaling to go uphill? Ride1Up says you will still need to pedal on inclines, though "it won't take too much effort" with the motor, and that pedaling is "likely necessary for higher speeds and steeper inclines." It also advises keeping your feet on the pedals "at all times" for safety and stability, and when turning, braking or stopping.

## How fast do electric bikes go without pedaling?

How fast do electric bikes go without pedaling? In the U.S., the federal definition of a low-speed electric bicycle caps motor-only speed below 20 mph ([15 U.S.C. 2085](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCODE-2011-title15/html/USCODE-2011-title15-chap47-sec2085.htm)). Under the three-class system that [NCSL describes](https://www.ncsl.org/transportation/state-electric-bicycle-laws-a-legislative-primer), Class 2 is the throttle class and its motor assist stops at 20 mph; Class 1 and Class 3 give help only while you pedal. Ride1Up says you can reach up to 20 mph on the throttle alone. Leoguar says throttle models reach about 20 mph under U.S. rules.

Heybike says "most states in America limit the top speed of Class 2 and Class 3 electric bikes to 21 mph for the throttle mode," which is a slightly different figure than the 20 mph in the statute and class definitions, and it doesn't say which states. I show both and don't resolve it.

Elsewhere, per [Wikipedia's e-bike laws page](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle_laws): Canada's federal limit is 500 W and 32 km/h (20 mph) on motor power alone, and British Columbia requires the motor to disengage when pedaling stops, the throttle is released or a brake is applied. The EU definition is a pedal-assist bike with 250 W or less that cuts off before 25 km/h. The page gives no throttle rule for the EU or U.K. and mentions a 6 km/h "walk function" only in a Danish section and the Australian pedelec rules, uncited. In the U.K., the [government's rules page](https://www.gov.uk/electric-bike-rules) says a bike can be propelled up to 15.5 mph without pedaling only if it has been approved, with "twist and go" as the example. For more on country limits, see [how fast can an e-bike go](/how-fast-can-an-electric-bike-or-ebike-go/).

## Can you ride an e bike 28 mph without pedals?

Can you ride an e bike 28 mph without pedals? Not on a legal e-bike in the U.S. The 28 mph class, Class 3, gives help only while you pedal, per Leoguar and NCSL, and the federal definition caps motor-only speed below 20 mph. Ride1Up says laws "limit the assisted speed of ebikes to up to twenty-eight MPH," and that riders can go faster when pedaling hard or on declines.

A bike that does more than that on a throttle alone is, in the sources, outside the e-bike definitions. ## Why do electric bikes have pedals?

Why do electric bikes have pedals? Four reasons from the sources:

1. **The law.** The federal definition requires "fully operable pedals." The [U.K. government's page](https://www.gov.uk/electric-bike-rules) says an electrically assisted pedal cycle must have pedals that can be used to propel it, and that a bike with no pedals that can propel it is classed as a motorcycle or moped, needing registration, tax, insurance, a licence and a helmet.
2. **Choice and exercise.** Leoguar says "Almost all e-bikes have pedals, giving you the choice to exercise if you want," and that pedals let you switch between pedaling and throttle whenever you like.
3. **Range.** Pedal assist saves battery, covered below.
4. **A dead battery.** With no power you can still pedal, but the bike is heavier; see [can you ride an e-bike without the battery](/can-you-ride-an-electric-bike-without-the-battery-manually/). Levy estimates unpowered riding feels 30 to 50% harder, an estimate rather than a measurement.

Why do electric bikes have pedals if the throttle works? Because the law treats the pedals as part of what makes it a bicycle.

## Range: throttle vs pedal assist

Every seller says throttle use drains the battery faster, but the numbers differ:

- **Levy:** throttle-only range typically 15 to 25 miles versus 40 to 80 miles with pedal assist.
- **Heybike:** 30 to 45 miles on pure electric versus 45 to 65 with pedal assist on its own models, and "the throttle will drain your battery faster."
- **Leoguar:** the battery might run out "15-20% faster" with heavy throttle use, and tests show about 25% longer battery life with pedal assist, with no source given for the tests.
- **Ride1Up:** 20 to 35 miles without pedaling, depending on battery, terrain and assist level.

These are not comparable: different bikes, batteries and riders. The direction is the same, and the size isn't settled. For charging, see [how to charge an e-bike](/how-to-charge-an-electric-bike-battery-time-cost-and-how-often/).

Which to choose? My view: pedal assist if you want range and exercise, a throttle if you want help starting from stops or have limited ability to pedal, and a bike with both if your state allows throttles. Heybike says combined-mode bikes cost more on average than single-mode ones, which is its own observation.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do e-bikes need pedaling?
Pedal-assist e-bikes do: the motor helps only while you pedal. Throttle (Class 2) e-bikes can move without pedaling, with motor help stopping at 20 mph.

### How fast do electric bikes go without pedaling?
Up to about 20 mph on a throttle under U.S. rules. Heybike says 21 mph for throttle mode in most states; the statute says under 20 mph.

### Can you ride an e bike 28 mph without pedals?
Not on a legal U.S. e-bike. The 28 mph class is pedal assist only.

### Why do electric bikes have pedals?
The law requires working pedals, and pedals let you save battery, exercise or ride with a dead battery.

### Can an e-bike go without pedals at all?
A bike with no pedals that can propel it is a moped or motorcycle in the U.K. (government rules page).

### Is a throttle illegal?
Some places don't allow throttle-only riding, per Leoguar; the U.K. requires approval for twist-and-go bikes. Check your state or country.

### Does pedal assist work if I stop pedaling?
No. Levy says the motor doesn't activate unless you're turning the cranks.

### Which uses more battery, throttle or pedal assist?
Throttle, per every seller I read, though they disagree on how much.

## The bottom line

Do e-bikes need pedaling: pedal-assist ones do, throttle ones don't, and the law still requires working pedals. How fast do electric bikes go without pedaling: up to about 20 mph under U.S. rules, and 15.5 mph in the U.K. only for approved bikes.
