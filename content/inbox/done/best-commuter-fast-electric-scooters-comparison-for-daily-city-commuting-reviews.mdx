# Best Electric Scooters for Commuting 2027

The best electric scooters for commuting 2027 for most people is the Segway Ninebot Max G3. ERideHero measured 28 mph, 24.9 miles of real range, a 3.5 hour charge and a 10.8 ft braking distance, and it has hydraulic suspension, 11 inch self-sealing tires and an IPX6 rating. If you want to spend less, the Navee ST3 Pro is ERideHero's value commuter, though it takes 10 hours to charge. If you carry your scooter up stairs or onto a train, the NIU KQi Air is the light one at 26.4 lb.

Those are my picks for the best commuter electric scooters 2027, and I chose them for the numbers that matter on a commute: tested range, braking, how long they take to charge, and how heavy they are to carry. Top speed matters far less than most lists suggest, because most city commutes never get near it. I haven't ridden these scooters. Every figure here comes from published tests by ERideHero and Electric Scooter Guide, plus a few first-impression reports and maker pages, and I say which is which.

One warning up front. Claimed range is the number most likely to mislead you. ERideHero says claimed range is often 40 to 60 percent higher than real-world range, and Electric Scooter Guide measured 21.2 miles on a scooter whose maker claims up to 56. So I rank by tested range.

## How I chose these commuter scooters and what I trust

A commuter scooter is a tool you use twice a day, so I weighed the things that fail on a commute.

1. **Tested range, not claimed range.** The tested range is what you get from a rider on real roads. ERideHero measures regular, fast and eco riding separately, which is more honest than one number.
2. **Braking.** ERideHero's braking distances run from 10.7 ft to 12.4 ft for the scooters here, and Electric Scooter Guide measured 13 to 14 ft from about 16 mph on the Max G3 Plus. Shorter is better, but the test speeds differ, so I don't rank on this.
3. **Charge time.** If you charge at work or overnight, this matters less. If you have a tight turnaround, it matters a lot, and I give it its own section.
4. **Weight and fold.** The 26 lb to 57 lb spread decides whether you can take the scooter upstairs.
5. **Weather and build.** IP ratings and tire type tell you whether a rainy commute is realistic.

How the sources differ: ERideHero uses a 175 lb rider and a GPS logger on a controlled course. In its Max G3 Plus review, Electric Scooter Guide used a heavier rider, about 200 lb, on a hilly, stop-and-go course, so that range number is lower. Levy's commuting page lists five scooters with maker figures and no tests, and it says non-Levy specs "change often." I treat that page and any "up to" range as claims. Where the sources disagree on price or rating, I show both.

## Best electric scooters for commuting 2027: my picks

### Segway Ninebot Max G3: best all-round commuter

ERideHero tested the Max G3 at 28 mph (matching Segway's claim), 24.9 miles of regular-riding range (Segway claims 50), 19.7 miles riding fast and 28.7 miles in eco mode. It does 0 to 20 mph in 6.5 seconds, climbed ERideHero's test hill at 16.2 mph, and stopped in 10.8 ft. The battery is 597 Wh and it charges in 3.5 hours. It has 11 inch tubeless self-healing tires, adjustable hydraulic suspension front and rear, IPX6 water resistance, a 286 lb rider limit and a weight of 54.2 lb.

The price depends on the day. ERideHero's metadata shows "from $999.99," Electric Scooter Guide's list shows about $1,399, and RiderGuide's March 2025 first-impressions article reported an $899 pre-order price rising to $1,399. RiderGuide's article was first impressions only, with no range test, so I used it for charging details only. The G3 is the one I'd buy for a daily commute of up to about 10 miles each way, because the tested range leaves a margin.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Max G3 | 597 Wh | 24.9 mi | 24.0 | 10.8 ft | IPX6 |
| ST3 Pro | 596.7 Wh | 24.9 mi | 24.0 | 11.5 ft | IPX5 |

The Air stands out for efficiency, which makes sense for a light scooter at a lower top speed. That is a different test, not a worse scooter.

## Best electric scooters for daily commuting 2027: how far you really need

For the best electric scooters for daily commuting 2027, range is the number to plan around, and a simple way is to start with your round trip. If you can't charge at work, you need the full round trip from a single charge. If you can, you need only one way plus a margin.

Here is a rule I'd use, and it is my own advice and not a tested standard: pick a scooter whose tested regular-riding range is at least 1.5 times your round trip if you can't charge at work. Tested range assumes a 175 lb rider on a flat, controlled course, so a heavier rider, hills, stops or fast riding will take miles off. Electric Scooter Guide's G3 Plus test, with a 200 lb rider, hills and Sport mode, got 21.2 miles, which shows how much the test conditions move the result. Expect your own number to vary.

On the tested numbers here, a 5 mile each way commute (10 miles round trip) is comfortable on any of them. A 10 mile each way commute with no charging at work is about the limit for the 24 to 27 mile scooters, and the Vmax VX2 Hub 18.2 Ah (35.6 miles) is the one with margin. If you commute a lot farther, see my [long-range scooters](/best-affordable-top-long-range-electric-scooters-for-adults-longest-range-reviews/).

How long the trip takes is a separate question. See my guide on [how long it takes to scooter a mile](/how-fast-is-a-scooter-compared-to-walking-and-how-long-per-mile/).

## Fast charging electric scooters for commuting to work 2027

If you want fast charging electric scooters for commuting to work 2027, the honest answer is that charge time is rarely tested and often claimed. Here is what I found.

| Scooter | Charge time | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Segway Max G3 | 3.5 h | ERideHero page |
| Segway Max G3 (two chargers) | About 2.5 h | Estimate in RiderGuide's first impressions, not tested |
| Segway Max G3 Plus | About 4 h | ESG review, approximate |
| NIU KQi Air | 5 h | ERideHero page |
| NIU KQi 300X | 6 h | ERideHero page |
| Navee ST3 Pro | 10 h | ERideHero comparison page |

RiderGuide says the Max G3 ships with two chargers and supports dual charging, which it estimates at about 2.5 hours, but its article was first impressions with no tests, and I haven't seen that time measured. ScooterRank's charge-time ranking lists several models, including the Levy scooter, the Acer Predator ES Storm Pro and the Vmax VX5 Pro LT, at 2.5 hours, but the page doesn't say whether those figures are tested or claimed, or when they were collected.

A useful way to compare is average charging power, which is battery watt-hours divided by charge hours. That is my own rough math, since real chargers run at a higher power early and taper off. It works out to about 171 W for the Max G3 (597 Wh over 3.5 h), 101 W for the KQi 300X, 90 W for the KQi Air and 60 W for the ST3 Pro. So the ST3 Pro charges at about a third of the speed of the G3. If you need to top up between shifts, the G3 is the quickest I found with a test behind it. ## Best electric scooters for city use 2027: what matters in traffic

City riding is stop-and-go, with potholes, wet paint and cars, so some features matter more than top speed.

- **Braking.** Tested stopping distances range from 10.7 ft (KQi 300X) to 12.4 ft (KQi Air) at ERideHero. A shorter stop gives you more margin when a car door opens.
- **Lights and signals.** Electric Scooter Guide measured the G3 Plus headlight at 170 lux at 6 ft and notes front and rear turn signals. ERideHero's page praises the KQi 300X's headlight visibility. Check for a bright headlight, a brake light and turn signals if you ride at dusk.
- **Tires.** ERideHero lists tubeless tires on the Max G3, ST3 Pro, KQi 300X, KQi Air and VX2 Hub, and self-healing tires on the Max G3 and KQi Air. ESG lists the G3 Plus as self-sealing. That cuts the chance of a flat on a commute. If you do get one, my [flat tire guide](/how-to-fix-a-flat-tire-on-an-electric-scooter/) helps.
- **Suspension.** Hydraulic suspension (Max G3, KQi 300X front) takes the edge off broken pavement. ESG notes the G3 Plus is harsh on gravel and loose surfaces, so it is an on-road scooter.
- **Weather.** The IPX6 Max G3 and VX2 Hub are the best-rated here for rain. See my [waterproof commuter guide](/best-waterproof-electric-scooters-for-daily-commute/) for more.

A top speed above about 20 mph is something you'll rarely use between traffic lights, and ERideHero's tested scooters here range from 20.1 to 33.5 mph. Levy's commuting page says most city commutes are capped at 15 to 20 mph, though it gives no source for that.

## Best electric scooters for adult commuting 2027: weight, size and rider limits

For the best electric scooters for adult commuting 2027, check the rider limit and the deck as well as the speed. The Max G3 is rated for 286 lb and the VX2 Hub 13 Ah for 287 lb, according to ERideHero. ERideHero says the KQi 300X has a spacious deck for riders of all sizes. I didn't find a rider limit in my sources for the ST3 Pro, KQi 300X or KQi Air, so check the maker's page. See my [weight limit guide](/how-much-weight-can-an-electric-scooter-hold/) to see why the limit matters.

Weight is the other adult question. In order, from lightest: KQi Air 26.4 lb, KQi 300X 48.7 lb, Max G3 54.2 lb, VX2 Hub 13 Ah 54.9 lb, ST3 Pro 55 lb, Max G3 Plus 55.1 lb, VX2 Hub 18.2 Ah 57.1 lb. Electric Scooter Guide lists the Max G3 Plus as "heavy for daily stair carries or train transfers," and at 54 to 57 lb that goes for the whole group except the Air and, to a degree, the 300X. The KQi 300X folds to 46.2 by 21.3 by 21.3 inches and the Air to 44.9 by 21.3 by 18.9 inches. If you use stairs or trains every day, the Air is the choice, and otherwise a heavier scooter with a bigger battery is easier to live with.

## Locking it up and keeping it safe

A scooter left outside is a theft target, so plan how you'll lock it. Electric Scooter Guide notes the G3 Plus has Apple Find My, proximity unlock and a built-in alarm. A review of a different scooter on The Gadgeteer makes the point that digital locking and tracking don't replace a physical lock or secure storage, and I agree. ERideHero's KQi 300X page mentions no security features beyond its folding lock. See my guide to [locking up an electric scooter](/how-to-lock-up-an-electric-scooter-to-a-bike-rack/), and if you can, bring the scooter inside.

## Safety, rules and gear

Wear a helmet every ride and check the brakes and tires before you leave. A commuter scooter has a big battery, and the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death). It asked manufacturers to certify to UL standards, including UL 2272. I couldn't verify certification for the scooters here, so check the box or the maker's page, and charge on a hard surface, not on a bed or a couch.

I haven't summarized state or city rules on where you can ride, speed caps, helmets or age limits, because they differ widely and I couldn't find reliable official summaries. Check your local rules before you commute, especially for sidewalks and bike lanes. My guide on [age limits and rules](/how-old-do-you-have-to-be-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-age-limit/) is a start.
