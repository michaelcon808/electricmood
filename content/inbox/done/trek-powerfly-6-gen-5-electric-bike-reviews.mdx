# Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 Review 2027: Specs and Tests

The Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 is a mid-priced aluminium hardtail electric mountain bike with Bosch's newest Performance Line CX motor and a large 800 Wh battery, and its main appeal is power and range for the money. The catch is that I found no independent hands-on test of this exact bike. This page collects trek powerfly+ 6 gen 5 2027 electric bike reviews and evidence in one place: spec sheets, tests of the Gen 5 motor, a test of the full-suspension Powerfly+ FS 8 that shares its platform, and one owner thread. I mark each one clearly.

What the evidence supports: the motor is quieter and smoother than the one it replaces, the battery is among the largest on a bike at this price, and the bike is heavy at roughly 24 kg (about 53.8 lb). What I can't tell you is how this specific hardtail climbs, corners or holds up, because no one I could find tested it.

I haven't ridden this bike. Sources are Bosch Gen 5 motor tests by Flow Mountain Bike and E-Mountainbike Magazine, a BikeX test of the FS 8, an Electric Bike Review forum thread, Trek's own page, retailers, 99spokes and Bike Magazin's spec listing. Prices are what those sources listed, not live prices. Checked October 10, 2026.

## What Gen 5 changes: Bosch Performance Line CX and the new battery

Trek's page for the hardtails compares the legacy Powerfly with the Gen 5 Powerfly+. Per Trek, the Gen 5 has a newer Performance Line CX motor, a battery of up to 800 Wh (up from 625 Wh), the RIB 2.0 battery system and singletrack geometry, with 120 mm of fork travel (100 mm on size S). Trek does not give US prices or weights on that page.

What independent testers say about the Gen 5 motor itself:

- **Quieter.** Flow reports that the gearbox "clacking" on descents is gone and the motor is slightly quieter on climbs. It rated the noise similar to the Specialized 3.1 and slightly louder than the Avinox M1. E-Mountainbike called the lower noise the main highlight and credited a decoupled drivetrain.
- **A little lighter.** About 2.8 kg, around 100 g less than Gen 4, with a magnesium casing (Flow, Bosch via Wheelbase).
- **Same headline numbers at launch.** 85 Nm and 600 W, the same as Gen 4. Flow says a mid-2025 software update raised the limits to 100 Nm and 750 W, applied through Bosch's Flow app in about 15 minutes.
- **Feel.** Flow: smooth, predictable, punchy power, with smoother response at low pedal inputs. E-Mountainbike: familiar to the previous generation, more sensitive in eMTB mode on technical climbs, but it can lose momentum on steep climbs in a heavy gear and shift frequently in a light one. Throttle response wasn't as quick as competitors, per Flow.
- **Sensors.** Torque and cadence sensors sample 1,000 times per second, and an inertial sensor reads gradient and vibration.

Flow's list of the eight preset modes (Eco, Tour, Tour+, Auto, Sport, eMTB, eMTB+, Turbo) matches what you'd see on a Trek. The battery meter in the app was found to be reasonably accurate.

## Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 motor power: 85 Nm, 100 Nm and what owners say

The 85 Nm motor is not a weak one, though it isn't the strongest on the market either. Three things to know:

1. **The 100 Nm update.** Flow says the Gen 5's torque and power can be raised to 100 Nm and 750 W through the Flow app. BikeX's FS 8 test bike ran the standard 85 Nm, and BikeX said the app can raise torque to 100 Nm and peak output to 750 W, with strong, consistent and finely adjustable power. I couldn't confirm whether every Powerfly+ 6 qualifies, so ask the dealer to check the bike's firmware.
2. **A shop listing disagrees.** A German shop listing for the Powerfly+ 4 800 Wh advertised 120 Nm for the 2026 model while the 2027 listing says 85 Nm, with no explanation. I treat that as a listing inconsistency and rely on Bosch's figures.
3. **Tour+ feels soft.** In an Electric Bike Review forum thread from January 2026, an owner said his wife wasn't impressed with the power in Tour+, finding it sluggish next to her Specialized Vado 3 and his Giant Quick E, while eMTB and Turbo felt strong and the bike climbed very steep terrain. Other posters explained that Tour+ mostly works in Eco and ramps up on steep climbs, and suggested switching modes or customising in Flow. That is anecdotal and posters were not certain.

The practical read: set up the modes in the app on day one. If you're a heavier rider on steep trails, the extra headroom in the app matters.

## Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 battery and range: what is known

The battery is a Bosch PowerTube 800 Wh. Bosch's Gen 5 batteries are light for their size: Flow lists the 800 Wh pack at 3.9 kg, lighter than the old 750 Wh at 4.4 kg, and the 600 Wh at 3.1 kg. A 250 Wh PowerMore range extender adds 1.5 kg and takes the bottle-cage mount, per Flow, for 1,050 Wh in total. E-Mountainbike and the Spanish outlet Híbridos y Eléctricos both describe the extender as an option for the Powerfly+ line.

I found no measured range for the Powerfly+ 6. What exists is motor-test context from other bikes:

- Flow got about 1,900 m of climbing (roughly 2.4 m per Wh) on a typical trail ride with a Cube Stereo Hybrid One77 and 800 Wh battery.
- Flow's standardised tarmac test on a Santa Cruz Vala with a 600 Wh battery gave 1,767 m, run before the software update.
- BikeX gave no measured range for the FS 8, which has the same 800 Wh battery.

Different bikes, riders and loops, so I don't average them or claim them for the Trek. Treat range as unproven until someone tests the Powerfly+ 6.

## How the Powerfly+ 6 should ride: what the FS 8 test tells us

The closest independent evidence on how a Gen 5 Powerfly+ rides is BikeX's test of the full-suspension FS 8 (August 2025), which scored "sehr gut" (very good):

- **Climbing:** excellent, with a sporty seating position, long chainstays and good traction.
- **Descending:** confident and well balanced, though not flashy, with sensitive suspension on rough trails.
- **Handling:** stable and secure, with a high front end (647 mm stack) and a low bottom bracket (335 mm).
- **Cons:** high weight (24.6 kg), basic components for the price and an exposed display.

That is a full-suspension bike at €6,499 with a 130/120 mm setup, so it doesn't tell you how a 120 mm hardtail will ride. A hardtail will be firmer on rough ground and, as a simple fact, has no rear suspension to help traction on roots and rocks. Trek's page says the Gen 5 hardtails have singletrack suspension and geometry, which is its own claim, not a test.

On fit, the bike comes in S to XL. Size S uses 27.5-inch wheels and a 100 mm fork and dropper, while the other sizes use 29-inch wheels, a 120 mm fork and a 170 mm dropper. The XS size has been dropped, per Híbridos y Eléctricos. If you're under about 5 ft 1 in, check that the S fits.

## Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 weight and components

At 53.8 lb (99spokes) or 24 kg (Bike Magazin), the Powerfly+ 6 is heavy for a hardtail, which likely comes from the large battery and aluminium frame, though no one has broken the weight down. The FS 8 test bike weighed 24.6 kg. Nobody I found weighed the 6 itself.

Component notes, all from listings:

- **Fork:** the RockShox Recon Silver RL is an entry-level air fork. BikeX called the components basic for the price even on the pricier FS 8, so expect basics here too.
- **Drivetrain:** Deore shifters, an XT derailleur and a 10-51 cassette. It's a decent mix for the price.
- **Brakes:** four-piston Shimano, with 203 mm rotors, which should be good for a heavy bike.
- **Dropper:** 170 mm on sizes M to XL, which is good, and just 100 mm on size S.
- **Tyres:** MTB Monster lists Bontrager Gunnison Pro XR. I'd check the casing, since heavy e-bikes are hard on tyres.
- **Range extender and display:** the Purion 200 is a simple controller. Many riders add a Kiox display or a phone mount.

These notes are my reading of spec sheets, not test findings.

## Who should buy the Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5, and who should look elsewhere

**It suits you if:**

- You want a large 800 Wh battery and a modern Bosch motor at a mid-range price.
- You're happy with a hardtail for cross-country trails, fire roads and bike paths.
- You'll set up the motor modes in the Flow app.
- You prefer a big dealer network. Flow cites Bosch's wide dealer network and reliability as a plus,.

**Look elsewhere if:**

- You need proof of how this exact bike rides. No independent test exists yet.
- You want a light bike. For a lighter, trail-bike feel, see my [Specialized Turbo Levo SL 2 Comp](/specialized-turbo-levo-sl-2-comp-electric-bike/) review, which has the opposite trade-off: light, but with a small battery and a modest motor.
- You ride rough, technical trails where full suspension helps. Look at the FS Powerfly+ models.
- You want a US price before you commit. Ask a Trek dealer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the Trek Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 worth it?
It looks like good value on paper: a Bosch CX Gen 5 motor and 800 Wh battery at about €3,800 to €4,000. But I found no independent test of this exact bike, so confirm the ride at a dealer.

### What motor does the Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 have?
Bosch Performance Line CX Gen 5, rated 85 Nm and 600 W peak at launch. Flow reports a mid-2025 update lets it reach 100 Nm and 750 W through the Flow app.

### How big is the battery?
A Bosch PowerTube 800 Wh. An optional 250 Wh PowerMore extender gives 1,050 Wh.

### How much does the Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 weigh?
99spokes lists 53.8 lb and Bike Magazin 24 kg. Neither is a measured weight.

### How much does it cost?
Listings range from €3,799 to €3,999 in Europe and £2,955 to £3,600 in the UK. I found no US price.

### What is the difference between Powerfly+ 4, 6 and 8?
The 4 has a 600 Wh battery, while the 6 and 8 have 800 Wh. The 8 is the €4,999 flagship hardtail with a better fork, per E-Mountainbike.

### Is the Gen 5 motor better than Gen 4?
Testers say it is quieter, slightly lighter and smoother, with the same launch torque and power.

### Is the Powerfly+ 6 Class 1?
Listings say assist cuts out at 20 mph (32 km/h) in the US and 25 km/h in Europe. Trek's class labelling wasn't on the pages I read, so ask a dealer.

### Does the Powerfly+ 6 have a throttle?
None of the listings I read mention a throttle.

### Is there a 2027 Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5?
A German shop lists a 2027 Powerfly+ 6 Gen 5 at €3,799 with the same 800 Wh battery and 85 Nm motor. I found no maker announcement of changes.

### Is it good for hunting?
I haven't tested it for that. For rules on e-bikes on public land, see my guide on electric bikes for hunting.

## The bottom line

For trek powerfly+ 6 gen 5 2027 electric bike reviews, the honest summary is this: the motor and battery are modern and well regarded, the price is reasonable, and the bike is heavy and untested. If you want an affordable electric hardtail with plenty of battery, it is worth a test ride at a Trek dealer. If you need evidence of how this exact bike climbs, corners and lasts, wait for an independent test.

Prices, specs and firmware update options change, so confirm the exact version, battery and assist limit on the day you buy. Since I haven't ridden this bike, treat my notes as a sourced summary of other people's tests and listings.
