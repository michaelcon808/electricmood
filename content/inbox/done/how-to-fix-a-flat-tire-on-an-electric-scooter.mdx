# How to Fix a Flat Tire on an Electric Scooter

How to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter, on a standard air-filled tire with an inner tube, comes down to this: power the scooter off, take the wheel off, let the rest of the air out, lever the tire off the rim, find the hole in the tube, then patch or replace the tube, put everything back without pinching the tube, inflate to the pressure printed on the tire and test ride. That is the sequence Levy and Apollo both lay out.

I haven't ridden or repaired any of these scooters myself. The steps below come from Levy (a scooter rental and sales company), Apollo, Varla, Gotrax and Gyroor (makers) and Rider Guide (a review site). None of them gives you a torque value or a PSI number for your scooter, so your manual and the tire sidewall decide those. Taking the wheel off a hub-motor scooter involves motor and brake cables, and if you are not comfortable with that, a shop is the sensible route. ## What tire do you have?

The fix depends on the tire, so check first. The sources describe three kinds:

- **Pneumatic with an inner tube.** The common type. Levy says a flat on its pneumatic models means replacing only the inner tube, and that a bike shop or you at home can do it.
- **Tubeless.** Apollo describes these as mounted directly to the rim with sealant and air, no inner tube, and says they are harder to mount without professional equipment such as an air compressor.
- **Solid.** Apollo and Gyroor say solid tires can't go flat. Apollo says changing them is often best left to the manufacturer, and Levy recommends an experienced mechanic for converting to solid because it needs proper tools and heating methods.

Apollo also says its City models use self-healing gel tires that patch punctures automatically. That is a maker claim for its own tires. If you aren't sure what you have, look at the sidewall markings and the manual, or ask the seller.

## How to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter: steps

How to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter with an inner tube, combining Levy's and Apollo's steps:

**Tools (Levy and Apollo):** tire lever, a replacement inner tube or patch kit, a pump, and screwdrivers, wrenches or hex keys sized to your manual. Apollo says a bent valve stem may need an angled adapter on a bike pump hose.

1. **Pick a safe flat spot.** Apollo says to use a surface that supports the scooter deck. Levy says to use a stand or lean it against a wall.
2. **Power the scooter off.** Levy also says to remove the battery if possible. I wouldn't open the battery housing or touch the pack; if your scooter has a simple removable battery, follow the manual.
3. **Take the wheel off.** Apollo says to disconnect the motor cable and any brake cables first, and warns: "NEVER start pulling on cables to disconnect them unless you know what you're doing." Keep the hardware in order. My [wheel removal guide](/how-to-take-off-and-put-on-an-electric-scooter-back-wheel/) covers this step in more detail.
4. **Let all the air out.**
5. **Lever the tire off the rim.** Work the lever around, then finish by hand. Apollo says hot water and soap around the bead can loosen a stubborn tire.
6. **Find the hole.** Apollo says to submerge the tube and watch for bubbles, then clean the area. Also check inside the tire for the thorn or glass, or it will puncture again.
7. **Patch or replace.** Levy says you can patch a punctured tube with a kit or replace it entirely, and to consider replacing the tire if it is damaged.
8. **Refit.** Seat the tube and tire without pinching the tube, which Apollo says can cause pinch flats, or "snakebite" punctures.
9. **Inflate slowly** to the pressure printed on the tire sidewall, which Levy says is usually shown there.
10. **Refit the wheel,** reconnect the cables, tighten the fasteners, spin the wheel to check it sits right, and take a short test ride (Levy and Apollo).

None of the guides gives torque values or a time estimate. If a brake disc or rear motor cable is involved and you aren't sure, a shop is better than guessing; see my [brake pads guide](/how-to-fix-adjust-and-change-brake-pads-on-an-electric-scooter/) for the brake side.

## Can you ride an electric scooter with a flat tire?

Can you ride an electric scooter with a flat tire? Most of the guides don't address it. The one that does is Apollo's support page, which says riding on flat or low-pressure tires removes the cushioning, so an impact from a pothole or obstacle can bend or crack the rim. It also says that damage can break the bead seal and cause air loss that looks like a puncture.

Levy's tire-types page adds that riding on low PSI makes tires more likely to puncture or leak around the sidewall. Neither source gives a safe distance for riding on a flat. My own view, labelled as opinion: don't ride it. A bent rim turns a cheap tube fix into a wheel replacement, and a squirming flat tire is also a handling risk. Walk it, or carry or roll it to a safe spot.

## Can you use Fix a Flat on a scooter tire?

Can you use Fix a Flat on a scooter tire? I found no source that covers aerosol flat fixers for scooters, so I can't say whether they work or are safe on scooter rims and valves. Don't treat that as a yes.

What the sources do cover is tire sealant, a different product that is added through the valve:

- **Gotrax** says sealant works best on "smaller punctures and cuts in the tire tube that are 1/4" or smaller," and calls it a temporary fix that may need reapplying or a new tube.
- **Varla** says it works on small holes and "will not help with larger punctures, like a proper blowout."
- **Rider Guide's** author says it won't stop blowouts. That article has no test data; its views are the author's own judgment.

If you want to try a repair product, read your scooter maker's guidance first, and treat anything that gets you home as temporary.

## Why does my electric scooter tire keep going flat?

If you keep asking why does my electric scooter tire keep going flat, the sources give a short list of causes:

- **Punctures.** Gyroor lists nails, glass and thorns, and Levy calls punctures the most common reason. My own tip, as opinion: run a finger carefully along the inside of the tire after each puncture, because anything left in will puncture the new tube.
- **Low pressure.** Gotrax says under-inflated tires are much easier to puncture, and Apollo says low pressure makes pinch punctures more likely.
- **Worn tires.** Gyroor says lost tread makes tires more prone to punctures and blowouts, and Apollo says to replace tires when the tread flattens in places. It also says rear tires wear faster.
- **Overinflation.** Gyroor says too much air can make the tire rigid and prone to bursting.
- **A faulty valve.** Levy says a faulty valve can cause slow leaks.
- **Overweight loads.** Levy says exceeding the scooter's capacity can make tires deflate faster.
- **A damaged rim.** Apollo's rim page says repeated flats with no visible puncture, or bubbles at the bead when you spray soapy water, can point to a bent rim rather than the tire.

For the rim case, Apollo says minor bends can go to a professional shop for straightening, and damaged rims should be replaced. Prevention options Rider Guide's author rates are correct pressure, sealant, liners and solid tires, with liners only working on tires with tubes. The author's views, not test results.

## How to put a tubeless tire on an electric scooter

How to put a tubeless tire on an electric scooter, per iScooter's step list:

1. Gather the tire levers, pump or compressor, soapy water and gloves.
2. Remove the wheel and old tire carefully so the rim and brake parts aren't damaged.
3. Apply soapy water around the bead of the new tire.
4. Work the tire onto the rim gradually and evenly.
5. Inflate in a quick burst to seat the bead, keeping hands clear because it can pop into place with force.
6. Check for leaks, and never exceed the pressure printed on the sidewall.

iScooter does not say whether your rim has to be tubeless-compatible, so I don't know. It warns that incorrectly applied rim tape can stop a good seal. Apollo's warning about needing an air compressor also applies, which is why many people leave this to a shop. If you are changing from a tube to tubeless, ask the maker if your wheel supports it before buying anything.

## Can I put bigger tires on my scooter?

Can I put bigger tires on my scooter? None of the sources I read covers upsizing. iScooter says to confirm compatibility with the manufacturer's manual or support team before fitting a different size, and I agree. A bigger tire could rub the fork, frame or brakes, and iScooter says to check after installation that the tire doesn't rub the brakes or frame.

What I can say is what the sources say about tire types. Varla notes that off-road tires are less prone to flats. Levy says solid tires can be retrofitted on existing 8.5 and 10 inch wheels, but only with an experienced mechanic. ## Preventing the next flat

A short checklist built from the sources:

- Check pressure regularly. Apollo says at least monthly, Levy says at least every other week if you ride often, and Apollo's rim page says before every ride. Those are different intervals, so pick the stricter one.
- Use a pump with a gauge (Gotrax).
- Inspect tires for wear, cracks and embedded objects (Gyroor).
- Avoid debris, potholes and curbs, especially when the tire is low (Apollo, Gyroor).
- Replace worn tires and tubes sooner rather than later (Varla).
- Carry a repair kit with patches, glue and a portable pump (Gyroor).

## Frequently asked questions

### How to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter?
Power off, remove the wheel, deflate fully, lever the tire off, patch or replace the inner tube, refit without pinching the tube, inflate to the sidewall pressure and test ride.

### Can you ride an electric scooter with a flat tire?
Apollo says it removes cushioning and can bend or crack the rim. I'd walk it.

### Can you use Fix a Flat on a scooter tire?
I found no source covering aerosol flat fixers on scooters. Tire sealant added through the valve is a different product, and it is a temporary fix for small holes.

### Why does my electric scooter tire keep going flat?
Sources point to debris left in the tire, low pressure, worn tires, a faulty valve, overweight loads or a damaged rim.

### How to put a tubeless tire on an electric scooter?
Use tire levers, soapy water on the bead, then a quick burst of air to seat it. Confirm your rim supports tubeless first.

### Can I put bigger tires on my scooter?
No source covers it. Confirm with the manufacturer, since a bigger tire may rub the brakes or frame.

### Should I patch or replace the tube?
Levy says either works for a puncture. Replace the tire if it is damaged, and Gyroor says large tears may need a new tube.

### What PSI should I use?
The pressure printed on the tire sidewall. The guides I read gave no universal number.

### Can a solid tire go flat?
No, but Gotrax and Rider Guide's author note less traction and cushioning.

### Does sealant stop blowouts?
No. Varla and Rider Guide say it won't help with blowouts or larger punctures.

### When should I go to a shop?
If you are unsure about the motor cable or brake cables, or have sidewall damage, a bent rim or a tubeless tire.

### Do tire liners work?
Rider Guide's author says they can eliminate flats, which is a claim, not a test result. They work only with inner tubes.

## The bottom line

How to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter with a tube is a job most people can do with a lever, a tube and a pump, as long as the wheel comes off without trouble. Don't ride on a flat, check the inside of the tire before refitting, and treat sealant as temporary. Hub-motor cables, brakes, tubeless tires and bent rims are the points where a shop is the safer choice.
