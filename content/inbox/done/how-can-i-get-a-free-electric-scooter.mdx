# How to Get a Free Electric Scooter

How can I get a free electric scooter? Honestly, it is rare. The realistic routes are winning a brand or creator giveaway, qualifying for a city or state voucher that covers most or all of the price, earning one through work such as charging shared scooters, or getting one from a brand as a tester or creator. Most programs I found are discounts, not free scooters, and a lot of the "free scooter" offers online are scams. Levy, a scooter seller whose guide I used, says truly free scooters are rare and that a brand-new one delivered at zero cost is rare.

This page walks through each route, says what I could verify and what I could not, and ends with the red flags. I haven't entered any giveaway, claimed any rebate or tested any program. Rebate amounts and eligibility change often, so check each program's own page before you count on a figure.

## How can I get a free electric scooter? The realistic routes

Here are the routes, with how realistic each looks. The realism column is my judgment, labelled as such, based on what the sources say.

| Route | What you get | My view on realism |
|---|---|---|
| Giveaways and contests | A scooter if you win | Possible, but a lottery |
| City or state vouchers | A discount, sometimes most of the price | Real, but limited vouchers and mostly e-bikes in what I checked |
| Free or discounted rides | Rides, no ownership | Real, but not a scooter you own |
| Working for a scooter company | Pay per scooter charged | Real, but it is work |
| Tester or creator programs | A scooter or code from a brand | Needs an audience |
| Employer or campus perks | Credits or cash | Depends on your employer |
| Trade-ins, refurbished, sales | A cheaper scooter | Very realistic, but not free |
| Mobility scooter programs | A mobility scooter, not a kick scooter | Only with a medical need |

Levy's guide puts it this way: free outcomes generally mean winning a contest, qualifying for a rebate that covers the full price, or building an audience for brand products, while low-cost outcomes are "absolutely realistic." Levy sells scooters and promotes its own services in that article, so read its list as a starting point.

## How to get an electric scooter for free through giveaways

How to get an electric scooter for free through a giveaway is simple in principle and slow in practice. Levy names brands that have run giveaways (NIU, RoadRunner, HOVSCO, Unagi, Segway with an annual contest, and Gotrax monthly) and two sweepstakes listing sites, and suggests following brands, using daily entries and entering creator giveaways, which often have fewer entrants. The review site Ride Review says it often gives scooters away and that free ones may be hard to get. I have not verified any of these and I do not know current terms.

If you are wondering how can I get a free electric scooter on social media, the FTC has a firm rule for you: **never pay to claim a prize.** Its prize and grant scam page says a prize letter that asks for a fee for taxes, reporting or handling is a scam, that a win in a contest you never entered is likely a scam, and that it is illegal to require payment to enter a sweepstakes or to claim buying something improves your odds. It adds that fake social media giveaways are hard to tell from real ones, and that one in four people who reported losing money to fraud since 2021 said it started on social media. The page is [on the FTC site](https://consumer.ftc.gov/all-scams/prize-grant-scams).

## City and state rebates and vouchers

These are not free scooters, but a voucher can cover most of the price. One I could check against the city's own page: San Antonio's low-income e-bike rebate program, funded by a U.S. Department of Energy grant, issues $1,000 vouchers by lottery, and the city says most participants pay only sales tax because e-bike prices start near $1,000. The first round issued 244 vouchers and the next offered 181. It covers e-bikes only, and the page does not list eligibility, so check the program site. The city's release is [here](https://www.sa.gov/Directory/News-Releases/City-of-San-Antonio-Continues-Low-Income-E-Bike-Rebate-Program).

Levy lists more programs, which I have not checked against the programs' own pages:

| Where | What Levy says |
|---|---|
| Austin, TX | Up to $600 off for Austin Energy customers, up to $1,300 for low-income customers; apply within 60 days of purchase |
| Denver, CO | $675 income-qualified voucher, can stack with the $225 state credit, combined savings capped at 80% of the price |
| Hawaii | 20% off, up to $500, for eligible participants |
| Connecticut | $500 standard, up to $1,250 income-qualified, currently paused |
| Vermont | Up to $400 by household income, some tiers paused |
| California | E-bike incentive project ended December 2025 |

Two warnings. Several of these are e-bike programs, so check that a kick scooter qualifies before you buy. And amounts, pauses and limits change, so treat Levy's list as dated.

## Free rides are not a free scooter

Levy lists equity programs from shared-scooter operators: Lime Access with discounted rides (free in Denver, limited to three per day), Bird community pricing at 50 percent off for groups such as veterans, teachers, seniors and Pell Grant recipients, Spin Access with rides as low as $0 for 30 minutes in some cities, and mandated discounts for low-income riders in Portland and Los Angeles. Levy also lists referral credits. These give you rides, not ownership. They are a good way to try a scooter before buying one.

## Earn a scooter by working

Levy says shared-scooter charging gigs pay $5 to $12 per scooter charged overnight and that 50 to 75 charges could fund a budget scooter, with Lime Juicer needing age 18 or over and U.S. work authorization. By my arithmetic, 50 to 75 charges at $5 to $12 is between $250 and $900, which is my calculation, not Levy's. It also mentions Bird fleet manager roles with much higher pay but a need for warehouse space and transport. I have not verified current pay, and it is work, not a free scooter.

## Tester, creator and employer routes

Levy lists brand programs that may send a scooter or code to creators (it mentions Eahora, OKAI, VORO Motors and Unagi) and says micro-influencers with 5,000 to 50,000 followers are often in demand, and that beta testers for unreleased models sometimes keep the device. I do not run a product-testing program and I cannot confirm any of these terms.

On the employer side, Levy cites a $300 Boston University benefit, a University of Maryland cash bonus for giving up a parking pass and Uber for Business credits. Ask your employer or campus about transport perks.

## Cheaper, if not free

Levy also covers the routes that make a scooter cheaper without being free: trade-in credit from some brands, refurbished and open-box units, zero-interest financing and timing a purchase around sales such as Black Friday. It notes that its own subscription plan costs more than buying over time. ## Mobility scooters are a different product

Some "free electric scooter" searches are really about mobility scooters for people with disabilities or reduced mobility. Levy says the VA Prosthetics and Sensory Aids Service provides free mobility scooters to eligible veterans with documented medical need, that Medicare Part B covers 80 percent of a medically necessary mobility scooter after the deductible with a prescription and exam (home use), and that Medicaid typically covers them with little or no cost-sharing, varying by state. Those are Levy's summaries, not official statements, so confirm eligibility with the VA, Medicare or your state Medicaid office directly. Scam ads for a "free government mobility scooter" often target seniors and ask for fees or personal information, per Levy.

## Scam red flags

Levy warns about a pyramid scheme called Lightning Shared Scooter Company (it says Minnesota's Attorney General warned about it in July 2025), $10 to $39 scooter ads on social media that use stolen photos, and risky Facebook Marketplace deals paid by Zelle, Venmo, Cash App or wire transfer. Its red flags include prices that are too low, required recruitment or investment, no verifiable company details, crypto or wire payment, urgency, upfront fees for "free" items and a social-media-only presence. It suggests paying by credit card, running a reverse image search and checking URL spellings.

If you want one rule, use the FTC's: if you have to pay to get the free thing, it is not free. Report suspected fraud at reportfraud.ftc.gov.

## Frequently asked questions

### How can I get a free electric scooter?
Realistically by winning a giveaway, qualifying for a voucher, earning one through work or getting one as a tester or creator. Truly free is rare, per Levy.

### How can I get a free electric scooter as a student?
I found no student-specific free scooter program. Levy mentions campus shared-scooter partnerships and some employer or university perks.

### How can I get a free electric scooter from the government?
Most programs I found are discounts on e-bikes, such as San Antonio's $1,000 voucher lottery. Check eligibility on the program site.

### How to get an electric scooter for free as a veteran?
Levy says the VA provides free mobility scooters to eligible veterans with documented medical need. That is a mobility scooter, not a kick scooter. Confirm with the VA.

### How to get an electric scooter for free by testing or reviewing?
Some brands send scooters to creators or beta testers, per Levy, but terms vary and I could not verify them.

### How to get an electric scooter for free on social media?
Be careful. The FTC says fake giveaways are hard to spot and never to pay to claim a prize.

### Is a $10 electric scooter ad real?
Levy says $10 to $39 scooter ads on social media often use stolen photos and end with nothing arriving, a knockoff or stolen data.

### Can I earn an electric scooter?
Levy says charging shared scooters pays $5 to $12 each and that 50 to 75 charges could fund a budget scooter. It is work and I have not verified current pay.

### Do free ride programs give you a scooter?
No. They give discounted or free rides, not ownership.

### What is cheaper than free?
Nothing, but refurbished units, trade-ins and sales are the realistic low-cost route.

## The bottom line

How can I get a free electric scooter? Enter giveaways without ever paying to claim a prize, check city and state voucher programs for your area (and whether kick scooters qualify), consider working or creator routes if they suit you, and treat any offer that asks for money or looks too good as a scam.
