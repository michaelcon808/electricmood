# Razor Electric Scooter Questions: Speed, Range, Battery and Tires

How fast does an electric razor scooter go? It depends on the model. Razor lists the E100 at up to 10 mph and the E300 and E300S at up to 15 mph. The E300's 10 miles of range is Razor's "up to" figure: Razor also lists up to 40 minutes of continuous use, and one independent tester measured about 7 miles. Battery life has two parts. A charge is rated for up to 40 minutes of riding, and the sealed lead-acid batteries on these scooters are described by sellers as lasting a couple of hundred charge cycles or about two years before capacity drops noticeably. To inflate the tires you need a pump with a Schrader valve, and the pressure figure depends on whom you ask, which I cover below.

This page answers the questions people actually search about Razor electric scooters: speed, range, how long the scooter and its battery last, charging, tires, and who each model fits. I haven't ridden or tested any Razor scooter. The specs come from Razor's own product pages for the E100, E300 and E300S, the tested numbers from EScooterNerds, and the rest from Levy (a scooter seller), Two Wheeling Tots and Scooter Guide. Where they disagree I show both.

## How fast does an electric razor scooter go?

How fast does an electric razor scooter go depends on which one you mean, and the label is the maximum on the flat. Razor lists up to 10 mph for the E100 and 15 mph for the E300 and E300S.

Tests are thin. EScooterNerds reports a 15 mph top speed for the E300 in its own test, matching the spec, and says a third-party test also recorded 15 mph. Its author saw 18 mph on the speedometer during a full-throttle run but does not say it was a controlled measurement, so I would treat 15 as the figure. Scooter Guide did not measure anything; it calls acceleration "fairly sluggish" and says the scooter takes a relaxed approach to reach its top speed. Two Wheeling Tots did no instrumented test of the E100 either. Its only evidence is kids racing on neighborhood streets, where it says the E100 is slower than a kick scooter.

Levy says rider weight, terrain and battery charge can all pull the speed below the label, and adds that heavier riders may notice a slight reduction.

My arithmetic, using minutes per mile = 60 divided by mph: 10 mph is 6 minutes per mile and 15 mph is 4 minutes per mile.

## How fast does an E300 electric scooter go?

How fast does an E300 electric scooter go? The label says 15 mph (24 km/h), and the one independent test I found matched it, with a speedometer reading of 18 mph in one informal full-throttle run. That is it. I did not find a GPS-timed test of the E300, so a real-world top speed for a heavier rider on a hill is unknown. The seated E300S carries the same 15 mph label. ## How far can a Razor electric scooter go?

How far can a Razor electric scooter go? For the E300 and E300S, Razor says up to 10 miles and up to 40 minutes of continuous use. It lists no range for the E100, only the 40 minutes.

EScooterNerds tested the E300 and reports 7 miles, while citing a third-party test at 10 miles. Its author's own run ended when the battery died after 7.4 miles. So the spec looks like the best case. Levy says a full battery gives 40 minutes to an hour of ride time, about 6 to 15 miles, depending on model, rider weight, terrain and climate. That range is wide and I would not rely on its upper end.

My arithmetic: 10 miles in 40 minutes means an average of 15 mph, so Razor's range and run time only line up at full speed. By the same math, 40 minutes at the E100's 10 mph is about 6.7 miles, but Razor does not claim that. EScooterNerds lists the E300's battery at 168 Wh; 168 Wh over a 7 mile test is about 24 Wh per mile. That is my division of two of its figures.

Tires matter too: the same review says the wide tires give a comfortable ride but drain the battery faster. For how range works in general, see [how far and how fast an electric scooter can go](/how-far-fast-can-an-electric-scooter-go-miles-speed-and-limits/).

## How long does a Razor electric scooter last?

How long does a Razor electric scooter last in years? No one source gives a single number, and I have not owned one. The pieces I found:

- **Battery:** EScooterNerds says capacity drops significantly after about two years of use, so replacement may be needed then.
- **Tires:** the same review says replacement is typically needed after one to two years of normal use, judged by wear indicators on the tread.
- **Chain:** Two Wheeling Tots reports that after about four months the chain on its E100 came off track so the motor stopped engaging, and it fixed it by reseating the chain. That is one owner's experience.
- **Warranty:** EScooterNerds lists a 90-day warranty; I could not confirm it on Razor's pages.

So how long does a Razor electric scooter last depends on use, storage and how you look after the battery. If you ride it gently and the battery is replaced when it fades, years is plausible; that is my judgment, not a tested figure. A replacement battery pack is described by EScooterNerds as a job of under 15 minutes with 8 deck screws, and Levy recommends official Razor replacement batteries.

## How long does a Razor electric scooter battery last?

How long does a Razor electric scooter battery last per charge and over its life? These are different questions.

**Per charge:** Razor says up to 40 minutes of continuous use. Levy says 40 minutes to an hour, about 6 to 15 miles.

**Over its life:** Levy says sealed lead-acid batteries last "200 to 300 full charge cycles" with proper care, and lithium-ion up to 500, and notes that most Razor scooters use sealed lead-acid. The models I checked (E100, E300, E300S) all list sealed lead-acid. EScooterNerds says capacity drops significantly after about two years. After that Levy says a battery may still work but hold less charge, shortening range, and replacement is usually cheaper than buying a new scooter.

For general battery care across brands, see my guide to [how long an electric scooter battery lasts](/how-long-does-an-electric-scooter-battery-last-on-one-charge-and-how-to-make-it-last-longer/).

## How long does a Razor scooter battery last: charging and care

How long does a Razor scooter battery last also depends on how you charge it. The advice I found:

- **Charge time:** Levy says 8 to 12 hours for most Razor scooters and a full 12 hours before first use. Two Wheeling Tots says the initial charge takes 12 hours and later charges take 8. EScooterNerds says to charge the E300 the full 12 hours even when the green light comes on. Razor's own product pages list no charge time.
- **Do not leave it for days:** Levy says not to charge for more than 24 hours, because overcharging can reduce capacity and lifespan.
- **Charge regularly,** even when you are not riding, so the battery never fully discharges, and avoid extreme heat and cold (Levy).
- **Use the original charger** (Levy).
- **No battery meter:** EScooterNerds says the E300 has none and advises against draining it fully.

These are seller and reviewer tips, not Razor's manual, so check the manual for your model.

## How to inflate tire on Razor electric scooter models

How to inflate tire on Razor electric scooter models depends on the model. The E100 has an 8 inch pneumatic front tire and a urethane rear wheel, so only the front tire takes air. The E300 and E300S have pneumatic tires front and rear.

Levy's guide for the E300, which is a seller's walk-through, says:

1. Use a bicycle floor or hand pump with a Schrader head and a built-in gauge (the same valve as a car tire).
2. **Front tire:** remove the valve cap on the inner side of the rim, press the pump on, inflate while watching the gauge, then replace the cap.
3. **Rear tire:** pull the right handlebar grip to get the valve extender, rotate the rear wheel so the sprocket opening lines up with the valve, slide the chain guard cover down, hand-tighten the extender on, inflate, then immediately unscrew the extender and close the cover. Levy warns that leaving the extender on can damage the valve core or cause slow leaks.
4. Check pressure weekly. Levy says tires lose about 1 to 2 PSI a week.

**The pressure figure is disputed.** Levy gives 45 to 60 PSI, optimal 50 to 55, minimum 40 and maximum 65, while EScooterNerds lists 40 PSI and says to see the tire sidewall. My view: use the number printed on your tire's sidewall and your manual, and do not exceed it. Levy also says the E300 tires are 10 inches, while Razor's page says 9 inches, which I show but cannot resolve.

If a tire goes flat, see [how to fix a flat tire on an electric scooter](/how-to-fix-a-flat-tire-on-an-electric-scooter/).

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast does an electric razor scooter go?
Razor lists the E100 at up to 10 mph and the E300 and E300S at up to 15 mph.

### How fast does an E300 electric scooter go in real life?
EScooterNerds and a third-party test recorded 15 mph, and one informal full-throttle run showed 18 mph on the speedometer.

### How fast does a Razor electric scooter go for kids?
The E100, which Razor lists for age 8 and up, goes up to 10 mph.

### How far can a Razor electric scooter go?
Up to 10 miles for the E300 and E300S per Razor. EScooterNerds measured 7 miles.

### How far can a Razor electric scooter go on one charge with an adult?
I found no adult-specific test. Razor lists up to 40 minutes of continuous use and Levy says range falls with rider weight.

### How long does a Razor electric scooter last?
No single figure. EScooterNerds says the battery loses significant capacity after about two years and tires need replacing after one to two years.

### How long does a Razor electric scooter battery last on a charge?
Razor says up to 40 minutes. Levy says 40 minutes to an hour.

### How long does a Razor scooter battery last before it needs replacing?
Levy says sealed lead-acid batteries last 200 to 300 full charge cycles. EScooterNerds says about two years.

### How long does a Razor electric scooter battery last if I never charge it?
I found no source. Levy says to charge regularly so it never fully discharges.

### How long does a Razor electric scooter take to charge?
Levy says 8 to 12 hours, with a full 12 hours the first time. Razor's pages list no charge time.

### How to inflate tire on Razor electric scooter E300?
Use a pump with a Schrader head. The rear tire needs the valve extender in the right handlebar, which you must remove after inflating. Check the sidewall for the pressure.

### What PSI should Razor E300 tires be?
Levy says 45 to 60 PSI and EScooterNerds says 40 PSI. Use the sidewall and your manual.

### Does the Razor E100 have air in both tires?
No. Razor lists an 8 inch pneumatic front tire and a urethane rear wheel.

### Can adults ride a Razor E300?
Razor lists it for age 13 and up with a 220 lb limit, and EScooterNerds says it is enough for most adults.

### Is a Razor electric scooter waterproof?
Scooter Guide says the E300 has no IP rating, so I would not ride it in the rain.
