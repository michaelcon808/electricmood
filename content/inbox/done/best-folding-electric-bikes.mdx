# Best Folding Electric Bikes 2027

For most people the best folding electric bike 2027 offers is the Lectric XP4 750. OutdoorGearLab scored it 86 and named it its best folding e-bike, with an 840 Wh battery, a 750 W motor and a price of $1,299 on sale. If you want to spend about $1,000, the plain XP4 at $999 is the value pick. If your priority is a bike you can carry up stairs or onto a train, the Brompton Electric and Tern Vektron fold far smaller, but they cost $3,249 to $4,950 in the sources I read. Most folders in this class are heavy, between 59 and 75 lb, so "folding" mostly means storage, not carrying.

I haven't ridden any of these bikes. This guide pulls together the folding lists from OutdoorGearLab, Electric Bike Review, Electric Bike Report, RiderGuide, Popular Science, Outdoor Life, Tom's Guide and The Inertia, and I say which number comes from whom. They disagree on prices, weights and even batteries, and I show those conflicts instead of picking a winner. The newest of them was updated October 7, 2026.

The honest limit of the evidence: only a few of these lists give a real tested range, and none gives long-term reliability data. Treat my picks as a well-sourced shortlist.

## Wheels, tires and fit

Most folders here use 20 inch wheels, with tire widths of 2.5 to 4 inches. The wide tires (Portola 3 inches, Sinch and Ranger 4 inches) add comfort and grip but also weight. Smaller wheels are also used: the Dahon K-Feather has 16 inch wheels with 1.5 inch tires, and the Brompton P Line has 16 inch wheels (Outdoor Life).

Fit is the folding trade-off. The Fold 1 Plus is the most flexible on paper, fitting about 4 ft 9 in to 6 ft 5 in (Popular Science). Taller riders wanted more leg extension on the Portola. Electric Bike Report calls the Portola best for short and average-height riders. If you are over about 6 ft, test ride a folder with an extendable stem before you buy.

Check the class too. The XP4 is class 2 or 3, the Portola ships class 2 and unlocks to class 3, the Fold 1 Plus is class 3, and the Mars 3.0 can exceed 30 mph according to Electric Bike Report. Wikipedia's [summary of e-bike laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle_laws) says federal law defines a low-speed electric bicycle as one with a motor under 750 W and a motor-only top speed under 20 mph, and states set their own rules on where each class may ride. A 750 W motor sits at that line. See my [e-bike class explainer](/what-are-the-different-e-bike-classes-class-1-2-3-and-4/) and [laws by state guide](/pa-california-florida-new-jersey-electric-bike-laws/).

## Taking a folding e-bike on trains, planes and in the car

This is why people buy a folder, so here is what the sources and the rules say.

- **Trains and buses:** operators set their own rules for size, weight and batteries, and I haven't summarized them. Popular Science says the Fold 1 Plus is not ideal for trains and that the Brompton folds to roughly a third of the size of the other bikes it tested. Check your operator.
- **Cars:** the Mars 3.0 barely fit a hatchback in Popular Science's test. Measure your trunk against the folded sizes above.
- **Planes:** the FAA's [PackSafe guidance](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/lithium-batteries) limits lithium-ion batteries to 100 Wh each, allows up to two spares of 101 to 160 Wh with airline approval, and its [packing poster](https://www.faa.gov/hazmat/packsafe/resources/spare-lithium-batteries-packing-poster-landscape.pdf) lists 161 Wh and above as forbidden. Only the Dahon K-Feather's 120 Wh battery falls in the 101 to 160 Wh band. The Urtopia (about 245 to 252 Wh), Brompton (300 to 345 Wh), Tern (400 Wh) and the rest are well above it, so assume they cannot fly as luggage. The FAA page does not mention e-bikes, so ask the airline.
- **Apartments:** large lithium batteries carry fire risk. Charge on a hard surface, never charge a damaged battery and check your building's rules.

## Battery safety and certification

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission published a proposed rule on June 24, 2026 that would make UL 2849 (e-bikes) and UL 2271 (replaceable battery packs) mandatory with modifications. The [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12749/safety-standard-for-lithium-ion-batteries-used-in-micromobility-products-and-electrical-systems-of) documents 227 micromobility battery incidents from 2019 to 2023, with 39 deaths and 181 injuries, and says 195 of them were fires. It is only a proposal, and I found no final rule. I cover it in my [e-bike trends and news](/electric-bike-trends-news-urban-market-regulations/) guide.

Practical points for folders:

- Lectric says its XPress2 (a different bike) has a UL 2271 battery and UL 2849 certification. I haven't seen a certification claim for the folders in this guide in my sources, so check the box or the maker's page.
- The Inertia removed the Rad Power RadExpand 5 from its list after the CPSC warned about battery fire risk from several Rad Power models. Electric Bike Review still lists the RadExpand 5 Plus at $2,099. I don't know whether the warning covers that exact battery, so check the CPSC notice before you buy any Rad.

PeopleForBikes says nearly half of 2024 e-bike sales were direct-to-consumer, and many folders are sold that way, so check the warranty and who handles repairs before you buy.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best folding electric bike 2027?
The Lectric XP4 750 for most people: OutdoorGearLab's best folding e-bike at a score of 86, with an 840 Wh battery, at $1,299 on sale.

### What is the best folding electric bike under $1,000?
The Lectric XP4 at $999 or the Ride1Up Portola at $895 to $995. The Portola was tested at 39.2 miles by RiderGuide.

### What is the lightest folding electric bike?
The Urtopia Carbon Fold at 29 lb (Fold 1) or 35 lb (Fold 2). The Brompton Electric is about 32 to 43 lb and folds smaller.

### What is the smallest folding electric bike?
The Brompton Electric, which every list that includes it shows as the smallest fold, followed by the Tern Vektron Q9.

### How far do folding electric bikes go?
From about 19 miles in OutdoorGearLab's throttle-only test of the XP4 to about 65 miles at the lowest assist in Electric Bike Review's list. Small-battery folders such as the Brompton go about 27 miles at max assist.

### Are folding electric bikes safe?
They are as safe as their brakes, fit and battery. The XP4 has hydraulic brakes, and check the hinge every time you unfold. Battery certification is the open question, and the CPSC's proposed rule would require UL standards.

### Can I take a folding electric bike on a plane?
Probably not. The FAA's PackSafe limits top out at 160 Wh for spares, and nearly every battery here is larger. The FAA page doesn't mention e-bikes, so ask the airline.

### Are folding e-bikes good for heavy riders?
Some are. The Velotric Fold 1 Plus lists a 450 lb total limit and the Heybike Ranger 3.0 Pro 440 lb.

### Is a folding e-bike harder to ride?
Smaller wheels change handling. Popular Science said the Brompton rides like a larger bike, but I haven't ridden any of these.

### What class are folding e-bikes?
Mostly class 2 or 3. The XP4 is class 2 or 3, the Portola ships class 2 and unlocks to class 3, and the Fold 1 Plus is class 3.

### Should I buy a folding or a non-folding e-bike?
Buy a folder only if you need the storage or transport. The non-folding picks in my best electric bikes guide weigh 37 to 94 lb, so folding does not make a bike lighter.

## The bottom line

For most people, buy the Lectric XP4 750 as the best folding electric bike 2027 offers, the XP4 if you must stay near $1,000, or a Brompton or Tern if you need to carry the bike up stairs or onto a train and can pay for it. Remember that anything over about 59 lb is a bike you store folded, not carry, and that the range numbers are claims unless I name a tester.

Prices and models change, so check the current price and specs on the maker's page before you buy. Since I haven't ridden these e-bikes, treat my picks as a well-sourced shortlist and read a hands-on review of the one you choose.
