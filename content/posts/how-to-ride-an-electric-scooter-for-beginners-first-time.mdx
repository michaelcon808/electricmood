# How to Ride an Electric Scooter for Beginners 2027

How to ride an electric scooter for beginners comes down to five moves: put on a helmet, check the scooter, kick off with one foot, squeeze the throttle once you are rolling, and brake early and smoothly. Most scooters need a push to about 2 to 3 mph before the motor engages, and learning the basics is quick. Levy says most people pick up the controls in under an hour, with full comfort developing within a week.

This guide walks through how to ride a scooter step by step, then covers the questions beginners ask next: how hard it is, how to ride on the road, which helmet to wear, whether you can ride without power or on gravel, and whether a scooter makes sense for getting to work.

I haven't ridden or tested any of these scooters. The steps come from RiderGuide, Gotrax and Levy, helmet advice from Helmets.org (summarising CPSC guidance) and injury figures from the CPSC. Where sources disagree I show both, and where something is my own opinion I say so.

## Before your first ride: gear and a pre-ride check

If you are wondering how to ride an electric scooter for the first time, start before you move. How to ride a scooter for the first time and how to drive a scooter for the first time are the same question, and the first answer is the same on any model: prepare before you push off.

**Gear.** RiderGuide says a CPSC-certified bike helmet is the minimum, and that "more speed = more gear": add long pants, gloves, a long-sleeved shirt and closed-toe shoes for higher speeds or road traffic. Levy and Gotrax also recommend knee and elbow pads (Levy adds wrist guards), while RiderGuide calls elbow and knee pads usually overkill for most riders. That is a genuine disagreement. As a beginner who will fall at least once, my view is that padding on the first few practice sessions is cheap insurance, but the helmet is the one everyone agrees on.

**Know the controls.** Find the throttle, brakes, lights and horn, and learn how the folding stem and handlebars lock. RiderGuide suggests you can test balance and steering with the power off, using the scooter like a kick scooter.

**Pre-flight check.** RiderGuide's list is: tires inflated correctly, folds locked, brakes adjusted, no unusual noises or damage, and enough battery for the trip. Levy's version is shorter: tires, brakes and battery. If the brakes feel loose, see [how to adjust, fix and replace electric scooter brakes](/how-to-fix-adjust-and-change-brake-pads-on-an-electric-scooter/) before riding.

**Check the limits.** Look up your scooter's weight limit before you buy or ride; I cover it in [electric scooter weight limits](/how-much-weight-can-an-electric-scooter-hold/).

## How to ride a scooter step by step: your first ride

Here is how to ride a scooter step by step, and how to drive a scooter step by step is the same sequence, because "drive" just means ride in everyday speech. Practise in a flat, empty area away from traffic, as both Levy and RiderGuide advise, such as an empty car park.

1. **Raise the kickstand** and stand beside the scooter.
2. **Take the right stance.** RiderGuide recommends a conventional stance with your non-dominant foot forward on the deck, because side-by-side foot placement is less stable.
3. **Kick off.** Push with your other foot a few times. Gotrax says to push off 2 to 3 times and to get to about 2 to 3 mph before you use the throttle. RiderGuide says the motor typically engages around 3 mph. Zero-start models don't need a kick, so check your manual.
4. **Squeeze the throttle gently** once your feet are set. Gotrax warns: don't hold the throttle while you push off, or the motor won't engage. RiderGuide suggests bending your knees and putting your weight on your rear foot, so a quick start doesn't tug you forward.
5. **Steer.** At very low speeds, steer with the handlebars. RiderGuide says above 5 mph you should keep the bars mostly straight and steer by shifting your weight and leaning, and that pushing or pulling hard on the bars signals a balance problem.
6. **Brake.** Bend your knees, shift your weight back and use both brakes, with the front doing most of the work (RiderGuide). Straighten up before an emergency stop, because braking while turning raises crash risk. Squeeze, don't grab.
7. **Stop completely and step off**, then repeat until it is boring.

Kick-start matters for another reason. Gotrax says the kick-start needs the battery to be at least 25 to 30 percent charged, and that it also saves battery. If your scooter has a cruise-control feature, Gotrax says it activates after you hold a steady throttle for 10 seconds or more, and that tapping the brake lever turns it off. Know that before you find out the hard way.

## How to ride an electric scooter for the first time: a practice plan

If you want to know how to learn to ride an electric scooter in a structured way, Levy's plan is a good one, and I'd follow it with two changes noted below. It suggests sessions of 30 to 45 minutes:

| Day | Focus |
|---|---|
| Day 1 | Balance, kick-start, throttle control |
| Days 2 to 3 | Turning and stopping |
| Days 4 to 7 | Longer rides on quiet paths |
| Week 2 onwards | Traffic riding |

Levy also suggests starting in the lowest speed mode, with beginner modes of about 10 to 12 mph and typical top speeds of 15 to 20 mph. If your scooter has speed modes, they are usually set through the app or buttons; I cover them in [electric scooter settings, including eco mode and lights](/how-to-turn-on-and-connect-bluetooth-on-an-electric-scooter/). My two changes: do the braking drills on day 1 instead of day 2, because a scooter that goes but doesn't stop is the whole risk, and skip traffic until you can stop in a straight line from top speed without thinking about it. That second point is my opinion, not Levy's.

Choose dry weather for the first week. Levy advises avoiding rain and poor conditions while learning, and I have more on wet riding in [can you ride an electric scooter in the rain](/can-you-ride-use-or-drive-an-electric-scooter-in-the-rain/).

## How hard is it to ride an electric scooter?

How hard is it to ride an electric scooter for a first-timer? Levy's answer is: not very. It says most people learn the basics in 20 minutes to an hour, feel comfortable within 1 to 7 days, and take 7 to 14 days of consistent practice to build full confidence, including in traffic. Riders with bicycle or skateboard experience progress faster, and designs with lower centres of gravity, wider decks and speed-limiting modes help beginners. Levy ranks e-scooters as the easiest of the options it compares, with bikes and e-bikes moderate and electric skateboards hardest. It is a scooter seller, so weigh that comparison accordingly.

How easy is it to ride a scooter if you've never done it? For most adults it is easy to start and harder to do well, because the skills that matter are braking and awareness, not balance. How hard is it to drive a scooter in traffic? Harder, and that is where the 7 to 14 days comes in. How hard is it to ride a scooter if you are small or older? I don't have a sourced answer, so test a scooter in person if you can.

**Age.** Levy cites a suggested minimum age of 8 from POSNA, junior models for ages 8 to 12 under supervision, and AAP advice that under-16s should not ride unsupervised. Local law decides the legal age; see [electric scooter laws and age limits](/how-old-do-you-have-to-be-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-age-limit/), and for younger riders my [best electric scooters for kids and teens](/best-kids-electric-scooters-for-teens-8-10-12-year-olds-reviews/) guide.

## How to ride a scooter on the road

How to ride a scooter on the road is mostly about rules and visibility, not technique. Gotrax says scooter laws vary by state and county, that you should check your local government's site, that personal electric vehicles are typically allowed in bike lanes and must follow standard bike rules, and that you shouldn't run red lights or stop signs. It also advises agreeing a route beforehand and not relying on headphones or hand signals, which it says can distract you.

How to drive a scooter on the road safely, in my own words, drawing on RiderGuide: look ahead and avoid obstacles without overcorrecting, and treat a stopping front wheel as the biggest hazard (RiderGuide's point). Use your lights, take corners at low speed, and give yourself more room than you think you need. If you plan to use a main road, check first whether scooters are allowed on it.

**Why this matters.** The CPSC's figures cover all micromobility devices, not scooters alone: about 360,800 estimated emergency-department visits from 2017 through 2022, and 233 related deaths in the same period (reporting incomplete), with all micromobility injuries up nearly 21 percent in 2022 per its [October 2023 release](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2024/E-Scooter-and-E-Bike-Injuries-Soar-2022-Injuries-Increased-Nearly-21). Separately, a JAMA study of 249 patients at two Southern California emergency departments found head injuries were the most common type (40.2 percent), only 4.4 percent of riders were documented wearing a helmet, and most scooter injuries came from falls, not collisions with cars, as summarised by Helmets.org.

## Can you wear a bike helmet on a scooter, and a scooter helmet on a bike?

Can you wear a bike helmet on a scooter? Yes, according to the sources I read. Helmets.org says the CPSC advised in 2005 that bicycle helmets, with knee and elbow pads, suit powered-scooter riders, and in 2006 that bike helmets are fine for low-powered motorized scooters. Its bottom line is that "CPSC believes that a bike helmet provides sufficient protection for electric scooter riding." RiderGuide agrees that a CPSC-certified bike helmet is the minimum.

Can you wear a scooter helmet on a bike? Helmets.org says there is no US government or other standard specifically for electric scooters or other e-mobility devices, so a helmet sold as a "scooter helmet" has no scooter-specific certification to speak of. My reading: if the label shows the same bicycle-helmet certification, it should be fine on a bike too, but check the label and don't assume. I found no source that says otherwise.

Speed changes the picture. Helmets.org notes that scooter speeds and impact velocities are similar to bicycle riding, but treats e-bikes separately because some assist up to 28 mph. If you ride fast scooters, RiderGuide's "more speed, more gear" is the sensible rule.

## Can you ride an electric scooter without power?

Can you ride an electric scooter without power? Usually yes, says Levy: most electric scooters can be ridden in a manual mode, although it is harder and slower. The efficient way is to kick with one foot while standing on the deck.

Can you use an electric scooter without power for long? Not really. Levy says the motor is disengaged when the scooter is off, but the weight still causes drag, and hub motors create more resistance than chain-driven systems. An average electric scooter weighs about 13 kg (28.6 lb), and high-performance models up to 22.6 kg (50 lb) or more, against 5 to 10 lb for a traditional kick scooter. Levy suggests manual riding for a few blocks, flat ground or getting to a charger, and warns against it for long distances, hills or daily commutes, since extended use can strain or damage the motor. Check your manual for a freewheel or manual mode setting. Levy also says to charge soon after the battery dies and to avoid regularly discharging below 20 percent. For the charging side, see [how far an electric scooter can go on one charge](/how-far-fast-can-an-electric-scooter-go-miles-speed-and-limits/).

## Can you ride an electric scooter on gravel?

Can you ride an electric scooter on gravel? It depends, according to Gyroor: on tire type, motor power, suspension and rider skill. It says pneumatic (air-filled) tires give better shock absorption and traction than solid ones, that front or dual suspension handles gravel much better than a rigid frame, and that a motor of 500 W or more helps keep speed and stability. Gravel raises the chance of skidding or losing balance, and wet or muddy gravel is especially slippery.

Its tips, which I'd follow as a beginner: keep your weight centred, avoid sudden braking, slow down on loose gravel, take wider turns and accelerate gradually. Check tire pressure and tread regularly. This is one maker's advice, and I have no tested figures, so as a first-timer I'd stay on pavement until braking and steering feel automatic.

## Can I use an electric scooter to get to work?

Can I use an electric scooter to get to work? Yes, if the route and the range fit. Navee, a maker, gives this commuting advice:

- **Test the route** on a weekend or outside peak traffic before relying on it.
- **Prefer protected bike lanes, paths and quiet streets** where scooters are permitted. A slightly longer route can be better if it avoids fast traffic.
- **Plan range with a buffer.** It says to plan for 1.3 to 1.5 times your normal round-trip distance, so a 12-mile round trip calls for about 16 to 18 miles of usable range, and a 30 to 50 percent reserve for hills, cold, high speeds, wind, heavy bags or no workplace charging. For real range figures, see [how far an electric scooter can go on one charge](/how-far-fast-can-an-electric-scooter-go-miles-speed-and-limits/).
- **Charge at work only where allowed**, with the charger supplied or approved for that scooter.
- **Lock the frame** to a fixed rack with a hardened U-lock or high-quality folding lock, not only through a wheel or handlebar. See [how to lock up an electric scooter](/how-to-lock-up-an-electric-scooter-to-a-bike-rack/).
- **On public transport**, check each operator's rules, practise folding at home and fold before the fare gate.

## Common beginner mistakes

These come straight from the sources above:

- **Holding the throttle while pushing off**, so the motor never engages (Gotrax).
- **Braking while turning**, which raises crash risk (RiderGuide).
- **Gripping and yanking the handlebars** instead of leaning, a sign of a balance problem (RiderGuide).
- **Letting the front wheel stop** against an obstacle, which RiderGuide calls the biggest hazard.
- **Forgetting cruise control**, which activates after a steady throttle for 10 seconds or more (Gotrax).
- **Riding in rain or traffic too soon** (Levy).
- **Skipping the helmet.** Only 4.4 percent of the injured riders in the JAMA study were documented wearing one.

## Frequently asked questions

### How to ride an electric scooter for beginners in one minute?
Helmet on, kick off to about 2 to 3 mph, squeeze the throttle gently, steer by leaning above 5 mph and brake early with both hands.

### How to ride an electric scooter for the first time without falling?
Practise in an empty flat area, use the lowest speed mode and learn braking before speed. Levy suggests 30 to 45 minute sessions.

### How to ride a scooter for the first time if the throttle won't work?
You probably need to be moving. Gotrax says the motor needs 2 to 3 mph and won't engage if you hold the throttle while pushing off.

### How to drive a scooter for the first time on a hill?
I have no sourced guidance. Stay on flat ground until braking is automatic, and check your scooter's hill rating.

### How hard is it to ride an electric scooter if I have never balanced on one?
Not hard. Levy says most people learn the basics in under an hour.

### How hard is it to ride a scooter compared with a bike?
Levy rates e-scooters easier than bicycles and e-bikes, and electric skateboards hardest, but it sells scooters.

### How hard is it to drive a scooter in busy traffic?
Harder than learning the controls. Levy puts full confidence including traffic at 7 to 14 days of consistent practice.

### How easy is it to ride a scooter for kids?
Levy says under 8 is not recommended, and 8 to 12 with junior models under supervision. Check local age rules.

### How to learn to ride an electric scooter quickly?
Short, frequent sessions: balance and throttle on day 1, turning and stopping on days 2 to 3, per Levy's plan.

### How to ride a scooter on the road legally?
Follow your local rules. Gotrax says scooters are typically allowed in bike lanes and must follow standard bike rules, but laws vary.

### How to drive a scooter on the road at night?
I have no sourced guidance. Use lights and reflective gear, and slow down.

### How to ride a scooter step by step with the motor off?
Stand with one foot on the deck and kick with the other, as RiderGuide suggests for testing balance.

### How to drive a scooter step by step if it has no kick-start?
Zero-start models move off with the throttle alone, per RiderGuide. Check your manual.

### Can you ride an electric scooter without power uphill?
Levy advises against manual riding on hills or for long distances.

### Can you use an electric scooter without power if the battery is dead?
For a short distance, yes, per Levy. Charge it soon afterwards.

### Can you ride an electric scooter on gravel with solid tires?
Gyroor says pneumatic tires are better on gravel, so solid tires are the weaker choice.

### Can I use an electric scooter to get to work in the rain?
Only with a rated scooter and care. See my rain guide linked above.

### Can you wear a bike helmet on a scooter at higher speeds?
CPSC guidance relayed by Helmets.org covers low-powered scooters. For faster riding RiderGuide says to add more gear.

### Can you wear a scooter helmet on a bike if it is certified?
Check the label for a bicycle certification. Helmets.org says no standard exists specifically for scooters.

## The bottom line

So, how to ride an electric scooter for beginners? Gear up, check the scooter, kick off to 2 to 3 mph, squeeze the throttle gently, steer by leaning once you are moving and brake early with both hands. Spend your first week in an empty, flat space, learn to stop before you learn to go fast, and save traffic, gravel and rain for later.
