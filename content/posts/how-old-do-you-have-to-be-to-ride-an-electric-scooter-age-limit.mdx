# How Old Do You Have to Be to Ride an Electric Scooter?

How old do you have to be to ride an electric scooter? It depends on the state. The National Conference of State Legislatures (NCSL) counted 15 states with a minimum rider age in its [September 2024 report](https://www.ncsl.org/transportation/states-roll-out-electric-scooter-laws): 8 in Utah at the low end, 18 in Arizona and Illinois at the high end, and 16 the most common figure. The American Academy of Pediatrics ([AAP](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/safety-prevention/on-the-go/Pages/E-Scooters.aspx)) goes further and says children under 16 should not ride motorized scooters at all. The weight question has a shorter answer: most adult scooters are rated for about 220 to 265 lbs, and heavier-duty models list 330 lbs or more.

This page covers both, because they are the two limits people hit first. It starts with age: how old you should be to ride an electric scooter, state by state, then helmet rules, rental apps and California. It ends with how much weight an electric scooter can hold and what happens if you go over.

I haven't ridden or tested any scooter for this. The age and law figures come from NCSL, the AAP, the California Vehicle Code text and a Sacramento city page. The weight figures come from scooter sellers (Levy, Hiboy, Gyroor), which are claims, not independent tests. Where sources disagree I show both. Laws change, so check your state or city before you rely on any number here; this is general information, not legal advice.

## How old do you have to be to ride an electric scooter?

There is no single answer, and I did not find one national rule in any source I read. NCSL's report is built from state laws, and it says 15 states set a minimum operator age. The rest are not on that list, which suggests they set no statewide minimum, though that is my reading of the report and not something it says outright. Cities and counties can add their own rules, and rental companies add theirs.

So how old do you have to be to ride an electric scooter? Three layers apply, and the strictest one wins:

- **State law:** anywhere from 8 to 18 where a minimum exists (table below).
- **City rules:** some local authorities set a higher age or ban scooters from sidewalks, paths or parks.
- **Rental company terms:** NCSL says Bird, Lime and Lyft require users to be at least 18.

Why does it matter? The CPSC reported 360,800 emergency room visits for micromobility injuries from 2017 to 2022, plus 233 deaths, with injuries up nearly 21 percent in 2022 alone. That figure covers all micromobility products, not only scooters, but it is the backdrop for the age and helmet rules below. The release is [on the CPSC site](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2024/E-Scooter-and-E-Bike-Injuries-Soar-2022-Injuries-Increased-Nearly-21).

## Electric scooter how old do you have to be: state by state

If you have searched electric scooter how old do you have to be for your own state, this is the table. It is NCSL's list of minimum operator ages, last updated September 6, 2024. Laws move, so treat it as a starting point.

| Minimum age | States (per NCSL) |
|---|---|
| 8 | Utah |
| 12 | Minnesota |
| 14 | Oklahoma, Virginia |
| 15 | Hawaii |
| 16 | Arkansas, California, Kentucky, Nevada, New York, Oregon, Washington |
| 16½ | Mississippi |
| 18 | Arizona, Illinois |

That is 15 states in total. If your state is not in the table, NCSL did not count it as having a minimum age, but check for city rules before you hand a child a scooter.

Sixteen is the figure that comes up most, which is why many guides say 16 is the usual minimum. Do not read that as a national rule. In this table, Utah allows 8-year-olds and two states want you to be 18.

Maker blogs give a different picture in places, and I cover that in the disagreements section below.

## At what age can you ride an electric scooter on a rental app?

If you are asking at what age can you ride an electric scooter you do not own, the answer is usually 18. NCSL says the shared-scooter companies Bird, Lime and Lyft require users to be at least 18.

The catch is enforcement. The AAP notes that many cities do not verify users' ages, so minors can sign up for rental apps without parental consent. I have not tested a rental sign-up, so I cannot say how any company checks. If your teen is under the age in your state, "the app let them" is not the same as "it is legal."

## What age can you have an electric scooter?

What age can you have an electric scooter, meaning own one? I did not find a law that sets a minimum age for owning or buying one. Every age rule I read is about operating one on public roads, paths or lanes. A parent can usually buy a scooter for a child, and the child's age then decides where and whether they can ride it.

On private property the state road rules may not apply, but I have no sourced guidance on that, so check with your local authority. For kids' models and how they differ from adult scooters, see my [kids and teen scooter guide](/best-kids-electric-scooters-for-teens-8-10-12-year-olds-reviews/).

## At what age can you drive a scooter or an electric scooter?

People ask at what age can you drive a scooter and at what age can you drive an electric scooter as if "drive" meant a licensed vehicle. For a standing electric kick scooter, "drive" just means ride, so at what age can you ride a scooter of this kind is answered by the state rules above.

There is one wrinkle. California's law requires a motorized scooter operator to hold a valid driver's license or instruction permit, which I cover in the California section. That sets a practical floor in that state. The word "scooter" can also mean a moped or seated scooter, which many places treat as a motor vehicle with licence rules of its own. I have not researched those, so this page is about electric kick scooters only.

If you are in England, Wales or Scotland, the rules are different again: my guide to whether [electric scooters are legal in the UK](/are-electric-scooters-legal-in-the-uk/) covers it.

## How old should you be to ride an electric scooter? What doctors say

The law tells you the minimum. How old should you be to ride an electric scooter safely is a different question. The AAP's HealthyChildren page (updated September 9, 2026) says: "Children under 16 should not operate or ride on motorized or e-scooters." It adds that some cities set a minimum age of 16.

Its other advice is practical, and I would follow it at any age:

- **Helmet every time**, which the AAP calls "the best way to prevent serious head injuries," plus closed-toe shoes.
- **Start slowly**, because the accelerator and brake tabs take time to get used to.
- **Knee and elbow pads and reflective clothing** if you ride at night.
- **No texting, earbuds or riding impaired.**
- **Bike lanes first**, otherwise the right side of the road. No sidewalks, beach paths or parks.

The AAP does not set a supervision rule. It tells parents to set expectations, understand how the scooter works and set an example.

Levy's learning guide, a seller's page, quotes the Pediatric Orthopaedic Society of North America as giving a minimum of 8 and the AAP as saying under 16 should not ride unsupervised. That is not what the current AAP page says, so I show both and rely on the AAP page itself. If you want a beginner plan, see [how to ride an electric scooter for beginners](/how-to-ride-an-electric-scooter-for-beginners-first-time/).

## Helmet rules by age

Helmet laws are usually tied to age, and they differ by state. NCSL lists five states that require helmets for younger riders:

| State | Helmet required for |
|---|---|
| California | Under 18 |
| Minnesota | Under 18 |
| Louisiana | 17 and younger |
| Hawaii | Under 16 (protective headwear) |
| Connecticut | 15 and younger |

Everywhere else NCSL did not list a helmet law, which does not make a helmet a bad idea. The AAP says to wear one every time, and I agree. The helmet rules are why the question of how old you should be to ride an electric scooter is not only about the minimum age: a 17-year-old in California has to wear a helmet and a 19-year-old does not, on paper.

## Speed limits, roads and sidewalks

Age is not the only rule. Here is what NCSL reports, in short:

- **Top speed of the scooter:** 17 states set 20 mph, and New Jersey sets 19 mph.
- **Operator speed limit:** twelve states set 15 mph, including Arkansas, Florida, Hawaii, Mississippi, Nevada, New York, Oregon, South Dakota, Tennessee, Utah, Washington and Wisconsin. Illinois is lowest at 10 mph, and Oklahoma is highest at 25 mph.
- **Roads:** Arizona, California, Oklahoma and Oregon bar e-scooters on roads posted at 25 mph or higher (California allows some 25 to 35 mph roads with certain bike lanes). Illinois and Texas bar them at 35 mph or higher, New York at 30 mph and Maryland at 50 mph.
- **Sidewalks:** Minnesota's law says e-scooters may not be operated on sidewalks "except when necessary to enter or exit a property." Eleven states leave sidewalk use to local governments.
- **Parks:** Wisconsin prohibits e-scooters in state parks.

## California e scooter laws 2027

California is the state people ask about most, so here are the California e scooter laws 2027 readers should check, based on the Vehicle Code section 21235 text as published on [California Public Law](https://california.public.law/codes/ca_veh_code_section_21235) and the City of Sacramento's [scooter safety and law page](https://www.cityofsacramento.gov/public-works/mobility-and-sustainability/transportation-planning/active_transportation/e-bike-and-scooter-guidelines/electric-scooter-safety-and-law). The code page's source note says the section was last updated January 1, 2019, and I could not open the official legislature site, so confirm the current wording before you rely on it.

| Rule | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Licence | The code says no operating "without a valid driver's license or instruction permit." |
| Minimum age | Sacramento says operators must be at least 16 with a licence or permit. NCSL also lists 16. The section text I read states no minimum age itself. |
| Helmet | Required for operators under 18 (bicycle helmet meeting section 21212). |
| Speed | 15 mph maximum. |
| Roads | Over 25 mph speed limit: only within a Class II bike lane or Class IV separated bikeway. Local authorities may allow up to 35 mph roads by ordinance. |
| Sidewalks | Not allowed except to enter or leave adjacent property. Sacramento lists a fine of up to $207 for sidewalk riding in the city. |
| Passengers | None. One rider only. |
| Parking | Do not block pedestrian passage; Sacramento says not to block ramps, doorways or crosswalks. |

So if you ask at what age can you ride a scooter in California, or at what age can you ride an electric scooter there, Sacramento and NCSL say 16, and the statute I read ties riding to a licence or permit instead of naming an age. That is a difference between sources, not something I can settle, and it is one more reason to check the current code.

These California e scooter laws 2027 questions often come with a second one, whether the same rules cover e-bikes. They do not; e-bikes have their own classes. See [electric bike laws by state](/pa-california-florida-new-jersey-electric-bike-laws/).

## When the sources disagree

I found real conflicts, and I am not going to pick a winner.

- **Which states have a minimum age.** Maxfind, a scooter maker, lists 16 for California, Colorado, Florida, New York, Ohio, Oregon and Arkansas, 18 for Arizona, 14 for Alaska, 12 for Minnesota and 15 for Connecticut and Hawaii. NCSL's age list does not include Colorado, Florida, Ohio, Alaska or Connecticut (Connecticut appears in NCSL only for helmets, 15 and younger). Maxfind's own page also says the common minimum is 16 and, elsewhere, that eleven states ban riders under 16, which do not obviously agree.
- **Speed in New York City.** NCSL puts New York's operator limit at 15 mph. Levy's speed-limit page lists NYC at 20 mph.
- **A federal speed limit.** Levy's page mentions a federal 20 mph figure, and the other sources I read either conflict with it or do not mention it. I would not rely on it.
- **Power caps.** Maxfind says many states cap motor power at 750 W. NCSL reports caps on top speed (20 mph in 17 states), not power.
- **Age guidance.** Levy attributes "under 16 unsupervised" to the AAP, while the AAP's current page says under 16 should not ride.

NCSL is a legislative research body and the AAP is a medical body, so I lean on them first. Check your own state's legislature or DMV page last, because it is the one that counts.

## How much weight can an electric scooter hold?

How much weight can an electric scooter hold? Most consumer adult scooters are rated for about 220 to 265 lbs (100 to 120 kg). Hiboy gives that range for most consumer-grade scooters, with entry-level models around 220 lbs, mid-range models at 265 to 300 lbs and heavy-duty models at 330 lbs (150 kg) or more, some up to 550 lbs (250 kg).

This is the rider limit, not the weight of the scooter itself. If you want the weight of an e-bike itself, see [how much an e-bike weighs](/how-much-does-an-electric-bike-or-ebike-weigh/).

The weight limit is the maker's rating, not a law. I did not find any rule that sets a legal weight limit for riders, and I did not find independent tests of what happens past the limit. Everything in this section comes from sellers: Levy, Hiboy and Gyroor.

Here is how two of them split the classes:

| Class | Levy | Gyroor |
|---|---|---|
| Lightweight / compact | 175 to 220 lbs, 250 to 350 W motors | Lightweight/commuter: 220 to 265 lbs, 250 to 500 W |
| Standard adult / commuter | 220 to 265 lbs, 350 to 500 W | (merged into the class above) |
| Mid-heavy / performance | 265 to 330 lbs, 500 to 1,000 W | 265 to 330 lbs, dual motor or single 800 W+ |
| Heavy-duty | 330 to 550+ lbs, 1,000 to 5,000 W+ | 330+ lbs, dual motors of 1,000 W+ each |

The two do not divide the classes the same way, so I have not merged their numbers. The pattern is the same, though: more weight capacity comes with bigger motors and stronger frames.

## What counts toward the electric scooter weight limit

The limit covers everything on the scooter, not only your body weight. Hiboy advises choosing a limit at least 20 to 30 lbs above your body weight to cover clothing, gear and anything you carry, and says to factor in backpacks and groceries.

Here is my arithmetic, labelled as mine: a 200 lb rider with a 15 lb backpack and 5 lbs of clothes and shoes is already at 220 lbs. That is the full rating of an entry-level model, which leaves no margin.

What sets the rating, according to Gyroor and Levy:

- **Motor power.** Gyroor says a 250 W motor may be enough for a 220 lb rider on flat ground, while a 265 lb rider needs 500 W or more. Levy pairs 250 to 350 W with 180 to 220 lbs.
- **Frame.** Standard models use standard-gauge aluminium; heavy-duty ones use reinforced aluminium or steel, with thicker tubing.
- **Tires.** Gyroor says pneumatic tires spread load better than solid ones and recommends 10 inches or larger for riders over 265 lbs.
- **Brakes, suspension and battery management.** All three have to cope with the extra mass and current.

Gyroor also warns that swapping components does not raise the frame's safe load limit.

## What happens if you go over the weight limit

The sellers agree on the direction and disagree on the size. I have put their claims side by side and not blended them, because each uses a different method or none.

| Effect | Levy (riders 20% over capacity) | Gyroor (at or past max load) | Hiboy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Range | 25 to 30% lower | Up to 15 to 30% lower (20 to 30% in another section) | Lower, no figure |
| Top speed | 10 to 15% lower | 3 to 5 mph below advertised | Lower, no figure |
| Acceleration | 0 to 15 mph in 6 to 9 s vs 4 to 6 s at capacity | 160 lb rider at 20 mph in about 7 s vs 10+ s at 260 lbs | None |
| Braking | 15 to 25% longer stopping distance | Brakes must handle more momentum | None |
| Hills | May need to kick-push | None | None |
| Wear | Frame cracks after 500 to 1,000 miles instead of 3,000+; shorter motor and tire life | Faster wear on pads, tires, suspension, stem joints | More wear |
| Battery | 350 to 600 cycles instead of 500 to 800 | Overload heat speeds degradation | Shorter life |
| Warranty | "Typically voids" | Can void; covers defects, not overload | "Voided warranty" |

Levy and Gyroor give specific numbers, but neither shows how it got them, so read them as the sellers' estimates. A second Levy page says overloaded motors may overheat, brakes "might not function optimally" and the frame "could become unstable."

My view, labelled as such: the warranty point is the one I would take most seriously, because it costs you money regardless of how big the performance drop is.

## Choosing a scooter if you are a heavier rider

These tips come from Gyroor unless I say otherwise:

- Choose a rating above your total load. For riders at 220 to 265 lbs, Gyroor suggests at least 275 to 300 lbs of capacity.
- Over 265 lbs, look for a 1,000 W+ motor, a reinforced frame, 10 inch or larger pneumatic tires and hydraulic or dual disc brakes.
- Levy suggests its Standard Adult class for riders of 180 to 240 lbs on commutes up to 10 miles.
- Check tire pressure weekly, brake pad wear monthly, and the bolts on the folding mechanism, stem and handlebars regularly.
- Inspect the frame and stem for cracks and listen for new creaks.
- Ride smoothly: avoid hard braking and sudden acceleration, and bend your knees over bumps.

## Frequently asked questions

### How old do you have to be to ride an electric scooter?

### How old do you have to be to ride an electric scooter in California?
Sacramento and NCSL say 16, with a licence or permit. The code text I read requires a licence or permit and states no age. Helmets are required under 18.

### How old should you be to ride an electric scooter?
The AAP says children under 16 should not operate or ride motorized scooters. Check your state's legal minimum too.

### At what age can you ride a scooter?
For an electric kick scooter, it varies by state from 8 to 18 where a minimum exists. For mopeds or seated scooters I have not researched the rules.

### At what age can you ride an electric scooter on rental apps?
NCSL says Bird, Lime and Lyft require users to be 18 or older. The AAP notes that many cities do not verify ages.

### At what age can you drive a scooter?
In California you need a driver's licence or instruction permit to operate a motorized scooter. Other states differ.

### At what age can you drive an electric scooter in the UK?
Private electric scooters are not legal on UK public roads or land. See my UK guide for the trial rules.

### What age can you have an electric scooter?
I found no law setting an age to own one. The rules I read are about riding.

### What age can you ride an electric scooter in New York?
NCSL lists 16 for New York, with a 15 mph operator limit.

### What is the youngest age to ride an electric scooter?
Of the states NCSL lists, Utah is lowest at 8, then Minnesota at 12.

### Do you need a licence to ride an electric scooter?
In California, yes, a driver's licence or instruction permit under the code text I read. I have no sourced answer for other states.

### Do you need a helmet to ride an electric scooter?
NCSL lists five states with helmet laws for younger riders. The AAP says to wear one every time.

### Can two people ride an electric scooter?
In California, no. The code bars passengers. I have no sourced answer for other states, and weight ratings are for one rider anyway.

### Can you ride an electric scooter on the sidewalk?
It varies. California bars it except to enter or leave property, Minnesota is similar, and eleven states leave it to local governments.

### How much weight can an electric scooter hold?
Most adult models are rated for about 220 to 265 lbs, with heavy-duty ones at 330 lbs or more, according to Hiboy and Levy.

### Does the weight limit include a backpack?
Hiboy says to factor in backpacks and groceries and to choose a limit 20 to 30 lbs above your body weight.

### What happens if you exceed an electric scooter's weight limit?
Sellers say reduced range, speed and braking, faster wear and a possible voided warranty. The numbers vary by seller.
