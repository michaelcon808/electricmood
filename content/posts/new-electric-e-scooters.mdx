# New Electric Scooters 2027: Launches and What to Wait For

The new electric scooters 2027 buyers are asking about fall into three groups, and the group matters more than the spec sheet. Some have shipped and been reviewed, like the NAVEE UT5 Ultra X. Some are on pre-order, like the Apollo City Stellar, which lists delivery for December 2026. And some are still announcements or teasers, like Segway's Max G3 Pro and ZT3 Max, which Segway announced on October 7, 2026 for Germany and France in November 2026, and Inmotion's next RS, which has no price or date. If you only want one takeaway: buy what has shipped and been tested, and treat everything else as a claim.

That is the honest answer to a question that sounds simpler than it is. I'm writing this on October 9, 2026, and almost no maker has labeled a scooter a "2027 model" yet. Most of the new e scooters 2027 shoppers will see in the next few months are 2026 launches that are still arriving, plus a few announcements for the winter. So this guide tracks what's new, says what status each one is in, and flags what independent testers have and haven't measured.

I haven't ridden any of these. Everything here comes from maker pages and press releases, trade-show coverage, and published reviews from Electric Scooter Guide, RiderGuide, The Gadgeteer, Notebookcheck, Electrek and others, and I say which is which. I'll update it as models ship.

## How I sort new scooters: shipped, pre-order and teaser

New scooter news mixes three very different things, and a good test is whether you can buy and receive it today.

1. **Shipped and reviewed.** You can buy it, and someone outside the company has ridden it. This is the only group where the speed and range numbers have a check.
2. **Pre-order or reserve.** You can pay or put down a deposit, but nobody has your scooter yet. Apollo's City Stellar page says delivery is expected in December 2026 and that you can cancel for a full refund before the fulfillment center starts processing. Read the cancel terms before you pay.
3. **Announced or teased.** There's a name, a few specs and a photo. Specs often change before launch. Electric Scooter Guide's CES roundup says much of the show lineup was "prototype or pre-release."

A model-year label is a weak signal. Electric scooters don't follow a car-style model year, and a "2027" tag usually means the maker's next release batch, not a particular date.

## NAVEE UT5 Ultra X: the new scooter with real reviews

The NAVEE UT5 Ultra X is the best-documented new electric scooter right now, and it is a good example of claimed versus measured.

NAVEE claims a 43 mph top speed, two 1,200 W motors rated at 2,400 W peak each, a 57.6 V, 22.3 Ah, 1,284 Wh battery, and a range of 74.6 miles at 9.3 mph or 37.3 miles at 24.9 mph. It weighs about 90 lb (90.4 on the product page and 89.3 in a PDF, according to The Gadgeteer) and lists at $2,499.99.

What reviewers found:

- **The Gadgeteer** (July 27, 2026) saw about 37 to 38 mph, below NAVEE's 43 mph, but didn't verify it with a GPS device. It didn't run the battery to empty, though it rode roughly 30 to 40 miles. Its verdict was that the scooter is "thrilling transportation, if you can handle its weight."
- **Notebookcheck** (July 26, 2026) says testing confirmed speeds up to 44 mph, and calls it a solid, high-torque ride. It lists a large throttle dead zone and unintuitive speed-mode switching as cons, and no measured range.

So the speed claim has one reviewer above it and one below it, neither using a GPS I can verify. The range is untested by both. My own math from NAVEE's numbers is that the battery works out to about 17 Wh per mile at the 9.3 mph range-test speed and about 34 Wh per mile at 24.9 mph, which shows how much the claimed 74.6 miles depends on riding slowly. At 90 lb, it is also a scooter you can't carry up stairs. ## How to read new-scooter speed and range claims

New launches lean on a few numbers, and they hide as much as they show.

- **Top speed** is often a firmware or ideal-condition figure. The UT5 Ultra X is claimed at 43 mph and measured by one reviewer at 37 to 38 mph and by another up to 44 mph.
- **Range** is quoted at a slow speed. NAVEE's 74.6 miles is at 9.3 mph, Segway's 100 km at 20 km/h. A range at 24.9 mph is half as far.
- **Peak power** is not nominal power. The Apollo City Stellar page gives three different motor figures for the same scooter.
- **Weight** often isn't stated at launch. The Segway ZT3 MAX and MAX G3 Pro had none.
- **Price** shifts. The same NAVEE model has two prices in one article.

A quick sanity check is watt-hours divided by claimed miles. In the tested data I used for other guides on this site, commuter-class scooters land near 19 to 27 Wh per mile, with fast ones higher. A claim far below that usually needs a very slow test speed. Those are my own rough numbers, not a standard. ## Should you buy now or wait?

Here is how I'd decide.

1. **Need a scooter this month:** buy one that has shipped and been tested. A "new" label doesn't make it better.
2. **Eyeing a pre-order:** confirm the refund terms, the delivery date and the warranty address. Apollo's City Stellar page, for example, allows a full-refund cancellation before processing starts.
3. **Waiting on a teaser:** the Inmotion RS successor and the VMAX VX6 are examples where price and date aren't confirmed, so don't plan around them.
4. **Watching prices:** an older model can drop when a new one lands. The Inmotion RS, for instance, was reported back at $4,000 after a $3,100 sale. That's one data point, not a rule.
5. **Check your local rules:** the Apollo page says the City Stellar won't ship to New York State because its top speed is above 20 mph. I haven't summarized state or city laws, so check yours before ordering anything fast.

Regional launches matter too. Several of the newest scooters, like the Xiaomi 6 series and the Segway Max G3 Pro, are launching in Europe first, and I found no US date for them.

## Safety and certification on new scooters

A new scooter is also a new battery pack. The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death), and asked manufacturers to certify to UL standards, including UL 2272. The Apollo City Stellar page lists UL 2271 and UL 2272, which is the maker's claim. I couldn't verify the certification for the other new models, so check the box or the maker's page, and charge on a hard, non-flammable surface.

Speed raises the risk, too. The faster models here, at 40 mph and above, call for a helmet and full protective gear, and they're not beginner scooters.
