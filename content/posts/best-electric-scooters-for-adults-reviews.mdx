# Best Electric Scooters 2027: Top Picks for Every Rider

The best electric scooter for most adults is the Segway Ninebot MAX G3. It's the most refined and comfortable scooter in the independent tests I read, with 28 mph tested top speed, dual hydraulic suspension and IPX6 water resistance. If you want most of that ride for less, the Navee ST3 Pro is my value pick. If you're on a tight budget, start with the Navee GT3 Pro.

Those are my picks for the best electric scooters for adults 2027, but the best e scooter 2027 for your money depends on how you ride: how far, how hilly, how much you need to carry it, and what you're willing to spend. This guide gives you 9 full picks, the tested numbers behind each one, and a simple way to choose.

I want to be straight about how this guide was built. I haven't ridden these scooters, and I won't claim otherwise. My picks come from independent track-test data, mainly ERideHero's measured speed, range, braking and hill results and TechGearLab's ranking of 18 scooters it bought and rode, plus the manufacturers' stated specs. Where a number is a test result rather than a claim, I say so. I'm writing this in October 2026 and I'll update the guide as new 2027 models launch.

## How I chose these scooters

Reviews that only repeat spec sheets don't help you, so here's the method behind these best electric scooter reviews 2027 picks. People often search for the top rated and best selling electric scooters 2027, but I don't have sales data, so I rank by test results instead.

1. **Tested numbers over claims.** Independent testers measure speed, range and braking themselves. ERideHero says claimed range is often 40 to 60 percent higher than what riders actually get. So the table above uses measured range.
2. **Agreement between testers.** A scooter that does well in more than one independent test earns more trust. TechGearLab's testers ranked the MAX G3 first among the 18 scooters they bought and rode, with the MAX G2 right behind it.
3. **Ride quality and comfort.** Suspension, tire type and deck size decide whether a 30-minute commute feels fine or punishing.
4. **Safety features and warranty.** Brakes, lights, turn signals, a water-resistance rating, a sturdy folding mechanism and clear warranty terms matter more than a headline top speed.
5. **Weight and portability.** A scooter you can't carry up your stairs is a different purchase than one you can.
6. **Reliability and support.** I favored established brands with a track record, because a warranty and parts availability matter once something wears out.

I left out hyperscooters for most of this guide, because at 100 pounds and higher they're a different category from the commuter scooters most people are shopping for. I mention them near the end.

## Segway Ninebot MAX G3: best overall

**Best for:** riders who want the most comfortable, refined commuter and don't mind paying more.

The MAX G3 sits at the top of two independent lists. TechGearLab's testers named it the best of the 18 scooters in their test and said they still enjoyed riding it after about 500 miles and a year of use. ERideHero picks it for the best ride quality in the commuter class.

The tested numbers are strong rather than extreme: 28 mph top speed, 3.5 seconds from standstill to 15 mph, a 10.8-foot braking distance, and 24.9 miles of real-world range from a 597 Wh battery. The motor is rated at 850 W, and the scooter carries riders up to 286 pounds.

What stands out is how it rides. It has dual hydraulic, adjustable suspension, 11-inch self-healing tubeless tires and an IPX6 water-resistance rating, the highest of the commuter picks. ERideHero calls its steering stable and precise even at top speed.

**Pros**
- Best suspension and ride quality in this guide
- IPX6 water resistance
- 286 lb rider capacity
- Strong acceleration and short braking distance

**Cons**
- Costs more than the ST3 Pro and KQi 300X
- Tested range is the same as the cheaper ST3 Pro
- At 54.2 lb and bulky, it's not a scooter to carry far

## Navee ST3 Pro: best value commuter

**Best for:** commuters who want near-premium comfort without the premium price.

The ST3 Pro is the one I'd point most budget-conscious commuters toward. ERideHero rates it the best value in the commuter class, and says its ride quality comes close to the MAX G3's at a lower price. The tested numbers back that up.

Tested figures: 24.8 mph top speed, 24.9 miles of range, 4.0 seconds to 15 mph, and an 11.5-foot braking distance from a drum-and-disc brake setup. It has a 596.7 Wh, 48V battery, a 600 W motor, dual suspension and self-healing 10-inch tubeless tires. It also has turn signals, GPS tracking and an IPX5 rating, and it climbed ERideHero's steepest 17 percent test hill with power to spare.

**Pros**
- Excellent value for what you get
- Dual suspension and self-healing tubeless tires
- Consistent 48V performance
- Turn signals and GPS tracking

**Cons**
- Heavy at 55 lb

If weight is your concern, the KQi 300X below saves about 6 pounds.

## NIU KQi 300X: best balanced commuter

**Best for:** riders who want a reliable all-rounder from a brand with a long track record.

The 300X doesn't win any single category, and that's the point. ERideHero measured 23.6 mph, 26.8 miles of range (better than the ST3 Pro and the MAX G3) and 3.8 seconds to 15 mph. It weighs 48.7 pounds, the lightest of the commuter picks, and has a 608 Wh battery and a 500 W motor.

Its dual hydraulic disc brakes stopped in 10.7 feet, the shortest distance in that group. It's front-suspension only, so the ride is less plush than the dual-suspension scooters, and the testers praise its folding mechanism as the best they've tried. TechGearLab scored it 73.

**Pros**

- Long tested range for its weight
- Short braking distance
- Strong app

**Cons**

## Apollo Go: best lightweight for hills

**Best for:** hilly commutes where you still need to carry the scooter.

The Apollo Go packs two motors, 700 W combined, into a 46 lb scooter, and ERideHero reports it held 12 mph on its steepest 17 percent hill, the best result among scooters under 50 lb. ## How to choose an electric scooter for adults

Specs only help if you know which ones matter. These are the ones I'd weigh, in order.

1. **Real-world range, not claimed range.** Plan on the tested figure, then add a safety margin. My guide on [how long an electric scooter battery lasts on one charge](/how-long-does-an-electric-scooter-battery-last-on-one-charge/) explains why range falls with speed, weight and hills.
2. **Battery voltage.** The testers note that 48V scooters hold their speed better as the battery drains than 36V ones, which lose noticeable power below about half a charge.
3. **Weight.** If you'll carry the scooter, anything over about 45 lb gets old fast.
4. **Suspension and tires.** Dual suspension and big tubeless tires make a rough commute far more comfortable. Self-healing tubeless tires also cut down on flats.
5. **Brakes.** A dual-brake setup with regenerative braking stops sooner and needs less upkeep.
6. **Water resistance.** An IP rating tells you how much splash or rain a scooter is built to handle. If you're not sure how to read one, [Wikipedia's explanation of the IP code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IP_Code) is a good primer, and my rain guide above covers what it means for scooters.
7. **Rider weight limit.** Check that the scooter's maximum load comfortably exceeds your weight plus anything you carry.
8. **Safety certification and warranty.** Look for a recognized electrical safety certification for the battery and charger, and read the warranty for what it excludes. A scooter with a clear warranty and local support is worth more than a bargain with none.
9. **Features you'll actually use.** Turn signals, GPS tracking, a decent app and a bright display all matter on a daily commute.
10. **Total cost.** The sticker price isn't the whole picture. See my guide on [how much an electric scooter costs to buy](/how-much-does-an-electric-scooter-cost-to-buy/) for what to budget.
