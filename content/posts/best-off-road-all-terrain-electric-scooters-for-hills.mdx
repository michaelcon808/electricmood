## How steep is steep: grade, percent and degrees

Hill claims are confusing because makers and testers use different units.

- **Percent grade** is rise over run. A 10 percent grade rises 10 ft for every 100 ft you travel horizontally, per Levy's explainer. This is the unit testers use.
- **Degrees** are what most spec sheets use. They sound smaller than they are. By simple trigonometry, 30 degrees is a grade of about 58 percent, and 35 degrees is about 70 percent. That's my math, not a source's.

That second point matters because the Kaabo Mantis King GT is listed at a 30 degree climb rating, and the EScooterNerds reviewer says he climbed a hill of roughly 35 degrees. He labels it an estimate. A 58 to 70 percent grade is a wall, far steeper than a real street, so treat any "30 degree" claim as a theoretical maximum, not a hill you will ever ride. By comparison, Electric Scooter Guide's own test hill is 10 percent over 200 ft, ERideHero's tougher test is 17 percent, and Levy says standard scooters handle about 10 to 15 percent while hill-focused models with motors of 500 W or more can reach 20 percent or more. Levy is a brand blog and gives no test data, so I use that as a rough guide only.

For real hills, the number that matters is how fast a scooter holds its speed on a known grade, and that's what the tested times below show.

## Best all terrain electric scooters 2027: what the label means

"All terrain" is a marketing word, so here is what to check before you believe it. RiderGuide's off-road advice, which I agree with, boils down to this:

- **Tires:** at least 10 to 11 inch pneumatic, with knobby tread if you ride loose dirt. ERideHero points out that tubeless self-healing tires (on the King GTR, Max G3, ST3 Pro and others) resist punctures better than tubed ones, and that tubes, like the Inmotion Climber's, are prone to pinch flats.
- **Suspension:** dual suspension with adjustable hydraulic damping beats a spring or rubber block. The King GTR, Burn-E 2 Max and Max G3 use dual hydraulic units, while the Climber has none.
- **Brakes:** hydraulic discs once you pass moderate speeds. A rear-only disc, like the Climber's, stops longer.
- **Ground clearance and deck height:** ERideHero calls out the King GTR's clearance. Low decks scrape.
- **Torque:** RiderGuide says torque and climbing ability matter more than top speed off road.
- **Ingress protection:** mud and spray beat an IPX4 rating quickly. The Hyperion RST's IP67 is the standout here.

By these tests, the best all terrain electric scooters 2027 buyers can realistically use are the King GTR (the real thing), the Mantis King GT (the lighter option) and the Hyperion RST (the most water resistant). A lighter scooter can handle hardpack: ERideHero rode the NIU KQi Air on rough asphalt, wet roads and hardpacked trails and says it never felt sketchy, though it has no suspension and calls that its main weakness on bumps.

## Motor power: single vs dual, nominal vs peak

Hill speed comes down to torque, which comes from motor power, battery voltage and weight.

- **Dual motors** share the load. ERideHero credits them for the Apollo Go's lead among sub-50 lb hill climbers, and ESG's fastest climbers are all dual-motor designs.
- **A big single motor can work too.** The Teewing GT4's single 1,500 W nominal motor beat several dual-motor scooters on ESG's hill (9.7 seconds), though it weighs 88 lb.
- **Nominal vs peak.** The GT4 is 1,500 W nominal and 2,400 W peak. The Max G3 is 850 W, and the VX2 Hub is 500 W nominal with 1,900 W peak. A peak number you can hold for seconds isn't the same as power you can hold up a long climb, which is why I rely on timed hills.
- **Boost modes.** Segway's ZT3 Pro uses one that is limited to about 30 seconds or until you brake, per ESG.
- **A rough floor.** A retailer roundup I read in my budget research suggests at least 500 W for hills, with 800 W peak ideal for steep climbs. That is a retailer's rule of thumb. ESG's times show a 500 W class dual-motor scooter (Hover-1 Journey Max) still averages about 11 mph on a 10 percent grade.
- **Rider weight.** Heavier riders slow down. ESG's Nami Klima time (7.7 seconds) came from a rider about 50 lb heavier than its usual tester. ERideHero notes very few cheap scooters will perform with loads above 200 lb. If you're heavy, favor the higher motor power and the bigger rider limits, such as 286 lb on the Climber and Max G3.

## Water, mud and maintenance

Dirt and wet roads are hard on a scooter, so the IP ratings matter more here. In the sources, the Hyperion RST is IP67, the Apollo Go IP66, the Inmotion Climber IP56, the Mantis King GT IPX5 (and the reviewer advises against regular wet riding), the Max G3 IPX6, the Teewing GT4 IPX4, and several off-road picks like the GOTRAX GX3 and Ausom Gosoul 2 Pro are IP54. A rating describes a lab test, not mud and puddles, and water damage is often excluded from warranties. See my [waterproof scooter guide](/best-waterproof-electric-scooters-for-daily-commute/) if you ride in the wet.

Off-road riding also wears parts. Brake pads wear faster on descents (see how to [adjust and replace scooter brakes](/how-to-fix-adjust-and-change-brake-pads-on-an-electric-scooter/)), and tubed tires like the Climber's puncture easily (see how to [fix a flat tire on an electric scooter](/how-to-fix-a-flat-tire-on-an-electric-scooter/)). Tubeless self-healing tires reduce that risk.

## Safety, rules and where you can ride

Hills and rough ground raise the stakes, because speed on a descent is easy to build and hard to scrub. Wear a helmet every time, and for the faster off-road scooters use full protective gear. ERideHero's advice for the fastest scooters, which applies here too, is that they're enthusiast machines for experienced riders on private roads. Hydraulic brakes matter more on hills, because the descent loads the brakes more than the climb.

On where you may ride, RiderGuide's off-road guide notes that legality varies by location and that high-power models may not be legal on public roads or paths. I haven't summarized state, city or land-manager rules, because I couldn't find reliable official summaries and they differ widely. Before buying a big dual-motor scooter, check your state's scooter definition and speed or power cap, and ask the landowner or park authority about trails. For the speed side, see my explainer on [how fast electric scooters go](/how-fast-do-electric-scooters-go-average-speed-in-km/).

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death), and asked manufacturers to certify to UL standards, including UL 2272. ESG's review says the Inmotion Climber lacks UL certification, and I couldn't verify the others, so check before you buy a large battery.
