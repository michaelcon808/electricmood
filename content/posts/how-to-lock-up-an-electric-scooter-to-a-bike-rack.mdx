# How to Lock Up an Electric Scooter

How do you lock up an electric scooter? Use a hardened U-lock (not a cable lock), run it through the scooter's frame and at least one wheel, and attach it to a fixed bike rack or pole in a busy, well-lit spot. Add a second lock if you can, take the battery with you if it comes out, and lock it every time, even for a few minutes. That is the shared advice from the campus police and security pages I read, and how to lock an electric scooter to a bike rack is the question most of them answer first, and it matches what the scooter sellers say.

This page covers the questions people ask: how to lock a scooter to a bike rack, whether a bike lock works on a scooter, where to put the lock, what lock to buy, locking up at school, and extras like GPS trackers and alarms. I haven't tested any lock or had a scooter stolen. The advice comes from [DePaul University](https://www.depaul.edu/news/prevent-bike-and-scooter-theft), the [University of Washington police](https://police.uw.edu/crime-prevention-tips/ebikes), [Missouri State University](https://blogs.missouristate.edu/safetyandcrimealerts/2024/08/16/e-scooter-theft-alert-how-to-keep-your-ride-safe), [Carleton University](https://carleton.ca/safety/2026/lock-it-before-you-lose-it-bike-e-scooter-thefts-on-the-rise/), and scooter sellers Levy and Hiboy, whose figures are claims and not independent tests.

## How do you lock up an electric scooter? Step by step

Here is how do you lock up an electric scooter in seven steps. It combines Levy's rack guide with the campus police advice; where they differ I say so later.

1. **Pick a spot.** A fixed rack or pole, in a visible, well-lit place with people around. Missouri State says to avoid dark or isolated areas.
2. **Check the anchor.** It should be too heavy to lift and too solid to cut or remove. Levy says to avoid damaged racks.
3. **Park close.** Levy says to get the frame touching the rack so there is little slack.
4. **Lock the frame, not only a wheel.** Missouri State and Carleton both say a wheel-only lock lets a thief remove the wheel and take the rest. Carleton: "Never lock only the wheel."
5. **Add the wheel.** Levy threads the U-lock through the front wheel and frame. Hiboy says to lock the frame and at least one wheel.
6. **Add a second lock** around the other wheel and frame if you have one (Levy), or a chain or secondary lock (UW police, Carleton).
7. **Tug it.** Levy says to pull on the lock before you walk away. Keep the lock tight and off the ground, with the keyhole facing down (Hiboy).

Take the battery with you if it is removable (Levy, UW police).

## How to lock a scooter to a bike rack

How to lock a scooter to a bike rack comes down to the rack as much as the lock. Hiboy says bike racks are cemented into the ground and a strong choice, and that many cities require bike racks or similar fixtures for parking. Its list of fixtures to avoid is trees, thin signposts, metal fences and short posts. Steel beams should be taller than the scooter so it cannot be lifted over the top, and not hollow, rusted or wobbly.

If you are working out how to lock an electric scooter to a bike rack that is crowded, park the scooter so the lock can reach both a solid part of the scooter and the rack, and do not leave slack. ## Can you lock a scooter to a bike rack?

Can you lock a scooter to a bike rack? Yes, and the sources I read recommend it. DePaul's public safety director says to attach the scooter to a firmly fixed rack or pole, and Levy and Hiboy say the same. I did not find a source that says locking a scooter to a bike rack is banned. Some places do restrict where scooters can be parked, though, and I cannot see yours, so check your city, building or campus rules. A tight spot on a crowded rack can also block other people's bikes; use common sense.

## Can you use a bike lock on a scooter?

Can you use a bike lock on a scooter? Yes, if it is a good one. Hiboy says a bike lock works if it is a high-quality U-lock or chain lock that fits the scooter's frame or stem, and discourages cable bike locks. Missouri State, Carleton and DePaul all say U-lock over cable because cable locks can be cut with bolt cutters or other tools.

The practical question is fit, and how to lock a scooter with a bike lock that is too small is the one thing the sources do not solve. A U-lock has to be wide enough to go around the stem or frame and the rack at the same time. Hiboy notes U-locks can be heavy and may not fit large objects. Try your lock with your scooter and a rack before you rely on it. If you are asking how to lock a scooter with a bike lock you already own, that fit test is step one.

## How to use a bike lock on a scooter: where to put it

How to use a bike lock on a scooter depends on the scooter's design. The sources agree on the principle, run the lock through the frame, but name different spots:

| Locking point | What the sources say |
|---|---|
| Stem or deck-stem junction | DePaul: best spots are around the stem or through the folding mechanism. Hiboy: the frame and deck-stem junction are the best points; the stem works only if the handlebars cannot be detached easily |
| Folding mechanism | DePaul and Hiboy both list it as secure |
| Frame plus a wheel | Levy (front wheel and frame), Hiboy (frame and at least one wheel), Carleton (frame and rear wheel) |
| Wheel only | Missouri State, Carleton and Hiboy discourage it |
| Handlebars, fenders, bolted-on accessories | DePaul says avoid; also avoid wheels that unbolt |

Two sources name the front wheel and one names the rear, so I do not claim one is better. Use whichever lets the lock go through the frame and a solid anchor while staying tight.

## How to lock an electric scooter with a bike lock or U lock: what to buy

How to lock an electric scooter with a bike lock starts with choosing the right one, and how to lock an electric scooter with a U lock is the same job with the type most sources prefer. Hiboy's lock list:

| Lock type | What Hiboy says | Weight claims |
|---|---|---|
| U-lock | Hardened steel shackle; best mix of security and convenience for daily use; can be heavy and may not fit large objects | Levy: shackles 16 mm or thicker |
| Chain lock | Very high security; better for overnight or high-theft areas | Hiboy: up to 15 lb; Levy: 10 to 14 mm links, 4 to 8 lb |
| Folding lock | Middle option; pair with another lock in risky areas | Levy: 3 to 5 lb |
| Cable lock | Least secure; use only as a secondary lock | n/a |
| Disc brake lock | Extra layer; does not stop the scooter being carried | n/a |
| Alarms and app locks | Alarms up to 120 dB; app locks only work while the device is powered | n/a |

The two sellers' chain weights do not agree (up to 15 lb versus 4 to 8 lb), probably because they describe different chains. Levy also recommends U-locks rated Sold Secure Gold or Diamond.

Sold Secure is a UK testing body. BikeRadar's explainer says it rates locks Diamond, Gold, Silver and Bronze after tests for angle grinders, drilling, cutting, levering and picking; Gold withstands attack for "around five minutes"; Bronze is typically cable and folding locks that stop opportunists but not experienced thieves. It also says the rating does not account for where the lock is used, testing is voluntary, and powered cycles face stricter criteria, so a lock may be Diamond for pedal cycles but Gold for powered ones. I have not tested locks and I do not name brands, because I cannot compare them.

## Two locks, and what to take with you

The UW police advice is blunt: use two locks and make one a U-lock; for long absences pair a U-lock with a chain lock. Carleton says the same for e-bikes. UW also says to remove the battery and take it with you, since thieves are less interested in a bike without one; Carleton says to remove detachable parts such as lights, bags, phone mounts and batteries. Levy suggests security screws, lockable wheel nuts or security skewers for removable parts.

I cannot say that a battery-less scooter is never stolen, and none of these pages gives data on that. Treat it as one more layer.

## How to lock up a scooter at school

How to lock up a scooter at school depends on the school. Every campus source I read is a university, and none addresses primary or secondary schools. Hiboy's guide does not address schools either, so treat what follows as university-based advice.

- **Use the campus rack** and lock the frame as above.
- **Register it.** Missouri State says its Campus Safety registration is free, includes a serial number and sticker with a unique code, and helps recovery. UW police recommend registering with Bike Index and keeping the serial number handy; Carleton recommends a registry called 529 Garage.
- **Avoid leaving it overnight** outdoors on campus when possible (Carleton), or use a monitored compound if the campus has one. Carleton mentions card-access compounds with CCTV.
- **Ask about indoor storage.** UW police suggest a secure bike room, locker or your own room, and say access rules vary by building.

Many of these tips come from universities that report recent thefts of e-scooters. Carleton and Missouri State describe a rise, but Carleton gives no numbers. For students, see my [best electric scooters for college students](/best-electric-scooters-for-college-students-large-campus/). If you are asking for a younger child's school, ask the school office what is allowed, since I have no source for it.

## Alarms, GPS trackers and registration

These add recovery and deterrence but do not replace a lock.

- **GPS or tag:** UW police suggest a small concealed tracker such as an AirTag or Tile. Hiboy says a GPS tracker helps you locate and recover a stolen scooter but does not prevent theft itself. Levy says to hide one under the seat, inside frame tubes or near the battery, and claims a Berlin case study found over 90 percent of stolen scooters were recovered within 48 hours. I could not verify that figure.
- **Alarms:** Levy says alarms typically reach 100 to 120 dB and that thieves often abandon attempts within 10 to 20 seconds; Hiboy says up to 120 dB. Both are seller claims.
- **Registration:** Missouri State, UW police and Carleton all recommend a registry or campus registration to help recovery. Keep the serial number somewhere other than the scooter.

## Storing it and insurance

Levy says 59 percent of e-bike and scooter thefts happen in residential outdoor areas and about 61 percent on streets, and recommends indoor storage or a locked shed or garage with motion lighting and cameras. I could not find the source of those figures, and 59 and 61 percent cannot both be shares of the same total, so I would treat them as rough claims.

On insurance, Hiboy says deductibles are typically $100 to $500, while Levy says renters or homeowners policies may cover scooters only at home, often with $500 to $1,000 deductibles. Those are different policy types. Both suggest checking your coverage in writing; I have not checked any policy.

## Where the sources disagree

- **Which wheel:** Levy says front wheel and frame; Carleton says frame and rear wheel. Everyone agrees on the frame.
- **Where on the scooter:** DePaul and Hiboy say the stem and the folding mechanism; Hiboy adds the stem only works if the handlebars cannot be detached easily, while DePaul says avoid locking around handlebars.
- **Chain lock weight:** up to 15 lb (Hiboy) versus 4 to 8 lb (Levy).
- **Insurance deductibles:** $100 to $500 versus $500 to $1,000 (different policy types).
- **Theft shares:** 59 percent and 61 percent in Levy's list, which cannot both be shares of the same total.

## Frequently asked questions

### How to lock a scooter to a bike rack?
Use a U-lock through the frame and at least one wheel, attached to a fixed rack, with no slack. Add a second lock and take the battery with you.

### How to lock an electric scooter to a bike rack if it folds?
DePaul and Hiboy list the stem and folding mechanism as good locking points, with the handlebars secure. Check your model.

### How to lock electric scooter to bike rack without a U-lock?
A high-quality chain lock also works, per Hiboy. Cable locks are the weakest, and should only be a second lock.

### How do you lock up an electric scooter at night?
Hiboy recommends chain locks for overnight or high-theft areas. Carleton says to avoid leaving a scooter outdoors overnight on campus when possible.

### How do you lock up an electric scooter in an apartment building?
UW police suggest a secure bike room, locker or your own room, and say to check with the building about access rules.

### Can you lock a scooter to a bike rack?
Yes. DePaul, Levy and Hiboy all recommend a fixed rack or pole. Check local parking rules.

### Can you use a bike lock on a scooter?
Yes, if it is a high-quality U-lock or chain lock that fits the frame or stem (Hiboy). Avoid cable locks.

### How to use a bike lock on a scooter if it only fits around the wheel?
Do not rely on that. Missouri State and Carleton say a wheel-only lock lets a thief remove the wheel and take the scooter.

### How to lock a scooter with a bike lock on a pole?
Hiboy says avoid trees, thin signposts, metal fences and short posts. A tall, solid, anchored pole or rack is best.

### How to lock an electric scooter with a bike lock and cable together?
Use the U-lock as the main lock and the cable as a secondary, per Hiboy. A cable alone is easy to cut.

### How to lock an electric scooter with a U lock on a thin stem?
A smaller U-lock may fit around a stem, but check that the handlebars cannot be removed and the lock fits the rack at the same time.

### How to lock up a scooter at school?
Use the school rack, lock the frame, register it and ask the school what is allowed. My sources are about universities, not primary or secondary schools.

### Does a GPS tracker stop theft?
No. Hiboy says it helps recovery and does not prevent theft.

### Should I take the battery with me?
UW police and Levy say to remove a removable battery. No source gives data on how much it helps.

### Should I register my scooter?
Missouri State, UW police and Carleton all recommend registering, because it helps recovery.

### What does Sold Secure mean?
A UK lock-testing scheme with Diamond, Gold, Silver and Bronze ratings. BikeRadar says Gold withstands attack for around five minutes.

## The bottom line

How to lock up an electric scooter: a U-lock through the frame and a wheel, on a fixed rack in a busy spot, with no slack, plus a second lock and the battery in your bag. That is the common advice across the universities and sellers I read, and it is only as good as the lock and the rack.
