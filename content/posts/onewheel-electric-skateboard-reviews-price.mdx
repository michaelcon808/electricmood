## What a Onewheel is, and how it differs from an electric skateboard

A Onewheel is a self-balancing board with one large tire. [Wikipedia describes it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onewheel) as a board you ride sideways, leaning forward to accelerate and backward to slow down, with a motor in the wheel and sensors that take readings about 14,000 times a second to keep you upright. The company, Future Motion of Santa Cruz, launched it on Kickstarter in 2014, and the design was meant to feel like snowboarding on powder.

Several features matter for buyers:

- **The tire.** A go-kart-sized tire is why it handles dirt, grass and rough paths that stop a typical electric skateboard.
- **Pushback and Haptic Buzz.** According to Wikipedia, every model uses them to warn you that you're approaching the maximum safe speed, with pushback gently raising the nose.
- **Simplestop.** From the Pint onward, you can lean back to slow the board and disengage so you can step off.
- **The app and riding modes.** You get several riding modes, and since late 2023 the GT and GT S-Series also offer Custom Shaping, which lets you adjust how the board responds.

The trade-off is the learning curve and the failure mode. A skateboard that loses power rolls on, but a self-balancing board that stops balancing can throw its rider forward. That's the heart of the safety story below.

## Safety first: the CPSC warning and the 2023 recall

I'm putting this ahead of the model reviews on purpose, because it should shape your decision.

**What happened.** In November 2022, the [CPSC warned consumers to stop using all Onewheel models](https://www.cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Warns-Consumers-to-Stop-Using-Onewheel-Self-Balancing-Electric-Skateboards-Due-to-Ejection-Hazard-At-Least-Four-Deaths-and-Multiple-Injuries-Reported), citing at least four deaths and multiple injuries, and Future Motion initially disagreed. In September 2023, the company announced a [recall of about 300,000 boards](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2023/Future-Motion-Recalls-Onewheel-Self-Balancing-Electric-Skateboards-Due-to-Crash-Hazard-Four-Deaths-Reported). The CPSC's notice says the company had received dozens of reports of incidents, including four deaths between 2019 and 2021 and injuries such as traumatic brain injury, concussion, paralysis and fractures. It covered the original Onewheel, the Onewheel+, the Onewheel+ XR, the Pint, the Pint X and the GT. The company's own explanation, as [NPR reported](https://www.npr.org/2023/09/30/1202857917/onewheel-e-skateboard-recall), was that the boards can stop balancing the rider if their limits are exceeded.

**The remedy.** It was a firmware update, not a physical recall. According to Wikipedia, the update adds haptic feedback when the board's limits are reached. NPR reported that the update would arrive for the GT within a week and for the Pint X, Pint and XR within six weeks. The CPSC and Future Motion both encourage riders to wear protective equipment.

**An earlier recall.** In August 2022, the company recalled about 20,500 GT front footpads after 813 reports of the footpad failing to disengage after the rider dismounted, [as the CPSC's notice records](https://www.cpsc.gov/Recalls/2022/Future-Motion-Recalls-Footpads-for-Onewheel-GT-Electric-Skateboards-Due-to-Bystander-Injury-Hazard-Recall-Alert).

**What I can and can't say.**

- The newer models, the GT S-Series, Pint S, XR Classic and Rally XL, were released after the 2023 recall, so they weren't on its list. I haven't found a CPSC notice about them, but that doesn't prove they can't nosedive. Reviewers argue that the S-Series is safer because of its higher voltage and power headroom. That's an opinion I can't verify.
- Future Motion has said its boards are safe when ridden with basic safe riding practices, including a helmet and other gear.
- If you buy used, an older board may not have the update. I cover that below.

My advice, which isn't a verdict from any agency: treat a Onewheel like a motorsport vehicle, wear a helmet and protective gear every ride, learn on a forgiving model, and ride below the board's limits. If that risk isn't acceptable to you, a conventional electric skateboard may suit you better.

## Where to buy a Onewheel

If you're asking where to buy a Onewheel, you have three realistic routes.

1. **Onewheel's own site.** It's the company's official store, with financing and the 12-month warranty, and it's where the current prices and sales appear. [Check Onewheel's official store](AFFILIATE_LINK_ONEWHEEL_OFFICIAL).
2. **Authorized dealers.** The official site includes a dealer locator, which is worth using if you want to see a board in person and get local service. Ask about the warranty terms and whether the board comes with the latest firmware.
3. **Used marketplaces and local sales.** You'll find lower prices, no warranty and more risk.

Be careful about vague listings. I saw marketplace listings that use "Onewheel" loosely for other products, so check the model name and make sure it's a genuine Future Motion board if that's what you want.

## Buying a used Onewheel: a checklist

If you're tempted by a used board, here's what I'd check. This is my own checklist, not a rule from any agency.

1. **Model and age.** Confirm exactly which model it is, and whether it predates the 2023 recall.
2. **Firmware and app.** The 2023 recall remedy was a firmware update, so ask the seller to show the board paired with the app and fully updated.
3. **Mileage and battery health.** Ask for the mileage from the app and how it's been charged and stored.
4. **Tire and footpad condition.** Look for wear, cracks and play in the footpad.
5. **Crash history.** Ask directly, and look for damage to the rails and the frame.
6. **Modifications.** Third-party modifications are common. They can void support, and non-standard electronics are a risk. Ask what's been changed.
7. **A short test ride** with the seller present, with a helmet on.
8. **Proof of purchase and serial number,** and a clear sense that the board isn't stolen.

If the seller can't answer these questions, walk away.

## Who should buy one, and who shouldn't

A Onewheel suits you if you want to ride rough ground, enjoy the snowboarding-style feel, are willing to learn a new skill, and accept the safety record with protective gear and caution. It may not suit you if you want a simple commuter, need to carry the board a lot (full-size models are heavy), or aren't comfortable with the risk that a self-balancing board can stop balancing.

A rough way to choose among the models:

- **First board, short rides:** Pint X, or the Pint if range doesn't matter.
- **Mixed riding and a compact board:** Pint S.
- **A full-size classic feel:** XR Classic.
- **Maximum range:** GT.
- **Power and speed for experienced riders:** GT S-Series or the Rally XL.

Whichever you choose, start in the gentlest settings, practice somewhere flat and soft, and wear a helmet.

## Where you can ride one

Rules for self-balancing boards vary and are changing. The National Park Service's new rule on powered micromobility devices, for example, covers self-balancing devices like the Onewheel as well as electric skateboards and scooters, and [GearJunkie's coverage](https://gearjunkie.com/parks-and-public-lands/hoverboard-national-parks) explains that each park superintendent decides where they're allowed. State and city rules differ too, and I couldn't find a trustworthy summary for Onewheels specifically. Check your state's transportation rules, your city's ordinances and the land manager's rules before you ride in public. ### Are Onewheel reviews 2027 positive?
