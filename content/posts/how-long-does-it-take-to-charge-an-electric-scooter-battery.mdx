# How Long Does an E-Scooter Take to Charge? 2027 Guide

How long does it take to charge an electric scooter? Most take about 3 to 8 hours from empty. Small commuter scooters sit at the short end, mid-size ones around 5 to 8 hours, and big dual-motor models can need 10 hours or more. You can work out your own number with one sum: battery size in watt-hours divided by charger power in watts, plus roughly 10 to 20 percent for losses.

That sum matters because the published times disagree. Levy lists 3 to 8 hours, Unagi says 4 to 8 for most scooters, and Vepace gives 12 to 28 hours for its high-performance category. Those figures come from retailers and brands, not from a lab. This guide shows the formula, runs it on real battery and charger specs, and separates what testers measured from what makers claim, so you know how long a scooter takes to charge before you buy or plug in.

I haven't ridden or charge-tested any of these scooters. The figures come from Notebookcheck, ERideHero, Wikipedia, the London Fire Brigade, the US Consumer Product Safety Commission's Federal Register notice, and brand and retailer pages that I label as claims. Checked October 10, 2026.

## How long electric scooters take to charge: the quick table

The honest answer to how long do electric scooters take to charge, and how long does it take to charge a scooter battery of a given size, is "it depends on battery size and charger power", so here are the ranges that sources give. I have not averaged them, because each source defines its categories differently.

| Source | What it covers | Charge time given | Type of figure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Levy | Most scooters | 3 to 8 hours | Retailer guidance |
| Unagi | Most scooters | About 4 to 8 hours | Brand guidance |
| Aventura-X | 300 Wh / 500 to 700 Wh / 1,000 Wh | 3 to 4 / 4 to 7 / 8+ hours on a standard charger | Retailer guidance |
| Gyroor | 250 / 500 / 750+ Wh | 4 to 5 / 6 to 8 / 8 to 10 hours | Brand guidance |
| Vepace | Light urban / mid-range / high-performance | 7 to 9 / 10 to 12 / 12 to 28 hours | Retailer guidance, based on its own models |
| Notebookcheck | Segway Ninebot Max G2 (551 Wh) | "Around six hours" | Review text; no test method described |
| Apollo support site | Apollo Pro / Apollo Go | About 6 / about 7.5 hours | Maker spec |

The same battery size gets different answers: a 500 Wh scooter is 6 to 8 hours at Gyroor and 4 to 7 hours at Aventura-X, and none of these tables name the charger, which is half of the sum.

## The formula for how long a scooter takes to charge

If you want to know how long does it take to charge an electric scooter for your own model, you need two numbers from the scooter or charger label.

1. **Battery energy in watt-hours (Wh).** Some labels give volts and amp-hours instead. Multiply them: 36 V × 10 Ah = 360 Wh.
2. **Charger power in watts (W).** Multiply the charger's output volts by its amps. A 42 V, 2 A charger is 84 W.

**Charge time in hours ≈ Wh ÷ W ÷ efficiency**, where efficiency is about 0.85 to 0.9. Unagi puts lithium-ion efficiency at 90 to 95 percent in its own worked example, which is a brand figure, so I use a more cautious range. The result is a floor, not a promise, for the reason in the next section.

Here is the sum on real specs. The Wh and watt figures for the first two rows are as Unagi lists them; the Max G2 figures come from ERideHero and Notebookcheck; the last two rows are hypothetical chargers I picked to show the effect. The "my sum" column is my arithmetic, not a test.

| Scooter or example | Battery | Charger | My sum (ideal, then at 90%) | Time listed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unagi Model One Classic | 259 Wh | 67.2 W (33.6 V, 2 A) | 3.9 h, 4.3 h | About 4.5 h (Unagi) |
| Unagi Model One Voyager | 360 Wh | 126 W (42 V, 3 A) | 2.9 h, 3.2 h | About 3 h (Unagi) |
| Segway Ninebot Max G2 | 551 Wh | 121 W (42 V, 2.9 A) | 4.6 h, 5.1 h | About 6 h (ERideHero, Notebookcheck) |
| Example: 720 Wh pack | 720 Wh | 2 A charger, about 109 W at 54.6 V | 6.6 h, 7.3 h | 7 to 8 h (Vepace, 48 V 15 Ah pack) |
| Same pack, faster charger | 720 Wh | 5 A charger, about 273 W at 54.6 V | 2.6 h, 2.9 h | 3 to 4 h (Vepace) |

The sum lands close for the small scooters and runs short for the Max G2, which is the next thing to understand. Vepace's own text writes its formula as Wh ÷ charger amps, which mixes units. The version above avoids that. If you only have amp-hours and charger amps, Ah ÷ A gives the same rough answer, for example 15 Ah ÷ 2 A is 7.5 hours.

## Why the label time is longer than the math

A lithium-ion pack does not charge at a steady rate. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) describes the standard method as constant current, then constant voltage: the charger pushes steady current while voltage rises, then holds the voltage at its limit while the current tapers off. The taper is the slow part. A scooter can look "almost full" long before the charger stops.

Unagi's own numbers show it. For the Model One Classic it lists about 4.5 hours total and 80 percent at 3.5 hours, so the last 20 percent takes roughly a fifth of the time. For the Voyager it lists about 3 hours total and 80 percent at 2 hours, so the last 20 percent takes a third. These are brand figures, and some of Unagi's other numbers do not add up: it lists 50 percent in 1 hour for the Classic, but half of 259 Wh is about 130 Wh, and a 67.2 W charger cannot deliver that in an hour. Treat the percentage checkpoints as rough.

The Max G2 is a useful warning. Both ERideHero and Notebookcheck list about 6 hours at roughly 121 W, while my sum for a 551 Wh pack gives about 5 hours. Neither review describes a charging test, so I can't say whether 6 hours was timed or copied from the spec sheet. Notebookcheck did measure one thing: after a 10 km ride that took the battery from 100 to 87 percent, recharging drew 103.7 Wh from the wall. That is more than 13 percent of 551 Wh (about 72 Wh), which fits charging losses, but the percent display is coarse, so I would not build a loss figure from a single measurement.

Practical reading, for anyone asking how long does a scooter battery take to charge in real life: expect the label time, not the ideal sum, and expect the last stretch to crawl. If you are in a hurry, stopping at 80 to 90 percent saves real time.

## Charge time by scooter size

Here is how long does a scooter take to charge by battery class, and how long does a scooter take to charge on a bigger pack, using the ranges sources give. I have kept each source's numbers separate because their size bands differ.

- **Small, about 200 to 300 Wh.** Aventura-X gives 2 to 4 hours for 200 to 300 Wh, and Gyroor gives 4 to 5 hours for a 250 Wh scooter. Unagi lists about 3 to 4.5 hours for its two 259 and 360 Wh models.
- **Mid-size, about 500 to 700 Wh.** Aventura-X says 4 to 7 hours, Gyroor says 6 to 8 hours for 500 Wh, and the Max G2 at 551 Wh is listed at about 6 hours. This is the class most commuters buy. My [commuter scooter guide](/best-commuter-fast-electric-scooters-comparison-for-daily-city-commuting-reviews/) covers models in it.
- **Large, 750 Wh and up.** Gyroor gives 8 to 10 hours for 750 Wh and above, and Aventura-X says 1,000 Wh packs take "upwards of 8 hours" on a standard charger.
- **Performance dual-motor models.** Unagi says high-end scooters with batteries up to 3,000 Wh take about 10 hours or more, and that optional fast chargers can bring that under 10 hours. Vepace lists the Dualtron Thunder 3 and X LTD at 15 to 28 hours, which is the longest figure I found. These are claims from sellers of those scooters.

If charge time is one of your buying filters, pair it with range. A bigger battery gives more miles but takes longer to refill on the same charger, which is why long-range scooters often ship with two charging ports or an optional fast charger. See my [long-range scooter guide](/best-affordable-top-long-range-electric-scooters-for-adults-longest-range-reviews/) for the trade-off, and [how far an electric scooter can go on one charge](/how-far-and-how-many-miles-can-an-electric-scooter-go/) for real-world miles.

## Tested charge time versus claimed charge time

Almost every number above is a claim, and that matters if you want to know how long does it take to charge e scooter models you haven't bought yet. Here is what I found on the difference.

- **Reviews repeat the spec.** ERideHero's Max G2 page says "Charging takes roughly 6 hours" and does not say it timed it. Notebookcheck's text also says around six hours and recommends charging the night before a ride. Neither describes a stopwatch test.
- **Maker pages give model-specific numbers.** Apollo's support site lists the Apollo Pro at about 6 hours on a 58.8 V, 5 A charger and the Apollo Go at about 7.5 hours on a 36 V, 2 A charger. Those are specs, not tests.
- **Crowdsourced data is messy.** ScooterRank lists 5 hours for the Max G2, which differs from the 6 hours in both reviews, and does not say whether that is a maker claim or user reports. Its category averages also disagree with each other: 7.89 hours across 250 scooters in one place, 8.15 hours across 183 in another.

So for how much time it takes to charge electric scooter batteries in practice, and how long does it take for a scooter to charge on your own wall socket, the best evidence I can offer is the arithmetic plus the label. If you own the scooter, the strongest data is yours: time one full charge with a smart plug or a simple timer and note when the charger light changes.

## What changes your scooter's charge time

These are the factors that move the number, in the order I would check them. They apply whether you ask how long do e scooters take to charge in general or how long does it take a scooter battery to charge on your own charger.

- **Charger output.** A 5 A charger on a pack that normally uses 2 A cuts the wait sharply, as the 720 Wh example shows, but only if the scooter and battery support it. Using a charger the maker did not approve is a fire risk, so check before buying one.
- **Starting charge.** Coming home at 30 percent takes far less than 0 percent, and Aventura-X's own example is about 2 to 3 hours to top off from 30 percent on a 2 A charger.
- **Temperature.** Wikipedia says lithium-ion charging works best from about 5 to 45 °C, that charging between 0 and 5 °C needs reduced current, and that many cells cannot be charged safely below 0 °C. Charging above 45 °C degrades the battery. In practice, bring a cold scooter indoors and let it warm up first.
- **Battery age.** Vepace and Unagi both list battery age as a factor. An old pack can reach "full" sooner because it holds less, and it may run hotter. I have no tested figures for how much, so I won't give one.
- **Charger design.** Some scooters, like the Max G2, have the charger built in and a second port for a faster external charger. Notebookcheck lists the main port at up to 2 A and the second at up to 5 A. Check your manual for a second port before assuming the slow rate is all you get.

## Can you charge an electric scooter faster?

Yes, with limits. This is the part where a lot of articles over-promise.

**Use the supported faster charger, if one exists.** Unagi says optional fast chargers can bring big-battery scooters under 10 hours, and Vepace says fast charging can cut the wait by up to 50 percent. Both note it is not free: Unagi warns that fast charging adds heat and stress, and Gyroor recommends standard charging for everyday use.

**Stop early.** On how long do you charge an electric scooter before unplugging: because the final stretch is slow, unplugging at 80 to 90 percent saves time and, according to Unagi, is gentler on the battery. ERideHero notes that the Max G2 app has a charging-limit option and that limiting to 80 percent suits storage. Not every scooter has this: Apollo's support site says its scooters do not currently support charge limit settings.

**Charge little and often?** Sources disagree. Aventura-X advises against frequent top-offs and suggests letting the battery fall to about 20 to 30 percent first, while Unagi says to charge after each ride and avoid running flat. I would follow whatever your scooter's maker says. ## How long should you charge an electric scooter overnight?

People often ask how long should I charge my electric scooter and whether overnight is fine, and how long should i charge my electric scooter if it has no auto-stop. The charge time tells you how long it needs, and how long does it take to charge a scooter battery overnight is usually less than the night: if the scooter takes 6 hours, the charger has finished well before morning. Whether it is safe to leave it on is a separate question, and fire services are cautious.

The London Fire Brigade's advice, in an [incident report from July 2025](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/incidents/2025/july/e-scooter-fire-acton/), includes: let the battery cool before charging, unplug the charger once it has finished, use the correct charger bought from a reputable seller, and "we would advise not to leave it unattended or while people are asleep." It also advises against charging on an escape route such as a hallway and says to fit smoke alarms where scooters are charged. The same page says that since the start of 2023 there has been an e-bike or e-scooter fire on average once every two days.

In the US, the Consumer Product Safety Commission proposed a battery safety standard for micromobility products in June 2026, covering e-scooters among others. It is a proposed rule, not a final one. The notice counts 227 incidents from January 2019 through December 2023, including 195 fires and 39 deaths, and says 120 incidents (53 percent) happened while the product was plugged in and charging. Details and comment history are in the [Federal Register notice](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/06/24/2026-12749/safety-standard-for-lithium-ion-batteries-used-in-micromobility-products-and-electrical-systems-of).

That does not mean charging is dangerous in itself. It means the sensible habit is to charge where you can hear a smoke alarm, with the maker's charger, on a hard surface, away from your exit. A smart plug with a timer, which Unagi suggests for overnight charging, means the charger switches off at a time you set. Charge cooler batteries, watch for heat, swelling or a strange smell, and stop using any pack that shows them. For setup, see [how to charge an electric scooter at home](/how-to-charge-an-electric-scooter-battery-at-home/) and [how to tell if your scooter is charging](/how-to-know-if-my-electric-scooter-is-charging-or-charged/).

## How to work out your own scooter's charge time

Here is the five-step version for anyone asking how long do you have to charge an electric scooter, or how long does it take a scooter battery to charge on a particular charger, or how long does it take to charge an e scooter like yours, and how long does it take for a scooter to charge from half full.

1. **Find the battery.** Look in the manual or the scooter's spec page for Wh, or for volts and Ah (multiply them).
2. **Find the charger.** Read the output on the charger label: volts and amps. Multiply for watts. If the scooter has two ports, use the one you will actually use.
3. **Divide and pad.** Battery Wh divided by charger W, then divide by 0.9 and add a margin of 10 to 20 percent.
4. **Compare with the maker's time.** If the maker's figure is longer than your sum, trust the maker's. They have measured the taper.
5. **Time one charge yourself.** A smart plug or phone timer from nearly empty to charger-off gives you the number that matters for your own routine.

Charging costs money as well as time, and how much time it takes to charge electric scooter batteries also decides when the electricity is used. ## Frequently asked questions

### How long does an electric scooter take to charge?
Most take 3 to 8 hours from empty, and how long does an electric scooter take to charge depends mainly on pack size. Small scooters can charge in 3 to 4 hours, and large performance models take 10 hours or more. Divide battery Wh by charger watts, then add 10 to 20 percent.

### How long does it take to charge an electric scooter from 0 to 100?
Use the maker's figure for your model. Typical full charges are 3 to 4 hours for about 300 Wh batteries and 8 hours or more for about 1,000 Wh packs on a standard charger, according to Aventura-X.

### How long do electric scooters take to charge on a fast charger?
Less, but how much depends on the charger and battery. Vepace says fast charging can cut the wait by up to 50 percent, and Unagi says optional fast chargers can bring big scooters under 10 hours. Heat and wear are the trade-off.

### How long does a scooter battery take to charge when it is only half empty?
Roughly half the time for the first part, but the last 20 percent is slow. Unagi lists 80 percent at 2 hours for a scooter that takes about 3 hours in total.

### How long do e scooters take to charge compared with e-bikes?
They are in the same range, since both use lithium-ion packs and the same sum applies. See my e-bike charging time guide for the bike-specific figures.

### How long does it take to charge e scooter batteries in cold weather?
Longer, and sometimes not at all. Wikipedia says charging below 0 °C is unsafe for many lithium-ion cells and that 0 to 5 °C needs reduced current. Warm the scooter indoors first.

### How long does it take a scooter to charge if the charger light stays red, and how long do you charge an electric scooter after the light changes?
It depends on the model, and a charger light's colours differ by maker. Check the manual, and see my guide on telling whether your scooter is charging.

### How long do you have to charge an electric scooter before it is full?
Until the charger or app says it is done. If your scooter has a charge limit, 80 to 90 percent is quicker and gentler on the battery, per Unagi.

### Is it safe to leave a scooter charging overnight?
Fire services advise against leaving a charger running unattended or while people sleep. The London Fire Brigade says to unplug when finished and avoid charging on escape routes.

## The bottom line

So, how long does an electric scooter take to charge? Plan on 3 to 8 hours for most, which is the short answer to how long does it take to charge an e scooter, which also answers how long does it take a scooter to charge for a typical commuter model, longer for big batteries, and work out your own figure with Wh ÷ W ÷ 0.9. Where the maker gives a time, trust it over the sum, because it accounts for the slow last stretch. Where sources disagree, as they do for 500 Wh scooters, check the charger rating rather than guessing.

For safety, use the maker's charger, charge somewhere you can hear a smoke alarm, and unplug when finished. If you are buying a scooter and charge time matters, compare the charger rating and whether the scooter has a second faster port. Since I haven't tested any of these scooters, treat the ranges as published claims and time a charge on your own.
