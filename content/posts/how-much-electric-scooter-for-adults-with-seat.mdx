# Best Electric Scooters with Seat for Adults 2027

The best electric scooter for most adults who want a seat is the EMOVE RoadRunner V3: Electric Scooter Guide tested it at 32.8 miles of real range and 36.4 mph, it has a swappable 1,124 Wh battery, a long seat and rear suspension, and it carries riders up to 330 lb. If you want the cheapest way to sit down, the GOTRAX Flex and A5 are the budget seated scooters, but they're slow, around 15 mph, and heavy for what they offer.

Those are my picks for an electric scooter for adults with seat 2027 shoppers should compare. A seat changes the whole buying decision. A seated scooter is heavier, it needs bigger wheels and better suspension because bumps go straight into your back, and the fast ones can cross a legal line where your scooter is treated like a moped. This guide shows tested numbers where they exist, flags where the only data is the maker's claim, and gives sourced prices so you can see what you'll actually pay.

I'll be straight about how this was built. I haven't ridden these scooters. My picks come from published reviews by Electric Scooter Guide, ERideHero and RiderGuide, and I say which source each number comes from. This is a thin category for independent testing: RiderGuide's seated-scooter roundup lists only claimed figures for its five picks, so I rely on Electric Scooter Guide's tests where they exist. I'm writing in October 2026 and I'll update this page as 2027 models arrive.

## Seated vs standing: who should get a seat

A seat is mainly about comfort on longer rides. RiderGuide's seated-scooter guide says a seat stops you thinking about foot position, deck space and leg fatigue, and that suspension matters more when you sit because bumps travel through the scooter and seat into your back. It also says larger tires, such as the 15-inch tires on the isinwheel H7, are calmer on rough pavement, and that once a seated scooter passes about 25 mph, brake quality becomes critical, which is why it favors hydraulic brakes.

From those points and the specs, a seat makes sense if you:

- **Ride more than a few miles at a time** and get tired standing.
- **Have knee, ankle or back discomfort when standing,** though I can't give medical advice, and you should ask your doctor.
- **Carry loads,** since two of these scooters include baskets (GOTRAX lists 40 to 50 lb).
- **Have somewhere to store a heavy scooter.**

A standing scooter is better if you:

- **Need to carry it up stairs or onto transit.** The seated scooters here weigh 37 to 135 lb, against 26 to 40 lb for the light standing scooters in my [college student guide](/best-electric-scooters-for-college-students-large-campus/).
- **Ride short trips,** where a seat is more bulk than benefit.
- **Want to spend under about $500** and still have good range. In the tests I've read for my other guides, standing scooters give more tested range per dollar.

RiderGuide itself says a seat isn't ideal for all riders. I agree, and I'd treat a seated scooter as a small vehicle, not a bigger scooter.

## How I chose these scooters

1. **Tested data first.** Electric Scooter Guide tested the RoadRunner V3 and the GOTRAX Flex. For the others, only the maker's claims were available, and the table says so.
2. **Seat quality.** The sources say almost nothing about seat padding or removability. RiderGuide describes the Pro V2's seat as a "longer plush saddle" and little else. Electric Scooter Guide gives the V3's seat height as about 28.5 inches and says it's long enough to reposition. I note where seat details are missing.
3. **Rider limit.** I list it for each scooter because it varies from 220 to 330 lb.
4. **Weight.** It's the biggest downside of a seated scooter.
5. **Certification.** UL 2271 or UL 2272 where listed.
6. **Conflicting data.** Where sources disagree (the GOTRAX Flex's weight is 47.6 lb in one and 61 lb in another), I show both.

I left out seated scooters I couldn't find any independent listing for, and anything with a single unverified source.

## Best electric scooters with seat: my picks

### EMOVE RoadRunner V3: best seated scooter overall

Electric Scooter Guide's test found 32.8 miles of range, run from full charge until the scooter stopped, against a maker's spec of 53 miles. It tested at 36.4 mph, with 0 to 30 mph in 14.4 seconds and a hill climb in 11.1 seconds, and it stopped from 15 mph in 10.2 feet. The scooter weighs 77.8 lb with its 1,124 Wh, 48V swappable battery installed, and the reviewer says the battery weighs about 15 lb. It has two 500 W hub motors, tubeless pneumatic tires, semi-hydraulic disc brakes, dual spring shocks with a rear swingarm and a 330 lb rider limit.

On the seat, the reviewer gives a height of about 28.5 inches, says riders of any height can use it and calls the seat more comfortable than the previous version's. The handlebars adjust from a cafe-racer position to upright, and the handlebars fold and the foot pegs come off by hand to make it narrower. RiderGuide lists the V3 at $1,695.

Two cautions from the same review. It has no IP rating (the maker says light rain is fine), and it has no UL certification on the battery, which the reviewer cites as a reliability concern. At 36.4 mph it also tests above the speed where some places treat a scooter as a moped, so see the rules section.

## What to check before you buy a seated scooter

- **Rider limit.** From 220 lb (GOTRAX A5) to 330 lb (RoadRunner V3, H7). My guide on [how much weight an electric scooter can hold](/how-much-weight-can-an-electric-scooter-hold/) covers how makers set the number.
- **Seat height and legroom.** The sources give the V3's seat height (about 28.5 inches) and little else. Try before you buy where you can, or check the maker's page for seat height and leg room.
- **Brakes.** RiderGuide says brake quality matters once you pass about 25 mph. Hydraulic or semi-hydraulic discs are the sensible choice for the fast ones. Drum brakes on the Flex need a firm squeeze.
- **Suspension and tires.** Larger pneumatic tires with rear suspension suit a seated rider. The V3, H7 and Pro V2 have dual suspension. The XG Pro has 20-inch wheels.
- **A removable battery.** RiderGuide notes it lets you charge indoors without carrying the whole scooter inside. The V3's battery weighs about 15 lb.
- **Water resistance.** The V3 has none, the Pro V2 is IPX5, and the others aren't listed in the sources I read. See my guide to [riding an electric scooter in the rain](/can-you-ride-use-or-drive-an-electric-scooter-in-the-rain/).
- **Certification.** UL 2271 or 2272, as listed for the XG Pro, and not listed for the V3's battery.

## Weight, storage and transport

Seated scooters are heavy, and the numbers are blunt. In this guide they run from 37 lb (JackRabbit XG Pro) through 47.6 to 61 lb (GOTRAX Flex), 51 lb (A5), 77.8 lb (V3) and 111.3 lb (H7), up to 135 lb for the Pro V2.

- **Under about 50 lb.** You can lift it into a car trunk with some effort.
- **60 to 80 lb.** You need a ramp, a hitch carrier or a second person. The V3's roughly 15 lb battery comes out, and its handlebars fold, which helps.
- **100 lb and up.** It stays where it is parked.

Charging is the other half of storage. A scooter you can't carry indoors needs a garage or a removable battery, which is one reason I'd put a swappable pack on the shortlist. If you'd rather have a lighter machine, my [lightweight and foldable scooter guide](/best-lightweight-portable-foldable-electric-scooters-for-adults/) covers standing scooters.

## Rules, speed and whether it counts as a moped

Speed is where seated scooters can get legally complicated. RiderGuide says scooters above roughly 35 mph may fall under moped or motor vehicle rules depending on where you live, and advises checking local laws. The V3 tested at 36.4 mph in Electric Scooter Guide's test, and the maker's own figure is 34 mph. The same reviewer notes the V3 is technically not a scooter, because it has foot pegs rather than a deck, and that it isn't an e-bike either, since pedaling doesn't control the motor.

I haven't summarized state or city laws, because I couldn't find reliable official summaries, and they differ widely. Before you buy anything faster than about 20 mph, check:

- Your state or city's definition of an electric scooter and any top-speed or power limit
- Whether registration, a license or insurance applies at higher speeds
- Where you're allowed to ride (bike lanes, roads, sidewalks)
- Whether helmets are required

For the speed side of this, see my guide on [how fast electric scooters are allowed to go](/how-fast-are-electric-scooters-allowed-to-go-legally/). Wear a helmet regardless.

## Safety, certification and batteries

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission counted at least 208 micromobility fire or overheating incidents in 39 states between January 2021 and November 2022, with at least 19 deaths, 5 of them involving e-scooters, in a December 2022 [CPSC news release](https://cpsc.gov/Newsroom/News-Releases/2023/CPSC-Calls-on-Manufacturers-to-Comply-with-Safety-Standards-for-Battery-Powered-Products-to-Reduce-the-Risk-of-Injury-and-Death). It asked manufacturers to certify to the voluntary UL standards, including UL 2272 for personal e-mobility devices.

That matters more for seated scooters, because they carry bigger batteries. The V3's 1,124 Wh pack is lithium-ion and, per Electric Scooter Guide, not UL listed, while the XG Pro lists UL 2271 and UL 2272 per RiderGuide. If you choose an uncertified scooter, charge it with the supplied charger, outdoors or in a garage where possible, never unattended and never on a surface that can burn. Stop using a battery that swells, smells or overheats.

## Can you add a seat to a standing scooter

Some people try to turn a standing scooter into a seated one. I cover how it works in my guide on [how to put a seat on and ride an electric scooter](/how-to-put-a-seat-on-and-ride-an-electric-scooter/). My own view is cautious: a standing scooter's stem, deck and brakes aren't designed for a seated rider's weight and position, and a bolt-on seat can void the warranty. If you want to sit, a scooter built for it is the safer route. ERideHero's GOTRAX Flex page does show a seat option sold by the maker, which is different from a third-party kit.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best electric scooter for adults with seat 2027?
The EMOVE RoadRunner V3 is the best-tested seated scooter I found, with 32.8 tested miles and a 330 lb limit. The GOTRAX Flex is the budget pick.

### What is the best electric scooter for adults with seat 2027 on a budget?
The GOTRAX Flex and A5, at roughly $300 to $400 in the sources I read. They are slow, around 15 mph, and heavy.

### How much is an electric scooter with seat?
From about $300 to $400 for a basic seated scooter to about $1,700 for the RoadRunner V3 and over $2,000 for premium models, according to RiderGuide and Electric Scooter Guide. Prices change, so check the maker.

### How much is an electric scooter with seat compared with a standing scooter?
Seated scooters cost more for the same performance, because they need bigger frames, wheels and batteries. A basic standing scooter can cost under $500 and a basic seated one costs about the same, but with less tested range per dollar.

### Are seated electric scooters comfortable?
Generally, over longer rides, if they have good suspension and larger wheels. RiderGuide says suspension matters more when you sit.

### How far do seated scooters go?
The tested figures I found are 13.5 miles (GOTRAX Flex) and 32.8 miles (RoadRunner V3). The 43 and 48-plus mile figures for the H7 and XG Pro are claims.

### Are seated electric scooters legal?
Many are, but above roughly 35 mph some places may treat a scooter as a moped. Check your local rules.

### How heavy is an electric scooter with a seat?
From 37 lb to over 130 lb in this guide, and most are over 45 lb.

### Can I put a seat on any electric scooter?
I wouldn't. See my guide on adding a seat.

### Do seated scooters have a weight limit?
Yes, from 220 lb (GOTRAX A5) to 330 lb (RoadRunner V3, H7).

### Is a seated scooter good for older riders?
It can be easier on the legs, but weight, mounting height and speed matter. I can't give medical advice, so ask your doctor if you have a health concern.
