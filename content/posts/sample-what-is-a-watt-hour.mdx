## The short answer

A **watt-hour (Wh)** measures stored energy: one watt delivered for one hour. It's the single most useful number on any battery spec sheet.

## Volts × amp-hours = watt-hours

Many batteries are labelled in amp-hours (Ah). Multiply by the voltage to get watt-hours:

| Battery | Voltage | Capacity | Energy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Small scooter | 36 V | 7.8 Ah | 281 Wh |
| Typical e-bike | 48 V | 14 Ah | 672 Wh |
| Portable power station | — | — | 1,024 Wh |

<Callout type="info">
Comparing two batteries by amp-hours only works when they share the same voltage. Always compare in **Wh**.
</Callout>

## How far does a watt-hour take you?

Roughly 8–15 Wh per km on an e-bike and 12–20 Wh per km on an e-scooter. Try the [range calculator](/tools/range-calculator/) for your own numbers.

## Worked example

A 672 Wh e-bike battery at 12 Wh/km gives about **56 km** — less in the cold or on hills.
